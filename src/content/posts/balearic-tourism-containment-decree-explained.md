In April 2025, the Balearic Govern approved a decree-law that it called a measure of tourism containment, the Decreto ley 4/2025 of 11 April. It is the most important regional legislation on holiday lets and tourist beds in recent years. This article explains what the Govern's announcement said it does, in plain terms. It does not cover every technical detail, and the full text should be read by anyone affected. It was last checked on 29 October 2026.

## Why the Govern acted

The Balearic Islands received more than 18 million tourists in 2024, according to the Consell Econòmic i Social. The number of residents is far smaller, and housing prices and crowding have become a main subject of public debate. A survey of residents carried out in 2024 by the Balearic tourism agency found that 75.6 per cent thought there were too many tourists at certain times or all year, and 77.2 per cent saw a major impact of tourism on housing costs. These findings are set out in more detail in another article in this series. The decree is the government's regulatory response.

## What the decree does

According to the Govern's own summary, the main measures are these:

- **A ban on new tourist places in multi-family buildings.** In all the islands, no new tourist rental places can be created in blocks of flats. The ban covers new registrations, not necessarily existing businesses.
- **A cap on tourist places.** The decree creates a global ceiling. New places can only be created by exchanging an existing place on a one-for-one basis, so the total does not rise.
- **A transitional bank of places.** The Bolsa de Plazas Transitoria is a mechanism for handling the exchange. The decree says it does not create any new places.
- **Island ceilings.** The island councils are asked to set their own ceilings on tourist places and to define the carrying capacity of their island. The decree does not itself fix the numbers for each island, so the figures depend on what the councils decide.
- **Higher fines.** Penalties go up by 25 per cent. Minor offences can lead to fines of up to 5,000 euros, serious ones up to 50,000 euros and very serious ones up to 500,000 euros.
- **Responsibility for platforms.** Booking websites share responsibility for illegal listings and must display registration numbers.
- **A route to reduced fines.** A sanctioned owner can get a reduction of up to 80 per cent if he or she transfers the property for use as social housing or price-limited rental.
- **Conversion of old hotels.** Obsolete hotels may be turned into affordable housing.

The Parlament validated the decree-law, as the Statute requires for urgent decrees, and the Govern announced that result on its press page.

## What it does not do

The decree does not close existing licensed businesses. It does not by itself abolish tourist rentals in houses and villas outside multi-family buildings, and it does not set the number of tourists who can come. Visitors who book hotels or licensed tourist accommodation are not directly affected. The decree concerns the supply of places, not the number of flights or ferry passengers.

It also overlaps with other levels of government. The national registry for rentals, and the national reform of the horizontal property law, are covered in a separate article. Town halls have their own planning powers. Some of them have been using those powers to limit tourist lets in specific areas.

## Who is affected

Owners who were planning to buy a flat to let to tourists in an apartment block are the most obvious group, since the new ban closes that route. Companies that manage holiday lets, and investors who hold tourist flats, will see higher penalties and tighter rules for platforms. Hotel owners are affected too, because the decree allows obsolete hotels to be converted into affordable housing.

Residents who rent long term are not directly changed by the decree. The Govern's stated aim is to stop the supply of tourist places from growing and to give owners an incentive to put homes into social or price-limited rental. Whether that reduces housing pressure is a question for the official data on prices and rents in the coming years, and this article does not forecast it.

## Enforcement

The decree makes platforms jointly responsible for illegal listings. Together with the national registration number, it gives the Govern and the island councils better tools to identify unlicensed lets. The Govern has inspectors, and the town halls have police powers. The decree's higher fines apply to listings that break the rules, and an owner caught operating without a licence faces the penalties set by the tourism law.

## What may change next

The decree's formal procedure means that the Parlament can amend it. The regional election is due on 23 May 2027, and the debate about holiday lets is likely to feature in that campaign. At national level, the general election on 29 November 2026 will produce a new Cortes, which may adopt further housing measures. Anyone planning to buy or manage a tourist let should check the position at the time of purchase.

## How to check the rules for your own property

- Look up your registration in the Govern's tourism register.
- Check your community of owners' statutes and minutes.
- Check the town hall's planning rules for your street.
- Check the island council's current ceiling on tourist places.
- Ask a lawyer who specialises in tourism law if you are buying.

## Questions people ask

**Does the decree affect hotel guests?** Not directly. It concerns the supply of tourist places and the penalties for illegal lets.

**Did the decree set the ceilings?** It asked island councils to set their own ceilings and define carrying capacity, rather than fixing the numbers for each island.

**Can I still buy a flat to let to tourists?** New tourist places cannot be created in multi-family buildings. Houses and other property types fall under different rules, so take advice before buying.
