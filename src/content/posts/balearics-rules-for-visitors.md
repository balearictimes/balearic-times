The Balearic Islands welcome visitors, but they also have rules that are enforced more firmly than in the past. Several of them target the party resorts, and some apply to everyone. Knowing them in advance avoids fines and awkward conversations with the police.

## Drinking in the street

In the islands' main party areas, drinking alcohol in the street is banned. Rules have been introduced for places such as Magaluf and Playa de Palma on Mallorca and the West End of Sant Antoni on Ibiza. You can drink inside bars, restaurants, hotels and at home, but not while walking along the road. Fines can reach into the thousands of euros for repeat or serious cases.

## Limits on promotions

The same zones restrict happy hours, open bars, bar crawls and similar promotions. Shops are limited in when they can sell alcohol at night, and the aim is to reduce excess drinking. Some all-inclusive hotels in those areas set their own limits on the number of drinks guests can have each day.

## Balconing

Jumping from or between balconies, or from a balcony into a pool, is called balconing. It causes deaths and serious injuries each year. Local authorities treat it as a risk to public safety, and the rules provide for heavy fines. Hotels may also throw out guests who do it. Do not take the risk.

## Noise and behaviour

Noise at night is regulated, and local police respond to complaints. Walking in the street in swimwear or without a shirt away from the beach is not permitted in many towns and can lead to a fine. Behaving in an antisocial way, such as urinating in public, can also attract penalties.

## Beaches and nature

- Do not take sand, shells or stones from the beach. Fines apply.
- Smoking is restricted on some beaches. Check signs at the entrance.
- Camping outside designated sites is banned, and sleeping in vehicles in some places is too.
- Respect protected areas, and stay on marked paths.
- Take your rubbish with you.

## Boats and anchoring

Seagrass meadows, called Posidonia, grow on the seabed around all the islands and protect the water and the coast. Anchoring on them is banned in most places, and fines for breaking this rule can be large. If you charter a boat, ask the skipper where you can anchor, and use official moorings where they are provided. Party boats have also been the target of new limits near the coast in some areas.

## Cars and bikes

Summer restrictions on vehicles apply on Ibiza and Formentera, and some roads in Mallorca, such as the road to the Formentor lighthouse, are closed to private traffic at certain times. Look up the current rules before you hire a vehicle or bring your own. Use a helmet on a scooter or moped, and carry your licence.

## Tourist rentals

Renting an unlicensed holiday flat is illegal and can lead to fines for owners. Check that your accommodation has a registration number, which should appear on the listing.

## Where to check

Rules vary by town and change from year to year. Check your hotel's guidance, the websites of the town halls and the Balearic government. If in doubt, ask your hotel reception, since staff know the local rules. For emergencies, call 112.

## The law behind the rules

In 2020 the Balearic government approved a decree-law on tourism excess, aimed at the party areas of Magaluf in Calvià, Playa de Palma in Palma and Llucmajor, and the West End of Sant Antoni on Ibiza. It banned happy hours, open bars and pub crawls in those zones, and restricted sales of alcohol in shops late at night. The decree was later turned into law by the regional parliament and has been extended and tightened. A further law, the Responsible Tourism rules, added stronger fines for behaviour such as balconing and for street drinking.

The details change, and the exact hours and fines are set out in the regional laws and in local ordinances. The Policia Local in each town enforces the rules.

## The party zones, in practice

In Magaluf, the strip of Punta Ballena is the focus. Bars and clubs operate in a limited zone, and the police patrol on foot. In Playa de Palma, the busiest sections are between balneario 5 and 6 and around the beer halls near balneario 15. In Sant Antoni, the West End streets are covered by the bar crawl ban, and visitors are told not to walk around with open drinks.

Hotels in these zones often have rules of their own. Some limit the number of drinks included in all-inclusive packages, and some have security staff who will refuse entry to anyone who is drunk.

## Balconing and falls

Balconing has caused a number of deaths of foreign visitors in Magaluf, Sant Antoni and other resorts. The authorities treat it as a risk to public order. Hotels may throw guests out and report them to the police. Insurance companies often refuse to pay for injuries caused by balconing. Do not climb on railings or jump into pools from balconies.

## Beaches and nature rules

Beaches are public up to the water line, but the rules vary by council. Many councils ban smoking on beaches, prohibit dogs in summer, and ban glass bottles. Inflatable boats, ball games and music may be restricted in busy areas. Collecting sand, stones or seagrass is banned in natural areas such as Es Trenc, Ses Salines and the Formentera park.

Parts of the coast are marked as protected, and walking on dunes is not allowed. In some natural parks, visitors must follow marked paths. Fines apply for lighting fires, which are a major risk in summer when forest fires can start easily.

## Boats and the sea

The Posidonia oceanica meadows around the islands are protected by law. Anchoring on seagrass is banned in many bays, and boats should use mooring buoys or anchor on sand. The Balearic government has a mapping tool that shows areas of seagrass. Fines can run to thousands of euros.

Party boats have been targeted by new rules in Palma and some other places. The rules limit the number of passengers, ban loud music near the coast and require boats to keep their distance from the shore. If you book a boat trip, choose a licensed operator and ask about the rules.

## Driving and scooters

Spanish traffic laws apply. The legal blood alcohol limit is 0.5 grams per litre for most drivers, and lower for new drivers. Helmets are compulsory on scooters and motorcycles. Using a phone while driving is banned. Hire companies may ask you to leave a deposit. Police checks are common in summer, especially near party areas.

## Rentals and accommodation

Holiday rentals must have a tourist rental registration number, which must appear in adverts. Unlicensed rentals are illegal, and councils such as Palma have banned new tourist rentals in some types of housing. If you rent privately, check that the property is registered, since an unlicensed rental can be closed down at short notice.

## Fines

Fines vary by offence and by municipality. Street drinking can result in fines of hundreds or thousands of euros. Fines for balconing can be much higher. Ignoring a fine can lead to bigger penalties later. Pay on the spot where permitted, or follow the instructions on the notice.

## If something goes wrong

Call 112 for emergencies. The British embassy and consulates can help with lost passports and serious problems. Carry a copy of your passport and your insurance details. Keep your hotel's address in your phone.

## Official information

The Balearic government and the town halls of Calvià, Palma, Llucmajor and Sant Antoni publish the local rules. The UK government's travel advice for Spain, at gov.uk, includes a section on local laws.
