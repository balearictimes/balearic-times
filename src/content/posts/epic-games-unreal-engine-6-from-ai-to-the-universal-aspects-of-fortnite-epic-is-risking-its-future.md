Epic Games' announcement of Unreal Engine 6 at State of Unreal 2026 was aimed at developers, but its plans could eventually change what players see and own in their games. Here is what it could mean.

## One engine, two worlds

Today, professional studios use Unreal Engine 5, while Fortnite's creators use a separate tool, Unreal Editor for Fortnite. Marcus Wassmer, who leads the UE6 project, says the two will be merged into a single product over the next two years. For players, that could mean a smaller gap between "Fortnite islands" made by the community and full games made by studios.

## Items that travel with you

The most eye-catching idea is interoperability: using open standards so that cosmetic items, skins and even accounts can move between different games built on UE6. Epic's own games, such as Fortnite and Rocket League, are expected to be the first test cases — a hat earned in one could, in theory, be worn in the other.

Whether other publishers will want to allow items bought in their games to be used elsewhere is another question.

## AI in the toolbox

UE6 will integrate artificial intelligence into the development process, with links to external AI services, to help creators generate assets, test games and automate repetitive tasks. Epic hopes this will allow smaller teams to make more ambitious games.

## A new language

UE6 will make Verse, the programming language Epic created for Fortnite, a core part of the engine, designed to work for anyone from a solo developer to huge teams.

## When will players notice?

Not soon. Developers will get early access at the end of 2027, and that phase is expected to last at least 12 to 18 months. Games built on UE6 are therefore unlikely to reach players before the end of the decade, and Epic says existing UE5 projects will be able to move across without starting again.

## The big picture

UE6 is a bet that the future of games looks more like Fortnite: connected worlds, user-created content and digital items that last beyond a single title. If it works, it could change how games are made and how players think about what they own.
