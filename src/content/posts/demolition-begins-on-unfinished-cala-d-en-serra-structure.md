## Demolition work underway
The Consell Insular d'Eivissa, Ibiza's island council, has started demolition work on the unfinished structure in Cala d'en Serra, a natural cove in the north of the island. According to the Consell, removing the abandoned construction will help restore the area's natural landscape and eliminate a feature that has altered the environment for decades.

## Background and funding
The demolition project is included in the Consell's 2027 budget, which was approved this week, with a allocation of 974,063.69 euros. The project has the support of the Impost de Turisme Sostenible, a sustainable tourism tax, in two phases. The first phase, which involved drafting the project, received 200,393.33 euros, while the second phase has funding of 1,256,754.86 euros, covering demolition, construction management, and safety coordination, as well as a future forest management plan for the area. In total, the sustainable tourism tax is contributing 1.46 million euros to the two phases.

## Environmental significance
The demolition work is taking place in an area declared to be of special natural interest, adjacent to the Natura 2000 network space in the north of Sant Joan. The Consell said the demolition will allow for the removal of the structures and progress in the environmental recovery of the site, while a future forest management plan will define the management actions for the area.

## Next steps
The removal of the unfinished structure is a key step in restoring the natural beauty of Cala d'en Serra, and the Consell's initiative is expected to have a positive impact on the area's environment and ecosystem. The future forest management plan will be crucial in ensuring the long-term conservation of the area.
