Housing is the issue that comes up first when people in the Balearic Islands are asked what worries them. This article explains the pressures that official sources describe and the main measures that national and regional authorities have introduced. It deliberately does not quote price figures from property websites or media reports, because the brief for this series is to rely on official sources. Where an official price series is relevant, the article points to where it is published. Details were last checked on 29 October 2026.

## The pressures

Three forces are widely discussed, and each has an official indicator.

**More people.** The Instituto Nacional de Estadística's population statistics show that the Balearics keep growing. For the second quarter of 2026, the INE's provisional continuous population statistics put growth in the Illes Balears at 0.36 per cent in the quarter, compared with 0.21 per cent for Spain as a whole. Spain's population reached 49,801,559 on 1 July 2026. A faster-growing population needs more homes in a place that has limited land.

**Demand from visitors.** The islands received 14,357,229 tourists between January and August 2026, according to IBESTAT's border tourism statistics. About 19 per cent of all jobs affiliated to Social Security in the islands are in tourism, which draws workers who need somewhere to live. Tourism also competes for housing stock when homes are used for holiday lets.

**Limited land.** The islands are small, large areas are protected and the construction rules restrict new building. Island territorial plans set limits on where building is allowed, and the island councils and town halls apply them.

Survey evidence suggests residents link these pressures. In the AETIB's 2024 survey, 77.2 per cent of residents saw a major impact of tourism on rising housing costs. That is perception, not a measurement of rents, but it is the official picture of public opinion.

## What national law offers

Spain's housing law, Ley 12/2023 por el derecho a la vivienda, gives autonomous communities the power to declare "tensioned" residential markets, areas where rents and prices are judged to be out of line with incomes. In such areas, the law allows measures to limit rent rises on new contracts. The law came into force in 2023, and the decision to declare an area tensioned rests with the regional government. The Housing Ministry publishes the list of declared areas.

The national rental registry, introduced by Real Decreto 1312/2024, aims to bring holiday and seasonal lets into the open. The reform of the horizontal property law in 2025 requires the agreement of three fifths of the owners before a flat in an apartment block can begin to be used as a tourist let. These measures are explained in a separate article.

## What the Balearic Govern has done

The Govern's Decreto ley 4/2025, of 11 April, bans new tourist places in multi-family buildings, caps the number of places overall, raises fines and makes platforms co-responsible for illegal listings. It also allows obsolete hotels to be converted into affordable housing and offers an 80 per cent reduction in a fine to an owner who transfers a sanctioned property for social or price-limited rental. The Govern includes housing policy in a single department, Housing, Territory and Mobility, whose conseller is José Luis Mateo Hernández, according to the Govern's website.

The Govern also owns land and housing through its public housing agency, the Institut Balear de l'Habitatge (IBAVI), which builds and manages public rental housing. IBAVI publishes its programmes on its own website.

## What island councils and town halls do

The island councils must set ceilings on tourist places and define the carrying capacity of each island, as the decree-law asks. Town halls control planning licences and local ordinances. Some of them have introduced restrictions on certain kinds of tourist let in their planning rules. The measures differ from municipality to municipality, so a resident or an investor must check the local plan.

## The tourist tax

The Sustainable Tourism Tax, collected from overnight guests in tourist accommodation, funds environmental and sustainability projects. The rates for 2026 are published by the Govern's tax agency, ATIB.

## What help exists for renters and buyers

Residents who struggle with rent can look at several channels, all official:

- The IBAVI, for public rental homes and housing programmes.
- The Housing Ministry's national programmes for young people and for rent subsidies, announced from time to time.
- Social services at the town hall, which can give advice on emergencies and on benefits.
- The Govern's housing department, which publishes calls for aid.

Anyone looking for help should check deadlines carefully. Calls for aid are published in the BOIB.

## What to watch

Several events could change the picture in the next year:

- **29 November 2026:** the general election. Housing was mentioned in the government's own statement announcing the vote as one of its pending reforms, and the next government will decide on any national housing legislation.
- **23 May 2027:** regional, island and municipal elections, which will decide who sets the planning and tourism rules locally.
- **New data:** the INE's rent and price statistics, and the Ministry's data on the rental registry, will show whether the measures are having an effect.

## Questions readers ask

**Has a tensioned area been declared in the Balearics?** The Housing Ministry publishes the list of declared areas. This article did not find one for the islands in the sources consulted, so check the Ministry's current list.

**Does the decree-law lower rents?** The Govern describes its aim as limiting the growth of tourist places and encouraging social and price-limited rental. The effects will show in the official data over time.

**Where can I see official rent data?** The INE and the Ministry of Housing publish rent and price statistics, and IBESTAT publishes regional series.

**Who decides on new housing?** Town halls grant building licences, island councils set territorial plans and the Govern sets regional housing policy.
