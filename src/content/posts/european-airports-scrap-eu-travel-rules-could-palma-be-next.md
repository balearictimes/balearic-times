Several European countries are reported to be relaxing the biometric checks required under the EU's new Entry/Exit System (EES) to avoid long queues during the summer — raising the question of whether Spain, and Palma airport, could do the same.

## What the EES requires

The Entry/Exit System became fully operational on 10 April 2026. On their first entry to the Schengen area after its introduction, travellers from outside the EU, including the UK, must:

- have a digital record created;
- give fingerprints;
- have their photograph taken.

The system is working smoothly at many airports, but at others it has caused queues of several hours.

## Greece and Italy

Greece has reportedly decided that British passport holders will not have to give fingerprints or facial images at its borders for the time being, a step confirmed by the Greek embassy as part of the flexibility allowed during the rollout.

Reports suggest Rome's two airports, Fiumicino and Ciampino, are considering a similar move to avoid what has been described as a potential "disaster" when summer crowds arrive.

## What about Palma?

Palma airport, one of the main gateways for British holidaymakers, has also experienced delays since the EES was introduced. Many travellers and tourism businesses would welcome a similar waiver, but the decision rests with the Spanish government and border police, and no change has been announced.

EU rules allow member states to suspend parts of the checks temporarily during the transition period when queues become excessive, so a change is possible if waiting times become unmanageable.

## Advice for travellers to Mallorca

- Allow extra time at the airport, both on arrival and departure.
- Check your airline's advice before travelling.
- Have your passport ready and follow staff instructions at border control.
- Remember that children must also be registered.

Once you have been registered in the EES, later trips should be quicker, as only a check against your existing record is needed.
