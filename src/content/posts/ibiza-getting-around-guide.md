Ibiza is small, about 40 kilometres from end to end, but the traffic can make short trips slow in July and August. How you move around depends on where you stay and how many beaches you want to see.

## Arriving at the airport

Ibiza airport sits a few kilometres south-west of Ibiza Town. A regular bus links it with the town and with Platja d'en Bossa, and taxis wait outside the terminal. Taxi fares are regulated, and the drivers use meters or set fares for common routes, so you should not need to haggle.

## Buses

The island has a network of public buses that connect Ibiza Town with Sant Antoni, Santa Eulària, Sant Joan and the main resorts. They are cheap and run often between the big towns in summer. Services to the smaller beaches are thinner and drop sharply outside the season, so look at the timetable before planning a day.

If you are staying in Ibiza Town, Sant Antoni or Santa Eulària, the bus is often enough. If you are in a villa in the countryside, it usually is not.

## Taxis and ride apps

Taxis can be hard to find at night in summer, especially around the clubs. Booking by phone or app ahead of time helps. Ranks outside the main venues build long queues in the early hours, so plan for a wait.

## Hiring a car or scooter

A car gives you the most freedom, and many visitors hire one for a few days to reach the northern and western coves. Book early for July and August, and read the hire terms about excess charges and damage to the underside, as some rental firms are strict.

Scooters are popular, but accidents involving visitors are common. Wear a helmet, check your insurance covers you, and remember that Ibiza's roads are narrow and busy in peak weeks.

## Limits on cars in summer

Ibiza's island council has introduced rules limiting the number of vehicles that can travel on the island during the busiest months. The system works through authorisations tied to the number plate, and it affects cars brought over by ferry as well as some rental arrangements. The dates, quotas and fees have changed from year to year, so check the official site, ibizacircular.es, and the ferry company's guidance before you travel. Motorbikes and mopeds have so far been left out of the scheme.

## Ferries to Formentera

Boats to Formentera leave from Ibiza Town's port and cross to La Savina in roughly half an hour on the fast services. Several companies run the route, and in summer you should book ahead for busy mornings. Day trips are popular, but you can also stay overnight.

## A simple rule

Pick a base near the beach or town you like best, use buses for the big journeys, and hire a car or taxi for the days you want to explore. That combination usually costs less and causes fewer headaches than a car for the whole week.

## Bus lines and routes in more detail

The island's buses are run by a network of private operators under the Consell d'Eivissa. The most useful routes for visitors link Ibiza Town with the airport, Platja d'en Bossa, Santa Eulària, Sant Antoni and Sant Joan de Labritja. Sant Antoni and Ibiza Town are joined by one of the most frequent lines, with frequent departures in the high season, and the journey takes about half an hour depending on traffic. Buses to Cala Comte, Cala Bassa and Cala Tarida leave from Sant Antoni in the summer.

The main bus stations are in Ibiza Town, near the port, and in Sant Antoni, near the seafront. Tickets can be bought on board, and contactless payment is accepted on most lines. Late-night services run on some routes in summer, though the last buses to the countryside usually leave before midnight.

## Airport to resort: what to expect

Ibiza airport has a single main terminal. Taxis queue outside arrivals, and the ride to Ibiza Town takes about 15 minutes, to Platja d'en Bossa about 10 minutes, to Santa Eulària about 30 minutes and to Sant Antoni about 25 to 35 minutes. At busy times the queue for a taxi can run to half an hour. Pre-booked transfers and the airport bus avoid the wait.

## The summer vehicle rules in practice

For the 2026 season the Consell d'Eivissa applied its vehicle limit from 1 June to 30 September, with a daily quota of authorisations. Vehicles taken to Ibiza by ferry need an authorisation, and fines for travelling without one can reach €10,000. Residents of other Balearic islands, seasonal workers and people with reduced mobility have separate arrangements. Ferry companies such as Baleària handle the authorisation for return tickets, and the island council runs the official portal at ibizacircular.es. The rules are reviewed each year, so confirm the dates and quotas before you book.

For visitors hiring a car on the island, rental firms are the ones that must meet the quota, so book through a licensed company and keep your rental contract with you.

## Cycling and walking

Ibiza has a growing network of cycle routes, though much of the island still has narrow roads without hard shoulders. The Santa Eulària promenade and the Ses Salines roads are suitable for easy rides. The village of Santa Gertrudis de Fruitera is a good starting point for gentle walks through fields and almond groves.

## Boats and water taxis

Boat taxis operate in the summer between Ibiza Town, Talamanca, Santa Eulària and a number of beaches. They are slower and more expensive than buses, but they can save a lot of time when the roads are jammed. From Sant Antoni, boats run trips along the west coast to the coves near Cala Comte and Es Vedra, and larger boats make day trips to Formentera.

## Getting between towns at night

After midnight, public buses run on only a handful of routes, so taxis become the main option. The two taxi ranks that matter most are at the Ibiza Town port and in the centre of Sant Antoni. Queues near the big clubs grow after four in the morning, so arrange your pick-up in advance if you can.

## Money-saving tips

- Buy a multi-trip bus card if you plan to travel daily.
- Share taxis with friends, since fares are by the vehicle.
- Stay close to a bus line and check the last departure before going out.
- If you hire a car, collect it on the day you need it and return it early to avoid paying for days spent in the town.

## Official information

The Consell d'Eivissa publishes bus timetables and the summer vehicle rules. The Baleària ferry site at balearia.com explains the authorisation process for vehicles, and Aena, which runs the airport, publishes arrival and departure information at aena.es.
