The Instituto Coordenadas de Gobernanza y Economía Aplicada, a Spanish think tank, has published a report to mark World Cancer Day naming twenty organisations it considers to be driving progress in cancer care in Spain.

## Progress through collaboration

The report's central argument is that advances in cancer no longer depend on a single actor. Jesús Sánchez Lambás, the institute's executive vice-president, says real progress comes when drug developers, research hospitals and the health system work together to bring new treatments to patients efficiently and fairly.

## Research centres and hospitals

The report highlights, among others:

- **Fundación Jiménez Díaz University Hospital** in Madrid, where Dr Jesús García-Foncillas leads work on bringing precision oncology into everyday practice;
- **the National Cancer Research Centre (CNIO)**, including Dr Mariano Barbacid's research on pancreatic cancer;
- **the Josep Carreras Leukaemia Research Institute**, a reference centre for blood cancers;
- **the Vall d'Hebron Institute of Oncology** in Barcelona, led by Dr Josep Tabernero;
- **the CAR-T programme at Hospital 12 de Octubre** in Madrid, led by Dr Joaquín Martínez López.

## Industry

Drug and technology companies named include Novartis, for radioligand therapy in metastatic prostate cancer; BeOne Medicines, for blood and lung cancers; AstraZeneca, which runs a global oncology hub in Barcelona; Gilead, for CAR-T cell therapies; and Novocure, whose tumour treating fields therapy for glioblastoma is now funded by the national health system.

## Specialist centres

The report also mentions the proton therapy centre at Quirónsalud, MD Anderson Cancer Center Madrid for early-phase clinical trials, IVI for fertility preservation in cancer patients, and a specialist skin cancer programme led by Dr Ricardo Ruiz.

## Patients and charities

Patient organisations and charities complete the picture: the Spanish Group of Cancer Patients (GEPAC), which campaigns for patients' rights; the Spanish Association Against Cancer (AECC), one of the country's main funders of cancer research; and the CRIS Cancer Foundation, which supports research in public hospitals.

## The challenge

The institute concludes that keeping this collaborative model going will determine whether Spain can turn scientific advances into real benefits for patients. A recurring theme is equal access: making sure that new treatments reach patients in every region, including islands such as the Balearics, where some advanced therapies still require travel to the mainland.
