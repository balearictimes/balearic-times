Spain's national parliament, the Cortes Generales, has two chambers: the Congreso de los Diputados, which holds the main power, and the Senado, which acts as a chamber of territorial representation. The Balearic Islands send members to both. This guide explains who they are and how their seats are decided. The information was last checked on 29 October 2026, a month before a general election, so it describes both the current situation and the process that replaces it.

## The Congreso: eight deputies

The Illes Balears elect eight deputies to the Congreso de los Diputados. The seat count comes from the official election guide published by the government for the vote on 29 November 2026. The Congreso has 350 seats in total. Each province gets at least two, Ceuta and Melilla have one each, and the remaining 248 are shared according to population. The Balearics, with eight, sit in the middle of the table: Madrid has 38 and Barcelona has 32.

Voters pick a closed list, and parties present a ranked list of eight names. Seats are distributed using the D'Hondt method, which gives a small advantage to bigger lists. In a constituency with only eight seats, a party needs a fairly high share of the vote, roughly one in ten or a little more, to win a seat, even though there is no formal threshold at that level.

The deputies elected in July 2023 held their seats until the dissolution of the chambers on 6 October 2026. Their names and groups are published on the Congreso's website, congreso.es, under the Illes Balears constituency. This article does not reproduce them, because the whole group changes after the next vote.

## Francina Armengol

The best-known Balearic figure in national politics is Francina Armengol, a deputy for the Illes Balears for the PSIB-PSOE, the Balearic branch of the Socialist party. She was President of the Congreso, the third highest office in the state in the protocol order, from 17 August 2023, according to the Congreso's own records. That role ended with the dissolution of the chamber on 6 October 2026. She now presides over the Diputación Permanente, the standing committee that acts on behalf of the Congreso while the chamber is dissolved. A former President of the Balearic Govern, she held that post before moving to Madrid.

## The Senado

The Senate has two kinds of members. The first are senators elected directly by voters in each province or island. Mallorca elects three. Menorca, Ibiza and Formentera each elect one. Voters in the three-seat constituency can mark a limited number of names, which gives the largest party a majority of the seats but leaves room for a second party to take one. The ballot paper explains how many names can be marked.

The second kind are senators appointed by the regional parliament. Each autonomous community designates one senator, and a further one for every million inhabitants. The Parlament de les Illes Balears chooses its senators after each regional election, and the choices reflect the balance of power in the regional parliament. The Senate's website lists the senators for the Balearics, including those designated by the Parlament and those elected on each island. Their mandate ends with the dissolution decree for those who were elected, and a new group will take office after the election.

## The Government's representative in the Balearics

The national government has its own representative in the islands, the Delegado del Gobierno en las Illes Balears. This person is appointed by the central government, not elected. The delegation coordinates state services in the islands, including the national police, the Guardia Civil and the civil protection services. The office is in Palma. The delegate's name changes when the central government changes, and it is published on the Delegation's page of the government's website.

## What the national chamber decides for the islands

The Cortes pass laws that affect the Balearics directly. Examples include:

- **Taxes and financing**: the system that decides how much money the Balearic Govern receives from the central state.
- **Housing law**: the national Ley 12/2023 por el derecho a la vivienda, which allows regions to declare tensioned residential markets, and the reform of the horizontal property law in 2025.
- **Immigration and residence**: rules for foreign nationals, including residence permits.
- **Transport**: airports and ports, and the discount that residents of the islands receive on air and sea fares.
- **Special regime for the islands**: the Statute and the Constitution recognise insularity as a factor to be taken into account, and Balearic politicians frequently argue in Madrid for a special financial treatment.

## What happens after 29 November

The new Cortes meet for their constituent sessions on 23 December 2026 at 10:00, as the decree says. Members of both chambers are sworn in, the chamber officers are elected, including the president of each chamber, and then the King consults the groups to propose a candidate for Prime Minister. A candidate needs a majority in the first vote in the Congreso, or more votes in favour than against in a second vote 48 hours later. If nobody succeeds within two months of the first vote, the Cortes are dissolved and a new election is called.

For the Balearics, the practical result is that eight deputies and the island senators will carry the islands' concerns into the new session, including those about housing, tourism, transport and financing. They also form part of national parliamentary groups, so they usually follow the party line on major votes.

## How to contact your representatives

Deputies and senators have public profiles on congreso.es and senado.es, with email addresses and office contacts. Many hold surgeries in their home constituency. Any resident can write to them, and parliamentary questions are public.

## Questions people ask

**Can I contact a deputy who does not live near me?** Yes. All deputies for the Balearic constituency represent the whole archipelago, and their contact details are on congreso.es.

**Who chooses the regional senators?** The Parlament de les Illes Balears, after each regional election.
