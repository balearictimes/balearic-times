Ibiza's nightlife is the reason many visitors come, and also the reason some residents want fewer of them. It runs on a seasonal calendar, a ticketing system and a few unwritten habits that are worth knowing before you land.

## The season

The big clubs usually open in May with opening parties and close at the end of September or early October with closing parties. July and August are the busiest weeks and the most expensive. June and September have many of the same names on the line-up, with smaller crowds and lower prices.

Outside the summer there is still music, but it moves to a handful of bars and smaller venues in Ibiza Town and Santa Eulària.

## The big names

The best-known venues include Pacha, Amnesia, Ushuaïa, Hï Ibiza, DC10 and Privilege. Each has a different style. Amnesia and Pacha are long-running institutions with a mix of house and techno. Ushuaïa is a daytime open-air hotel party that goes on into the night. DC10 has a reputation for underground sounds, and Privilege is among the largest clubs in the world by capacity.

Line-ups change each year, and each club tends to give its nights a theme and a resident promoter. Look at the schedule before you buy a ticket.

## Tickets and prices

Tickets for the big nights sell online ahead of time, and many sell out. Prices rise steeply in peak weeks and for big-name DJs. Door sales are usually more expensive and sometimes not available at all. Buy from the club's own site or a licensed reseller, and keep an eye on resale scams.

Drinks inside are expensive, and so are cloakrooms and bottle service. Plan what you will spend before you go in.

## Timing

Clubs rarely fill until after midnight and many run until six in the morning or later. A common pattern is to eat late, go out around one and stay until dawn. Day parties and sunset sessions are an easier introduction for people who do not want to stay out all night.

## Which area suits you

- **Ibiza Town and the harbour**: bars, cocktail spots and a mix of crowds. This is the most convenient base for clubbing, with the old town above.
- **Sant Antoni**: the west coast resort has the sunset strip and a lively bar area. Its streets are covered by local rules on drinking outdoors, and fines have been issued to visitors who ignore them.
- **Platja d'en Bossa**: beach clubs and big hotels, close to the airport.
- **Santa Eulària**: quieter, with restaurants and bars for people who want an evening out without the club scene.

## Staying safe

Drink water, avoid leaving drinks unattended, and arrange the journey home in advance. Taxis are scarce in the early hours, and queues are long. Take only what you need, since pickpockets work crowded areas. Be aware that the island has tightened its rules on drinking in the street and on bar crawls in recent years, so check the guidance for the area where you are staying.

## The main venues in more detail

Pacha, whose cherry logo is known worldwide, sits close to the marina in Ibiza Town and has run since 1973. It is among the oldest clubs on the island, and its nights tend to be house and disco led. Amnesia, on the road between Ibiza Town and Sant Antoni, is known for its large terrace and its main room. Privilege, a few minutes away near San Rafael, holds the title of one of the largest nightclubs in the world by capacity.

Hï Ibiza and Ushuaïa are both on or near Platja d'en Bossa, within walking distance of one another. Hï Ibiza opened in 2017 on the site of the former Space, and it is known for its big line-ups. Ushuaïa, attached to a large hotel, is an open-air venue where the music begins in the afternoon and carries on until late evening. DC10, near the airport, is the smaller of the group and is best known for its Monday afternoon parties held by the Circoloco collective. In Sant Antoni, Eden and Es Paradís are the clubs most associated with the West End area.

## Sunset first

Many nights begin with the sunset. On the Sant Antoni seafront, Café del Mar and Café Mambo opened in the 1980s and 1990s and helped make the bay famous for sunset music. Both are on the Passeig de ses Fonts, and queues for the best tables start early in the afternoon. In the south, Sa Trinxa on Ses Salines beach and the beach clubs of Cala Jondal host sunset sessions, while in the north Benirràs holds its traditional Sunday drum circle on the sand.

## Beyond the big clubs

Ibiza Town's old harbour has bars and small clubs close together, and the streets of Dalt Vila have quieter spots for a drink once the sun goes down. Santa Eulària has a handful of bars along its seafront. Live music venues exist in the north and centre of the island, and some hotels host concerts and DJs by the pool.

## Costs and planning

A ticket to a big-name night can cost well over €50, with premium areas costing much more. Drinks inside clubs are expensive, and a single cocktail can cost around €15 to €20. Entry to beach clubs may include a minimum spend for a table. Ask the club's staff about dress codes, since some venues turn away visitors in swimwear or flip-flops. If you want to see the island at its best on a lower budget, plan one or two nights out and spend the other evenings on the beach or in the harbour towns.

## Health and safety

The Spanish emergency number is 112, and nightclubs have medical staff on site in most cases. Do not accept drinks from strangers, and keep an eye on your friends. Taxis are available at ranks, and some bus routes run later in the high season. The rules on drugs are strict, and possession is punished by fines. Carrying your passport or an ID card is a legal requirement, and police checks around the clubs are common.

## Rules on drinking

Sant Antoni's West End, along the streets Carrer Santa Agnès and Carrer Sant Mateu, is the area most affected by the rules on street drinking and on bar crawls. Fines have been issued for drinking in the street and for promotional offers aimed at tourists. Check with your hotel for the latest list of areas covered by the rules.

## After the season

When the season ends in October, a handful of venues stay open for the locals. Ibiza Town's bars and the winter programme of concerts and exhibitions keep the town lively. The island's music culture continues in winter at a much smaller scale.
