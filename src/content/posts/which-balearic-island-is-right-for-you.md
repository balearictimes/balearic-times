The four main Balearic Islands lie within a short flight or ferry ride of each other, but they are quite different. Here is a straightforward way to choose between them.

## Mallorca: the all-rounder

Mallorca is the largest and the most varied island. It has resorts for every taste, a handsome capital in Palma, a dramatic mountain range in the Tramuntana, and beaches ranging from busy bays to quiet coves. It suits families, couples, cyclists, walkers and groups of friends. Flights are frequent and direct from many UK airports, and prices span a wide range, from budget packages to expensive villas.

The drawbacks are the crowds at popular spots in July and August and the party scene in a few resorts. The island is large, so a hire car helps for exploring.

## Menorca: the quiet one

Menorca is calmer, greener and slower than the others. It has pretty coves on the south coast, a long coastal path, prehistoric sites and a relaxed atmosphere. It suits families with young children, walkers and couples who want rest. Nightlife is low-key, and there are fewer flights, mainly in summer.

You will usually need a car to reach the best beaches. Prices are mid-range to high, and many places close in winter.

## Ibiza: the party island, and more

Ibiza is famous for its clubs, and in summer the nightlife is world class. The island also has a quieter side, with pine-covered hills, beautiful coves in the north and west, yoga retreats and a bohemian atmosphere. It suits friends who want to go out, couples, and travellers seeking sunsets and good restaurants.

Costs are among the highest in the Balearics in peak summer, especially for clubs, beach clubs and villas. Summer vehicle limits apply, so check the rules before you take a car.

## Formentera: the small escape

Formentera is tiny, flat and bike friendly, with some of the clearest water in the Mediterranean. It suits couples and families who want beaches, cycling and quiet evenings. There is no airport and no club scene, and most visitors arrive by ferry from Ibiza. Accommodation is limited and can be expensive in high season.

## Choosing by travel style

- **First visit with children**: Mallorca or Menorca.
- **Romantic break**: Menorca, Formentera or the north of Mallorca.
- **Group trip with clubbing**: Ibiza, or Magaluf on Mallorca for a cheaper, rowdier option.
- **Walking and cycling**: Mallorca's mountains, or Menorca's coastal path.
- **Short stay of three or four days**: Palma, Ibiza Town or Mahón, which have easy transport.
- **Combining two islands**: Mallorca and Menorca by ferry, or Ibiza and Formentera.

If you cannot choose, Mallorca offers the most options in a single trip, while Menorca and Formentera are better for visitors who know they want peace.

## Mallorca in more detail

Mallorca covers about 3,600 square kilometres, making it by far the largest of the islands. Palma, the capital, has a Gothic cathedral on the seafront, the round Bellver Castle, and an old town of narrow streets and courtyards. In the north, the old walled town of Alcúdia and the bays of Pollença and Alcúdia suit families. The Serra de Tramuntana is a walkers' range with villages such as Valldemossa, Deià and Sóller. The south-east has coves around Santanyí. Cala Ratjada and Cala Millor are on the east coast, and Magaluf, Palma Nova and Playa de Palma are the large resorts.

Mallorca is also the place for cycling. Spring and autumn bring thousands of cyclists to the island, and Port d'Alcúdia, Port de Pollença and Can Picafort are popular bases.

## Menorca in more detail

Menorca covers about 700 square kilometres. The island has two historic towns, Mahón and Ciutadella, and a coast of coves. The south coast has Macarella, Turqueta and Mitjana, and the north coast has Cavalleria and Pregonda. The island is a UNESCO Biosphere Reserve. It has prehistoric Talayotic sites, the Camí de Cavalls footpath, and a food culture built around cheese, lobster stew and gin. Hotels are smaller and the atmosphere is calm.

Menorca suits visitors who prefer a slower holiday, with long days on the beach, a hike along the coast and a quiet evening in a harbour restaurant. The island has a family-friendly feel and fewer big clubs.

## Ibiza in more detail

Ibiza covers about 570 square kilometres. Ibiza Town is a UNESCO World Heritage site, with the walled upper town of Dalt Vila above the harbour. Sant Antoni is the sunset town, Santa Eulària the family and marina town on the east, and Sant Joan and the north are quiet and green. Beaches such as Cala Comte, Cala Salada, Benirràs and Ses Salines show the island's range. The clubs, Pacha, Amnesia, Ushuaïa, Hï Ibiza and DC10, draw international crowds from May to October.

The island also has a long tradition of alternative culture, with hippy markets at Las Dalias and Es Canar, yoga retreats, and a thriving food scene. It is possible to have a calm, scenic holiday in the north of Ibiza without going near a club.

## Formentera in more detail

Formentera covers about 83 square kilometres, and nearly all visitors arrive by ferry from Ibiza. The beaches of Ses Illetes, Llevant and Migjorn, the lighthouses at La Mola and Cap de Barbaria, and the salt flats of Ses Salines are the main sights. The island is flat, so cycling is easy. Nightlife is low-key, and the evenings are for sunset and dinner.

Formentera suits couples, families with older children, cyclists and anyone who wants a quiet beach holiday. It can be expensive, and accommodation is limited, so book well in advance.

## Matching the island to the traveller

- **A couple wanting romance and views**: Menorca, Formentera, or the Tramuntana villages of Mallorca.
- **A group of friends wanting nightlife**: Ibiza for clubs, Magaluf or Palma for a cheaper evening out.
- **A family with young children**: Alcúdia and Muro in Mallorca, Cala Galdana and Son Bou in Menorca, or Santa Eulària in Ibiza.
- **Active travellers**: Mallorca for cycling and mountain walking, Menorca for the Camí de Cavalls.
- **Foodies**: Menorca for cheese and lobster stew, Mallorca for markets and wine, Ibiza for restaurants.
- **People who dislike crowds**: Menorca and Formentera, in June or September.

## Time of year

July and August are crowded everywhere. For the best balance of weather and space, choose June or September. For winter, Mallorca offers the most open services, while Menorca, Ibiza and Formentera are much quieter.

## Costs

Ibiza and Formentera are generally the most expensive in summer. Mallorca has the widest range, from budget packages to luxury villas. Menorca sits in the middle, with higher prices at the coastal resorts. Eating in local restaurants away from the beach lowers costs on all four islands.

## A simple decision guide

If you want one island with everything, choose Mallorca. If you want calm and nature, choose Menorca. If you want music and sunsets, choose Ibiza. If you want clear water and a slow pace, choose Formentera, ideally combined with Ibiza.

## Official information

The tourism offices of each island council publish visitor guides, event calendars and maps, and the Spanish tourist office can advise on travel from the UK.
