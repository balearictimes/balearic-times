Menorca rewards visitors who leave the beach for part of the day. The island has some of the best-preserved prehistoric monuments in the western Mediterranean, two very different historic towns and a coastal path that circles the entire island.

## The Talayotic monuments

Menorca is dotted with stone structures built by the island's early inhabitants more than 2,000 years ago. The talayots are round or square towers, and the taulas are T-shaped stone monuments, thought to have had a ritual role. In 2023 UNESCO listed the Talayotic Culture of Menorca as a World Heritage site. Sites such as Torre d'en Galmés and Trepucó can be visited, and the Naveta d'en Tudons, a boat-shaped burial chamber near Ciutadella, is one of the oldest buildings in Europe still standing.

## Ciutadella

Ciutadella was the island's capital until the early 18th century. It has a compact old town with narrow lanes, a cathedral, noble houses and a small harbour surrounded by restaurants. Its Sant Joan fiesta in June, with horses and riders in the streets, is the most famous in the Balearics. Visit in the evening, when the port is at its liveliest.

## Mahón

Mahón stands above one of the largest natural harbours in the world. The town has a distinctly Georgian look in places, with sash windows and red-painted doors, left over from the British, who ruled the island for much of the 18th century. You can take a boat trip around the harbour, walk the waterfront and visit the market.

## Lighthouses and viewpoints

The Far de Favàritx on the north-east coast stands on a bare, dark landscape of slate and rock. The drive to it is part of the experience. Far de Cavalleria, on the north coast, offers views along cliffs and out to sea. Both are best seen at sunset, when the light is soft.

## Fornells and the north coast

Fornells is a small fishing village on a large bay, popular for watersports, including windsurfing. A short walk takes you to a watchtower with views of the bay. It is also the home of lobster stew.

## The Camí de Cavalls

The Camí de Cavalls was originally built to defend the island and now serves as a footpath that runs round the whole coast, about 185 kilometres long. Short stretches are suitable for families, and the section between Cala Galdana and Cala Macarella passes through woodland. Carry water, wear sturdy shoes and avoid midday heat.

## Cova d'en Xoroi

Cova d'en Xoroi is a cave bar built into a cliff near Cala en Porter, with a terrace overlooking the sea. It is popular for sunset drinks, and tickets can be expensive in summer. Book ahead for the peak months.

## Rainy day options

Mahón's museum has displays on the island's history, and the Mahón-Menorca cheese farms and Xoriguer distillery offer tours. A food market or a long lunch is a fair choice for a grey day.

## Prehistoric Menorca in more detail

The island has more than 1,500 prehistoric sites, one of the densest in Europe. Torre d'en Galmés, near Alaior, is the largest Talayotic settlement, with three talayots, a taula enclosure and a water cistern. Trepucó, near Mahón, has a large taula and a talayot. Talatí de Dalt, also close to Mahón, has a well-preserved taula. Naveta d'en Tudons, on the road from Ciutadella to Mahón, is a collective burial chamber built around 1000 BC. At Cala Morell on the north coast, a set of rock-cut tombs overlooks the sea.

## Mahón and the harbour

The harbour stretches for around five kilometres, and boat trips leave from the port. Isla del Rey, an island in the middle of the harbour, has the ruins of a naval hospital that dates from the 18th century. La Mola, a fortress at the harbour mouth, was built in the 19th century and can be visited. Fort Marlborough, built by the British, has tunnels and exhibits. The Museu de Menorca, in a former convent, covers the island's history, and the church of Santa Maria has a baroque organ. Plaça de s'Esplanada is the town's main square.

## Ciutadella in more detail

The cathedral, built on the site of a mosque, is a Gothic building. Plaça des Born, the main square, has a plinth commemorating the Turkish attack of 1558. Around it stand the Ajuntament, the Palau Saura and the Palau Martorell. Ciutadella's market is held in a covered hall, and the narrow harbour is lined with restaurants. The fiesta of Sant Joan, on 23 and 24 June, includes a procession of horses and riders and a lively mix of music and dancing in the streets.

## Natural areas

S'Albufera des Grau is a natural park in the north-east, with a lagoon, woodland and wetland. Birdwatchers visit the park, and a visitor centre explains the wildlife. Cap de Favàritx, with its lighthouse, stands in a lunar landscape of black slate. The north coast has the red cliffs of Pregonda and the lighthouse at Cavalleria, built in the 19th century. The south coast has the cliffs of Cala en Porter and the gorge of Algendar, which runs down to Cala Galdana.

## Activities

- **Walking**: sections of the Camí de Cavalls from any cove.
- **Cycling**: roads through the interior.
- **Horse riding**: Menorca is famous for its black horses, called Menorquín, which take part in the Ciutadella fiesta.
- **Watersports**: sailing, kayaking and diving, especially around Fornells and the south coast.
- **Boat trips**: from Mahón, Ciutadella and Cala Galdana.

## Food and drink stops

A visit to the Xoriguer distillery in Mahón shows how local gin is made. Cheese farms around Alaior offer tastings. In Fornells, a lunch of lobster stew caps a day on the north coast.

## Rainy day options

Mahón's museum, the churches of Ciutadella and the cheese factory at Alaior are good for bad weather. The Hauser & Wirth gallery on Isla del Rey, which opened in 2021, shows modern art and has a restaurant and a garden on the island, reached by a short boat trip from Mahón.

## Sunsets

The best sunset spots include the lighthouse at Cavalleria, the cliffs near Cala en Porter, the port in Ciutadella and the cliff-top bars at Cala en Porter.

## Practical tips

- Book boat trips ahead in summer.
- Carry water and sun protection for the prehistoric sites, which have little shade.
- Many sites close on Mondays or have limited winter opening, so check before you go.
