Formentera is a seasonal island. Its beaches, restaurants and boats follow the summer, and a visit in January feels very different from one in July. The right month depends on what you want most: sun, space or both.

## High season: July and August

These are the busiest and hottest months. The sea is warm, daytime temperatures often exceed 30°C and the main beaches are crowded at midday. Accommodation is expensive and fills up early. Ferries from Ibiza are busy, and traffic on the narrow roads can be heavy. If you go, book as early as possible and start your beach days early.

## June and September

Many regulars say these are the best months. The sea is warm, the beach clubs and restaurants are open, and the crowds are smaller. Prices are lower than in peak season, and the evenings are long and warm. September in particular has settled weather and calm water.

## May and October

Early May and early October can be good, with pleasant days and quiet beaches, though the sea is cooler. Some places have not yet opened or have already closed for the season. October often brings the first autumn rain.

## Winter

From November to April, many hotels, restaurants and rental shops are closed. Ferries run less often, and the island is very quiet. The weather is mild compared with northern Europe, but it can be windy and wet. It suits walkers and people who want solitude, though you should check what is open and how you will get there.

## Nightlife

Formentera is known for its calm. Unlike Ibiza, it has no large clubs, and the evening is mostly about sunset, dinner and a drink on a terrace. Es Pujols is the liveliest area, with bars, restaurants and some music. Some beach bars host DJs or live music on summer evenings, but the volume is limited by local rules.

Many visitors go to Ibiza for a night out and return by ferry. Late boats run in summer, but the last departures vary, so check the timetable before you go and keep a back-up plan in case of delays.

## Weather notes

- Summer is dry, with little rain between June and August.
- The wind can turn the sea choppy, so look at the forecast before taking a boat or kayak.
- Evening temperatures cool a little even in July, so pack a light layer.
- The sun is strong. Use high-factor sunscreen and drink water.

## A short summary

- **Best weather and sea**: June to September.
- **Best balance of space and warmth**: June and September.
- **Quietest**: November to April, with many closures.
- **Most lively**: July and August.

## Month by month on the island

In January and February, almond and fig trees are bare, and the island is almost empty. Many hotels and restaurants are closed, and ferries run a reduced timetable. March and April bring spring flowers, and the first walkers and cyclists arrive. Easter sees some hotels reopen, but the sea is still cold.

In May, the season begins. Beach restaurants at Ses Illetes and Migjorn reopen, and ferries add sailings. The sea is cool, around 18°C, but the beaches are quiet and prices are low. June warms up, with sea temperatures reaching about 21°C. The feast of Sant Joan, on 23 and 24 June, is celebrated with bonfires in some villages.

July and August are the hottest and busiest months. The sea warms to 25°C or more, and Formentera sees thousands of day trippers from Ibiza. Ferry queues are long in the morning, and the cycle paths and beaches are crowded at midday. The feast of Sant Jaume, on 25 July, is the island's main festival and is celebrated in Sant Francesc.

September is calm and warm. The crowds drop, the sea remains at about 24°C, and restaurants stay open. October is changeable, with the first autumn storms. Many seasonal businesses close at the end of the month. November and December are quiet, with local fiestas and few visitors.

## Weather and wind

Formentera is dry and very sunny for most of the year. The island is flat and exposed, so the wind can make the beach less comfortable. The tramontana from the north and the migjorn from the south change the sea conditions on either side of the island. In summer, the breeze is often light and welcome.

## Evenings in more detail

Es Pujols is the busiest village in the evening. Its seafront has bars, restaurants and shops, and a handful of venues play music until late. Sant Ferran de ses Roques has a lively square with a few bars, and it is where many residents go for a drink. Es Pilar de la Mola has its market in the afternoon in summer and a quiet atmosphere after dark. Beach restaurants at Migjorn and Ses Illetes host sunset sessions, with DJs or live musicians.

Most visitors who want a full club night take an evening ferry to Ibiza. The last boats back usually leave in the early hours in summer, but the schedule varies. If you plan to go, check the last sailing and book a ticket in advance.

## Events and festivals

- **Easter**: processions in Sant Francesc.
- **23 and 24 June**: bonfires and Sant Joan celebrations.
- **25 July**: Sant Jaume in Sant Francesc.
- **Late summer**: village fiestas with dances in traditional costume.
- **Autumn**: small village events and the end of the season.

## Choosing by interest

For swimming and beaches, June to September is best. For cycling and walking, April, May and October have milder temperatures. For birdwatching, spring and autumn migrations bring many species to the lagoons. For a quiet break, the weeks either side of the main season offer the best balance of services and calm.

## Costs

Accommodation is most expensive from mid-July to the end of August. Prices fall in June and September, and are lowest in winter, when many places are closed. Hire bikes and scooters are cheaper in the shoulder months.

## Official information

The Consell de Formentera publishes events and visitor information. AEMET, at aemet.es, provides weather forecasts. Ferry operators publish timetables, and these change between seasons.
