## Introduction to the New Regulations
The Consell d'Eivissa, Ibiza's island council, has approved a new set of regulations aimed at protecting the night environment. The regulations, which were passed unanimously, will apply to the entire island, including the coastline, smaller islands, and islets. The goal of the new rules is to reduce light pollution, improve energy efficiency, and ensure that nighttime lighting is compatible with the safety of people, economic activity, and the protection of the territory.

## Key Provisions of the Regulations
The regulations divide the island into four zones based on their vulnerability to light pollution. Zone E1, which requires the highest level of protection, includes natural protected areas, Natura 2000 network areas, and areas of special natural interest. The regulations also establish reference points that require maximum protection due to their astronomical, landscape, or biodiversity characteristics. These points include the Cala d'Hort Telescope Astronomical Observatory and the Ses Salines Natural Park. The Consell d'Eivissa may declare new reference points in the future if areas with specific wildlife values are detected.

## Implementation and Enforcement
The new regulations will apply to all outdoor lighting installations, both public and private, as well as indoor installations that project light outdoors. The regulations set limits for the intensity, color, and direction of lighting, and require that lighting be turned off when not in use, except in cases where safety reasons justify its use. The regulations also establish specific rules for different types of lighting, including street lighting, ornamental lighting, and advertising lighting. The Consell d'Eivissa will maintain a register and cartography of the island's lighting zoning and will provide technical assistance to municipalities and individuals.

## Compliance and Sanctions
New lighting installations that begin the permitting process six months after the regulations come into effect will have to comply with the new rules. Existing installations will have to adapt to the new regulations within a maximum period of four years, although some public lighting installations and sports facilities may have up to eight years to comply. The regulations also establish a system of sanctions, with fines ranging from 215 to 86,000 euros for non-compliance. As Conseller Ignacio Andrés noted, "protecting the night environment does not mean turning off Ibiza, it means lighting it better, with more efficiency, more responsibility, and more legal security for everyone." The regulations will come into effect 20 days after their publication in the Official Gazette of the Balearic Islands.
