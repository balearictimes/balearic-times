The organiser of the Mallorca Live Festival has said it suspects "private interests" are behind a legal challenge to the permits for major music events in Calvià, including the festival itself and the Es Jardí concert venue.

## The legal action

The Balearic leisure and entertainment association Abone, which represents nightlife businesses, has filed an administrative appeal against the authorisations granted by Calvià town council for Mallorca Live and Es Jardí, and asked the courts to suspend them as a precautionary measure while the case is decided.

## The organiser's view

The promoter, La Isla & Co, has defended the legality of its events and suggested that the case is motivated by competition for audiences rather than by genuine concerns about compliance. Large outdoor events attract thousands of people on the same nights that clubs and bars in the area are trying to fill their own venues.

## The other side

Associations representing nightlife businesses have argued in the past that large-scale events should be subject to the same requirements on licensing, noise and safety as permanent venues, and that they should compete on equal terms. Their case is that the rules must be applied consistently to everyone.

## Why it matters

Mallorca Live, held at the former Aquapark site in Magaluf, has become one of the island's biggest music events, attracting tens of thousands of people and international artists. Es Jardí hosts a summer programme of concerts and club nights. Both are part of Calvià's strategy to reposition Magaluf away from its image of excess towards higher-quality tourism.

Promoters argue that events of this kind generate significant economic, tourist and social benefits for the municipality and for Mallorca as a whole. Residents in some areas, meanwhile, have raised concerns about noise and traffic on event nights.

## Update

In late July, a Palma administrative court rejected the request to suspend the permits, finding that the applicant had not shown the harm required to justify a precautionary measure. The authorisations remain in force while the main case continues. The organisers welcomed the decision, saying it gave legal certainty to artists, workers, sponsors and the public, and said they would continue to work with the authorities to ensure their events comply with the rules and fit in with local life.
