The most common way to run a business in Spain is through a sociedad limitada, usually called an SL. It limits the owners' liability to the capital they put in, and it can have a single owner. A company registered in Spain can trade from the Balearic Islands, hire staff, hold property and open a bank account. This guide follows the official guide to creating an SL published by the Spanish government's small business portal, ipyme.org, and adds what is specific to the islands. It is general information, not legal or tax advice. It was last checked on 29 October 2026, and procedures are updated from time to time.

## Before you start: choose the form

The main options for a business in Spain are to work as a self-employed person (autónomo), to form an SL, or to open a branch of a foreign company. The self-employed route is simpler but leaves the owner personally liable for debts. An SL separates the company's assets from the owner's. A separate article covers the autónomo route. This one covers the SL.

## Minimum capital and partners

The government guide says the minimum number of partners is one. The share capital cannot be less than 1 euro and must be fully subscribed and paid up at incorporation. Contributions can be cash or assets with economic value, but not work or services. Cash contributions need a bank certificate showing the deposit for each partner. In practice, advisers often recommend a capital that reflects the real needs of the business and the expectations of banks and suppliers, but the legal minimum is 1 euro under the creation and growth law, Ley 18/2022.

## Step 1: reserve the company name

The first step is to request a negative name certificate from the Registro Mercantil Central, which confirms that no other company has the same name. It can be requested online, in person, by post or through the notary. The certificate is valid for three months and can be renewed for another three. A name can also be reserved for six months through the register of company names.

## Step 2: prepare the proof of capital

Each partner pays in their contribution and obtains a bank certificate. If partners contribute assets rather than cash, documents are needed to justify their value, and the notary should be consulted first. Partners married under a regime of separate property must bring their marriage contract.

## Step 3: file the electronic form

The Documento Único Electrónico (DUE), filed through the CIRCE system, starts the process electronically. You can send it yourself with a digital certificate or through a Punto de Atención al Emprendedor (PAE), an advice point run by public and business bodies. Sending the form books an appointment with a notary. The guide recommends printing the form and checking it before sending.

## Step 4: sign the deed

Signing the public deed (escritura pública) before a notary is mandatory. You bring the capital deposit certificate, the negative name certificate and the partners' identity documents. The deed must be signed within six months of the issue of the name certificate. The notary sends the information to the tax authority, which issues a provisional tax identification number (NIF).

## Step 5: tax registration and transfer tax

The company then files its census registration with the Agencia Tributaria, using form 036 or 037, before it starts activity. The request for the NIF must be made within a month of incorporation. Company incorporation is exempt from the transfer tax, but the exemption still has to be documented with the regional tax authority, and CIRCE obtains that for you. The returns must be filed even though no tax is due.

## Step 6: register with the Registro Mercantil

The registrar of the province checks the deed and records the company, which gives it full legal personality. The guide says registration is generally expected within one month of the deed. After that, the Registro sends the data back to the notary, and the tax authority issues the definitive NIF.

## Step 7: Social Security

The company gets an employer account code with Social Security and registers its partners and workers. A partner who works as an administrator or manager may have to register as self-employed within 30 days. An employer must register before hiring, and workers must be affiliated before they start. The article on hiring explains this in more detail.

## Step 8: licences and books

Licences depend on the activity and the municipality. A shop, a bar or a workshop needs approval from the town hall, as another article in this series sets out. Some activities need registration with sector authorities. The company's accounting books must be legalised electronically within four months of the end of the financial year.

## Costs and time

The government guide does not give a single fee for incorporation. It notes that for companies with capital of 3,100 euros or less, the notary and registry fees follow a reduced scale under Real Decreto-ley 13/2010 when the company is created through CIRCE. Costs also include the name certificate, the notary's fees, the registry's fees and any adviser. Timing depends on appointments and on the speed of each office, and the guide sets deadlines rather than a fixed total.

## If you are a foreign founder

Foreign partners need a NIE, the foreigner's identification number. Non-resident partners who simply invest capital need a NIE for economic purposes. A foreign partner who will work in the company needs a NIE and, if from outside the EU, a residence and work authorisation for self-employment. A married partner has to supply the spouse's identity document. A foreign investor may also have to report the investment to the Spanish authorities for statistical purposes, so ask the notary or adviser.

## After the company exists

- Open a business bank account.
- Keep accounting records and file VAT and corporate tax returns on time.
- Register for any sector licences.
- If you hire staff, follow the employer registration steps.
- Consider a gestoría, a local administration firm, for the monthly paperwork.
