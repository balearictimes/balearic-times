Cats have a reputation for independence, but research suggests that their owners' personalities may have a significant influence on their health and behaviour.

## The study

Researchers from the University of Lincoln and Nottingham Trent University in the UK surveyed more than 3,300 cat owners about their own personalities and about their cats' health and behaviour.

They found that owners who scored higher on neuroticism — a personality trait linked to anxiety and worry — were more likely to report:

- health problems in their cats;
- weight problems;
- stress-related behaviour, such as aggression, fearfulness or hiding.

## Not a matter of blame

The researchers stress that this does not mean anxious owners directly cause problems in their cats. The likely explanation is that an owner's emotional state shapes the home environment: how predictable routines are, how often there is playtime, and how calm the home feels. Cats are highly sensitive to these cues.

## Why routine matters

Cats thrive on predictability. When their owner is stressed, meals may be irregular, play forgotten and the house noisier. Changes like these can raise a cat's stress levels and lead to:

- avoiding the litter tray;
- urine marking;
- digestive upsets;
- over-grooming and hair loss.

Vets in Spain report seeing more of these stress-related problems as cat ownership grows, and say many are linked to the environment rather than to illness.

## Cats have personalities too

Cats are not blank slates. A separate study of more than 2,800 cats identified five main personality dimensions: neuroticism, extraversion, dominance, impulsiveness and agreeableness. Each cat has a fairly stable temperament, and the home environment influences how it is expressed — a shy cat may withdraw further in a chaotic house, while a confident one may cope well.

## Simple steps

- **Keep regular feeding and play times.**
- **Provide enough resources:** one litter tray per cat plus one, water bowls, scratching posts and hiding places.
- **Play every day:** ten minutes with a wand toy makes a difference.
- **Introduce changes gradually,** and give your cat a safe retreat.
- **Look after yourself:** a calmer home benefits everyone in it, including the cat.
