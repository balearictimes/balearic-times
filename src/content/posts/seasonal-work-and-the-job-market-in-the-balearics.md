If you want to understand work in the Balearic Islands, look at a calendar. Hotels open in spring, fill in summer, and thin out in October. Bars and restaurants hire in April and release staff in the autumn. Thousands of people move to the islands for the season and thousands more leave when it ends. This guide explains how that cycle works, what the official statistics say and what rules apply to the people involved. It uses data from the Consell Econòmic i Social (CES), the Balearic tourism agency (AETIB), the Instituto Nacional de Estadística (INE) and the guides published by the Spanish government on starting a business and hiring. It was last checked on 29 October 2026.

## The size of the swing

The CES reports that in July 2024 there were 34.9 per cent more workers registered with Social Security in the islands than in December. In 2024, the average number of affiliates over the year was 607,250, a historical high. The difference between the peak and the trough therefore runs into the tens of thousands of people.

The INE's Labour Force Survey for the third quarter of 2026, the summer quarter, gave the Balearics an unemployment rate of 4.63 per cent, the lowest of any region. The annual average rate was 8.2 per cent in 2024, according to the CES, which shows how much the summer lowers the figure compared with the rest of the year.

## Which jobs are seasonal

Tourism accounts for 94,706 registered workers, or 19.1 per cent of the total, in the fourth quarter of 2025. Many of the seasonal jobs are in:

- **Hotels and apartments:** reception, housekeeping, kitchens and maintenance.
- **Restaurants and bars:** waiting staff, cooks and bar staff.
- **Beach and leisure:** beach clubs, boat trips, water sports and tour guiding.
- **Retail and transport:** shops, car hire, taxis and delivery.
- **Construction and maintenance:** many hotels and villas renovate in winter, before the season begins.

Winter has its own work. Public administration, health, education, supermarkets, building work and maintenance keep going all year.

## The contract types

Spanish labour law offers several ways to hire people for part of the year. The most relevant is the fijo discontinuo, a permanent contract in which the employee works in the periods when the business needs them and is called back each season. The worker remains an employee between seasons and, if they are not called, may be entitled to unemployment benefit depending on the contributions they have made. Fixed-term contracts are also used, but labour reforms in recent years have narrowed the cases in which they are allowed.

The AETIB reports that the temporary contract rate in tourism was 13.6 per cent in the fourth quarter of 2025, up 4.3 percentage points. Employers and workers should check the current collective agreement for hospitality in the Balearics, which sets pay scales and working conditions by job category. Collective agreements are published in the Butlletí Oficial de les Illes Balears.

## Registration steps for employers

According to the guide to starting as an autónomo published by the Spanish government's small business portal (ipyme.org), an employer must:

- Register with the Tesorería General de la Seguridad Social as an employer before hiring.
- Affiliate each worker with Social Security before they start work.
- Register each employment contract with the public employment service (SEPE) within 10 days of signing.
- Notify the regional labour authority of the opening of the workplace, either before opening or within 30 days after.
- Display the work calendar at the workplace.

Those are the legal minimums. The rules on working hours, rest periods and overtime follow the Workers' Statute.

## For workers arriving from abroad

A person who wants to work in the islands needs the right to work. European Union citizens can work in Spain without a permit. They still need a NIE, the foreigner's identification number, to sign a contract and register with Social Security. British citizens and other non-EU nationals need a work and residence authorisation, unless they qualify under another route. The Ministry of Foreign Affairs and the immigration offices publish the requirements and forms. Working without registration is illegal and leaves a person without cover in an accident.

The minimum wage in 2026 is 1,221 euros per month in 14 payments, which is 17,094 euros a year, set by Real Decreto 126/2026. Collective agreements in hospitality often set higher pay for specific roles. Accommodation is a practical problem for many seasonal workers, and some employers offer staff housing.

## Unemployment support

Workers who have paid enough contributions can claim unemployment benefit when a contract ends. The public employment service handles claims and training. The Balearic employment service, the SOIB, runs job portals, training courses and orientation. The details of eligibility, such as the number of days of contributions required, are set by the national rules and published by the SEPE. Workers should apply as soon as their contract finishes, since deadlines apply.

## What happens in the off-season

The AETIB reports that in the fourth quarter of 2025 there were 19,400 unemployed people who had been unemployed for less than a year, up 14.1 per cent, and the 2025 annual average was 20,400, down 16.0 per cent. Those figures suggest that many of the unemployed in the islands are seasonal workers who move in and out of employment, rather than people who have been out of work for years.

Some workers move to winter jobs elsewhere, others train, and some run their own small businesses. The self-employed route is explained in a separate article on setting up a business.

## Tips for finding work

- Start looking early, since many employers hire in March and April.
- Check that the employer registers you with Social Security from the first day.
- Ask about accommodation before accepting.
- Learn basic Spanish, and Catalan if you can. It is a real advantage in most jobs.
- Keep copies of contracts and payslips.
