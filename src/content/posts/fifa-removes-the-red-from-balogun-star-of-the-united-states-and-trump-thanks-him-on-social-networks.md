FIFA has lifted the automatic suspension of United States striker Folarin Balogun, allowing him to play in the World Cup round of 16 against Belgium despite being sent off in the previous round.

## The red card

In the round-of-32 match against Bosnia and Herzegovina, Balogun was shown a straight red card for a hard tackle that left an opponent with an ankle injury. A straight red card normally carries an automatic suspension for the next match.

## FIFA's decision

FIFA announced that, under article 27 of its disciplinary code, the application of Balogun's automatic one-match suspension would be suspended for a probationary period of one year. In practice, this means he is free to play against Belgium.

## Reactions

The decision surprised many observers. Belgium had expected to face the United States without their striker. US coach Mauricio Pochettino said there had been no bad faith on Balogun's part in the challenge.

US President Donald Trump thanked FIFA on social media for "doing the right thing" and "reversing a great injustice". The message drew attention to Trump's close relationship with FIFA president Gianni Infantino, which has been widely noted during the tournament co-hosted by the United States.

## Controversy

Critics questioned the decision, arguing that suspending an automatic ban is highly unusual and risks creating the impression that the host nation receives special treatment. Supporters of the decision argued that the disciplinary code allows such measures and that the challenge was not malicious.

## What's at stake

If the United States beat Belgium, they will meet the winner of the match between Spain and Portugal in the quarter-finals. Balogun's availability could be important for the Americans, who are hoping to make the most of home advantage.

## The bigger picture

The episode adds to the debate over the governance of football and the relationship between FIFA and political leaders. Whatever the outcome on the pitch, decisions like this one are likely to be scrutinised closely in a tournament where the political profile of the host country has been unusually high.
