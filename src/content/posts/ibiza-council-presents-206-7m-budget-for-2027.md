## Introduction to the Budget
The Consell d'Eivissa, the island council of Ibiza, has presented its budget for 2027, which totals €206,673,938.95. This represents a 14.93% increase from the previous year, with the aim of strengthening public services and addressing the growing demands of the island's population. According to the Consell d'Eivissa, the budget will focus on social welfare, public transport, culture, education, and environmental protection.

## Key Areas of Investment
The Department of Social Welfare, Family, and Equality will receive €45.46 million, with a focus on supporting people with disabilities, improving services at the Hospital Residència Assistida Cas Serres, and providing assistance to municipalities and social entities. Additionally, €12.9 million will be allocated for the care of minors, representing a 2.55 million increase from the previous year. The budget also includes provisions for expanding public transport, with a focus on maintaining the free transport service and improving the road network.

## Environmental Initiatives
The budget includes a plan to reduce waste, with an investment of €4.6 million in 2027, as part of a nine-year plan valued at €74 million. The initiative will be funded by the Sustainable Tourism Tax and will involve collaboration with municipalities. The Consell d'Eivissa also plans to recover and protect the island's natural landscape, with investments in the maintenance of the countryside and the removal of abandoned constructions.

## Financial Projections
The budget includes a forecast of €115.7 million in income from the Balearic Government, representing a 5.6 million increase from the previous year. The Consell d'Eivissa also plans to take out a loan of €28.2 million, which will be used to finance investments and can be repaid using remaining balances, specific agreements, or new sources of funding. As the president of the Consell d'Eivissa, Vicent Marí, said, "the most important thing, beyond the figures, is the ability to convert these resources into services, infrastructure, and solutions to improve the quality of life of residents." The conseller of Presidency, Finance, Economic Management, and Sports, Salvador Losa, added that "the numbers must support the policies we want to implement and guarantee that they can be carried out."
