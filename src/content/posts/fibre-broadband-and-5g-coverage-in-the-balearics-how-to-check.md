For remote workers, small businesses and families, a reliable internet connection can decide whether an address is workable. The Balearic Islands generally have good coverage in towns and resorts, but quality can differ in rural areas, on hillsides and in older buildings. This article sets out what the official statistics show and, more usefully, how to check a specific address before signing a lease or buying a home. It uses the Ministry for Digital Transformation's annual coverage report and the National Statistics Institute's survey of household technology. It does not reproduce operators' marketing claims. It was last checked on 29 October 2026.

## National coverage

The Ministry's 2024 coverage report, with data to 30 June 2024, was presented in June 2025. It found that:

- Fibre to the home (FTTH) reached 95 per cent of Spain.
- 5G reached 96 per cent of the population, with the faster 3.4 to 3.8 GHz band reaching 74 per cent.
- Fixed networks capable of gigabit speeds reached 94 per cent of homes.
- In rural areas, 5G reached 80 per cent of the population, compared with 26 per cent in 2021, and gigabit-capable fixed networks reached 84 per cent of rural homes.

A Ministry news item from May 2026 carries a headline saying that 5G now reaches 99 per cent of the Spanish population. The page could not be read in full for this article, so only the headline figure is given.

The report says that coverage figures can be broken down by region, province and municipality in downloadable data and interactive maps. The text of the press note that was consulted did not give separate figures for the Balearic Islands or other island groups, and this article does not guess them.

## How people in the islands use the internet

The National Statistics Institute (INE) survey on household technology for 2025 reports figures for people aged 16 to 74 in the Illes Balears:

- 97.3 per cent used the internet in the last three months.
- 94.1 per cent used it every day.
- 63.6 per cent bought something online in the last three months.

For Spain as a whole, the survey says 97.4 per cent of households have internet access through fixed or mobile broadband, 86.8 per cent have fixed broadband and 10.7 per cent have mobile-only internet. The release does not give household equipment figures for the Balearics.

## Why an address can still be a problem

Coverage statistics describe averages and the footprint of networks. A specific home may not get the service shown on a map. Common reasons include:

- **Buildings without fibre inside.** Some older blocks need the community of owners to approve the installation of fibre cabling.
- **Rural properties.** Remote houses may rely on mobile broadband or radio links.
- **Terrain.** Valleys and hillsides, such as parts of the Serra de Tramuntana, can reduce mobile signal.
- **Seasonal congestion.** Mobile networks in busy resorts can slow down in July and August.

## How to check an address

- **Use the Ministry's map.** The Ministry for Digital Transformation publishes interactive coverage maps and downloadable data by municipality.
- **Check the operators.** Each operator has a coverage tool on its website where you enter an address. Because these are company tools, treat them as indications and ask for written confirmation of the installation date.
- **Ask the landlord or seller.** Ask which operators serve the property, whether fibre is installed inside the flat and what speed they have recorded.
- **Test mobile signal in person.** Visit the property and test your own phone at different times of day.
- **Ask the community.** In a block of flats, the president of the community or the administrator knows whether fibre has been installed.

## Remote workers

The teleworker visa described in another article requires proof of a remote job, not a specific internet speed. Practical advice for remote workers is to confirm the connection before committing to a rental, to keep a mobile data plan as a back-up and to find a coworking space in a nearby town. The article on digital nomads explains the visa.

## Visitors and roaming

Visitors from the European Union can generally use their mobile plans under EU roaming rules. British visitors should check their own provider's terms, since roaming charges depend on the provider after Brexit. Wi-Fi is common in hotels, cafés and many public buildings.

## Consumer rights

Telecom customers in Spain have rights when a promised service is not delivered. Complaints can be made first to the operator, and if it is not resolved, to the Ministry's telecommunications users' office. The national regulator, the CNMC, publishes reports on the telecom markets. Keep copies of contracts, which should state speed and installation dates.

## What to watch

- **Fibre inside buildings** and rural gaps.
- **5G rollout** in rural areas and on the smaller islands.
- **Public investment** in broadband, which the Ministry and the regional government announce from time to time.

## Questions to ask before you rent or buy

A short list can save weeks of frustration after you move in:

- Is fibre installed inside the flat, or only in the building's entrance?
- Which operators can connect the address, and how long does installation usually take?
- Is there a router and a socket in the room where I will work?
- Is there a mobile signal indoors on the floor where I will live?
- If the property is in a community of owners, does the community allow new cabling?

## Business premises

Shops, bars and offices depend on connections for card payments, bookings and stock systems. Before signing a commercial lease, ask the landlord for written confirmation of the connection and ask the operator about business plans with a service guarantee. A back-up mobile connection is a sensible precaution for a card terminal.
