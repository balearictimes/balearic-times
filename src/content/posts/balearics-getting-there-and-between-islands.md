The four main Balearic Islands lie in the western Mediterranean, off the east coast of Spain. Mallorca, Menorca and Ibiza have airports, and Formentera can be reached by sea from Ibiza. Choosing the best route depends on where you start and how many islands you want to see.

## Flying from the UK

Mallorca and Ibiza are served by direct flights from many British airports, including London, Manchester, Birmingham, Glasgow and Edinburgh, as well as regional airports. Flights take around two and a half hours from the south of England. Menorca has fewer direct links, mostly seasonal. Several airlines operate routes, including low-cost carriers and tour operators.

Prices are highest in July and August. Midweek flights are often cheaper than weekend ones, and booking several months ahead helps. In winter, services are reduced and some routes stop.

## Flying from the rest of Europe

Palma is one of the busiest airports in Spain, with direct links to Germany, France, the Netherlands, Scandinavia and many other European countries. Ibiza has a smaller network but strong summer links with European cities. Menorca has a seasonal programme with some direct routes, and most winter traffic goes through Barcelona, Madrid or Palma.

## By ferry from the mainland

Ferries run from Barcelona, Valencia and Dénia to the islands. Barcelona and Valencia link with Palma, Ibiza and Menorca, and Dénia is a short crossing to Ibiza. Crossings take from a few hours on the shortest routes to overnight on the longest. They are a good way to bring a car or bike, and they avoid the weight limits on flights. Check the timetables and book early for summer.

## Between the islands

- **Mallorca and Menorca**: a fast ferry from Alcúdia to Ciutadella takes about one to two hours depending on the boat. Flights between Palma and Mahón are short, around 30 to 40 minutes.
- **Mallorca and Ibiza**: flights between Palma and Ibiza take about 40 minutes. Ferries also run, with longer crossings, and they carry cars.
- **Ibiza and Formentera**: ferries leave from Ibiza Town for La Savina in roughly 30 minutes, and several companies run the service.
- **Menorca and Ibiza**: direct links are limited. Most travellers go through Mallorca or Barcelona.

## Tips for island hopping

- Check the schedule for your return trip as well. Some routes run only a few times a week outside summer.
- Fast ferries can be cancelled in bad weather, so allow extra time before a flight.
- If you are taking a car, find out about vehicle rules on Ibiza and Formentera before booking.
- Travel insurance should cover missed connections.
- Hand luggage rules on low-cost airlines are strict, so check the size and weight allowance.

## Which route is best

For a first trip, flying directly to one island and staying put is simplest. For a longer visit, Mallorca and Menorca work well together by sea, and Ibiza and Formentera are easy partners. Mixing Mallorca with Ibiza is also possible but may involve more travel time than you expect, so decide whether the extra island is worth a day lost to a transfer.

## The four airports

Palma de Mallorca airport, Son Sant Joan, is the busiest in the islands and handles tens of millions of passengers a year. It has one main terminal with a separate area for low-cost carriers. Ibiza airport, on the south of the island, is smaller but very busy in summer, and it can be crowded in peak weeks. Menorca airport, near Mahón, is the smallest of the three main airports and receives seasonal flights from the UK. Formentera has no airport.

Aena, the Spanish airport operator, runs all three airports and publishes flight information, parking details and passenger advice at aena.es. In summer, queues at security and passport control can be long, so arrive at the recommended time.

## From the UK

Direct flights to Palma leave from airports across the UK, including London Gatwick, Heathrow, Stansted, Luton, Manchester, Birmingham, Bristol, Edinburgh and Glasgow. Ibiza has direct flights from many of the same airports in summer. Menorca has seasonal flights from several UK cities. Airlines include easyJet, Jet2, Ryanair, British Airways, TUI and Vueling. The flight from London takes roughly two hours and a half.

In winter, there are fewer flights to Ibiza and Menorca, and some routes stop. Mallorca keeps flights all year, mainly from London and a few regional airports.

## By ferry from the mainland

Baleària, Trasmed and other operators run ferries between the mainland and the islands. The ports of Barcelona and Valencia connect with Palma, Ibiza and Mahón, and Dénia connects with Ibiza and Palma. Crossings from Barcelona to Palma take around seven to eight hours on a conventional ferry, and shorter on fast boats. From Dénia to Ibiza, the fast ferry takes about two hours. Ferries carry cars, motorbikes and bicycles, and cabins are available on overnight services.

## Inter-island options

A summary of options between islands:

- **Palma to Ibiza**: a 40-minute flight, or a ferry that takes several hours.
- **Palma to Menorca**: a 35-minute flight, or the fast ferry from Alcúdia to Ciutadella.
- **Ibiza to Formentera**: the ferry from Ibiza Town to La Savina.
- **Mahón to Palma**: flights several times a day in summer.

Binter and Air Nostrum are among the airlines that operate inter-island routes, and flights are often cheaper if you book ahead. The ferry timetable changes between seasons, and the fast services may be cancelled in strong winds.

## Taxis and transfers at each airport

At Palma airport, taxis are plentiful, and the fare to the city centre is moderate. Many visitors pre-book transfers to resorts, which can be cheaper for families. At Ibiza airport, taxi queues can be long, and the airport bus is a useful alternative. At Menorca airport, taxis and buses serve Mahón and the resorts.

## Customs and baggage

Rules on duty-free goods, food and plants apply to visitors arriving from the UK. Low-cost airlines charge for hold luggage and large cabin bags, so check allowances before travelling. Liquids over 100 millilitres are restricted in hand baggage.

## Planning an island-hopping trip

If you want to see two islands, pick a pair that are easy to connect. Mallorca and Menorca are linked by a short ferry and flights. Ibiza and Formentera are linked by an easy ferry. If you want to see three islands, allow extra days for transfers.

## Official information

Aena publishes flight information at aena.es. Baleària, at balearia.com, and other operators publish ferry timetables. The UK government's foreign travel advice for Spain, at gov.uk, covers entry rules.
