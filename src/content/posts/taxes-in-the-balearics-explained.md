Taxes in the Balearic Islands come from three levels of government. The state sets the main taxes and collects them through the Agencia Tributaria. The Balearic Govern has its own powers over some taxes, which it collects through its tax agency, the Agència Tributària de les Illes Balears (ATIB). Town halls and island councils levy others. This guide explains the main taxes and rates that most people meet, using the published rates from the Agencia Tributaria. It is general information, not tax advice, and rates can change, so confirm figures with the tax authorities or a registered adviser. It was last checked on 29 October 2026.

## Income tax (IRPF)

Spanish residents pay personal income tax, the Impuesto sobre la Renta de las Personas Físicas, on worldwide income. The tax is progressive, so higher incomes pay higher rates. The state scale applied to the general taxable base has six brackets, according to the Agencia Tributaria:

| Taxable base (euros) | State rate |
| --- | --- |
| Up to 12,450 | 9.5 per cent |
| 12,450 to 20,200 | 12 per cent |
| 20,200 to 35,200 | 15 per cent |
| 35,200 to 60,000 | 18.5 per cent |
| 60,000 to 300,000 | 22.5 per cent |
| Above 300,000 | 24.5 per cent |

That is only the state part of the tax. Each autonomous community adds its own scale, so the total bill depends on where the taxpayer lives. The Balearic scale is set by the Govern and is published by ATIB, so the combined rate cannot be read from the table above alone.

Employees usually have tax withheld from their pay every month and file an annual return. Self-employed people pay in instalments each quarter.

Non-residents are taxed differently. The Agencia Tributaria publishes a separate scale for non-resident taxpayers. The first brackets match the table above up to 60,000 euros, and 22.5 per cent applies to everything above that amount. Whether a person is resident depends on days spent in Spain and where their economic interests lie, and the rules are set out in the Agencia Tributaria's guidance.

## VAT (IVA)

Value added tax is charged on most goods and services. The Agencia Tributaria lists a general rate of 21 per cent, reduced rates of 10 per cent and 4 per cent, and a zero rate for certain operations. The Agencia publishes a yearly list of which goods and services fall in which rate, so a business should check the 2026 list for its own products. Basic foods are generally at the reduced rates, and most other goods and services are at 21 per cent.

The Canary Islands, Ceuta and Melilla have their own indirect tax systems. The Balearic Islands are part of the standard Spanish VAT territory.

## Company tax

Companies pay corporate tax, the Impuesto sobre Sociedades. The general rate is 25 per cent in 2024, 2025 and 2026. Newly created companies can pay 15 per cent in the early years of activity, subject to conditions set in the law. Small companies with turnover below one million euros pay a reduced rate that, in 2026, is 19 per cent on the first 50,000 euros of taxable profit and 21 per cent on the remainder. The Agencia Tributaria says the conditions for the new-company rate are in Ley 28/2022 and in the transitional provisions of the corporate tax law, so a founder should check them before assuming the rate applies.

## Property taxes

Buying and owning property involves several taxes, and some are set at regional or local level:

- **Transfer tax or VAT on purchase.** Buying a resale home normally attracts the transfer tax, the Impuesto de Transmisiones Patrimoniales, whose rate is set by the autonomous community. Buying a new home attracts VAT and a stamp duty. ATIB publishes the Balearic rates.
- **Annual property tax.** The Impuesto sobre Bienes Inmuebles (IBI) is charged by the town hall each year, based on the cadastral value of the property.
- **Capital gains.** A gain from selling a property is taxed as income for residents. Non-residents pay a separate tax on gains.
- **Rental income.** Income from letting a home is taxable, for residents under IRPF and for non-residents under a separate regime. Holiday lets also need to be registered, as explained in another article.

## The tourist tax

The Impuesto sobre Estancias Turísticas en las Illes Balears, known as the sustainable tourism tax, is charged per person per night in tourist accommodation. It applies on all four islands. For 2026, the high season runs from 1 May to 31 October and the low season from 1 November to 30 April. As a guide, a 5-star hotel pays 4 euros per person per night in high season and 1 euro in low season, a 4-star hotel 3 euros and 0.75 euros, a 3-star or lower hotel and holiday rentals 2 euros and 0.50 euros. Children under 16 are exempt, and the rate is halved from the ninth consecutive night. The accommodation provider collects the tax and passes it to ATIB. Confirm the current figures on ATIB's website.

## Social security

Employees and employers pay social security contributions, which fund pensions, unemployment benefits and public healthcare. Self-employed workers pay a monthly contribution to the special scheme for self-employed workers, called RETA, which since 2023 is based on net income. The details are explained in the article on setting up as self-employed.

## Local and other taxes

Town halls also charge a vehicle tax, a tax on construction works and, in some cases, a tax on the increase in value of urban land when it is sold. Some municipalities charge rubbish collection fees. Rules and amounts differ from town to town, so check the municipal tax ordinance.

## Where to get help

- The Agencia Tributaria's website and phone service for state taxes.
- ATIB for regional taxes, through its website and electronic office.
- Town halls for local taxes.
- A registered adviser (gestor or asesor fiscal) for personal circumstances.
