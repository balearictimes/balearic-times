The Balearic Islands suit anyone who likes to exercise outdoors. The climate allows training for most of the year, the landscape ranges from mountain ridges to flat salt pans, and the coastline is long. This guide describes the main routes and activities island by island, and sets out the safety points that the authorities stress. It is a practical guide, not training advice, and anyone with a health condition should ask a doctor before starting a new programme. It was last checked on 29 October 2026.

## Mallorca: mountains, roads and the Tramuntana

The Serra de Tramuntana, on the north-west of Mallorca, was named a UNESCO World Heritage cultural landscape in 2011. It is the island's main walking area, with villages such as Valldemossa, Deià, Sóller and Pollença linked by old paths. The long-distance route known as the GR 221, or Ruta de Pedra en Sec, runs along the range and is divided into stages with refuges for overnight stays. The refuges and the path are managed by the Consell de Mallorca, which publishes information on stages, bookings and closures.

Cyclists are drawn to the same hills. The roads of the Tramuntana, the Formentor peninsula and the flat plain of the centre offer routes for all levels. Spring and autumn are the busiest seasons for road cycling. Note that restrictions apply on the road to the Formentor lighthouse in 2026, where private vehicles are limited at certain hours between mid-May and mid-October. Check the Consell's information before you go.

For running, Palma has the seafront promenade and the paths around the bay, and Bellver Castle's park has forest trails. Inland, towns such as Inca and Manacor have local parks and tracks.

## Menorca: the Camí de Cavalls

Menorca's coastal path, the Camí de Cavalls, circles the whole island, about 185 kilometres. It follows an old military and horse route and is divided into stages. It passes beaches, coves, cliffs and farmland, and it is open to walkers, riders and cyclists on most sections. The island council manages the path and publishes maps and closures. Because the path is exposed, carry water and sun protection, and avoid the middle of the day in summer.

Menorca is also a UNESCO Biosphere Reserve, and the council has rules to protect the countryside. Stay on the path, close gates and keep dogs under control.

## Ibiza: quieter corners

Ibiza's reputation is for nightlife, but the island has good walking and cycling in the north and west. The hills around Sant Joan de Labritja and the cliffs near Sant Antoni offer paths with sea views. The Ses Salines natural park in the south is flat and suitable for cycling and bird watching. Road cycling is possible, but traffic is heavy in summer. Check the island council's website for the current road rules.

Swimmers use the calm bays in the north, such as the beaches of Portinatx and Cala Xarraca, in summer. Always swim near lifeguards where possible.

## Formentera: flat and easy

Formentera is flat, and cycling is the standard way to get around. The island has cycle paths and quiet roads between Es Pujols, Sant Francesc, Es Pilar de la Mola and the beaches of Migjorn and Ses Illetes. The slopes at La Mola are the hardest climb. The sea is clear and often calm, so swimming and paddle sports are popular. The Consell de Formentera publishes rules on vehicles in summer.

## Open-water swimming

Swimming in the sea is a major form of exercise. Authorities advise:

- Swim at beaches with lifeguards, and follow the flag system. Green means safe, yellow means take care and red means do not swim.
- Do not swim alone, and stay close to the shore.
- Use a bright cap or a tow float so boats can see you.
- Keep away from boat channels and moorings.
- Be aware of jellyfish warnings.
- Never swim after drinking alcohol.

Boat traffic is heavy in summer, and swimmers have been injured by boats. Stay inside marked swimming areas where they exist.

## Heat and timing

Summer midday is too hot for hard training. Run, ride or walk early in the morning or in the evening, and carry water. The Spanish weather agency AEMET issues heat warnings, and the article on staying healthy in the sun explains how they work. Spring and autumn are the best seasons for long outdoor sessions.

## Fire and nature rules

In summer, fire risk is high, and the authorities restrict fires and some activities in natural areas. Do not light fires, and dispose of cigarettes carefully. Respect protected areas, stay on marked paths and take your rubbish with you.

## Safety basics

- Tell someone your route and expected return time.
- Carry a charged phone and the emergency number 112. Coverage can be poor in the mountains.
- Wear a helmet when cycling, which is mandatory in many cases on public roads outside towns.
- Check the weather before setting out.
- Start easy if you are not used to the heat.

## Joining a group

Most towns have walking, running and cycling clubs, and the town hall's sports department can point you to them. The Consell in each island publishes lists of sports facilities, and many councils run sports centres with public pools. Foreign residents often find clubs through community groups.

## Questions people ask

**When is the best time to hike?** Spring and autumn offer the mildest weather. In summer, start at dawn.

**Do I need a permit for the long-distance paths?** Booking is needed for refuges on the GR 221, and some closures apply. Check with the Consell de Mallorca. The Camí de Cavalls on Menorca is a public route, with local closures published by the island council.

**Can I hire a bike easily?** Bike hire is widely available in the main tourist areas, and road bikes can be rented in Mallorca. Ask for a helmet and check the brakes.

**Is it safe to swim alone?** Authorities advise against it. Swim near lifeguards and with a companion.
