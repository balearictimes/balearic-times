After years of rapid growth, house prices in the centres of Spain's three largest cities are showing signs of fatigue, while the outer districts are rising faster as buyers look further afield.

## The figures

Data on asking prices on the main property portals, compiled by the analytics firm Atlas Real Estate Analytics, shows that between January 2025 and April 2026 outer districts outpaced central areas in all three cities.

| City | Centre | Rest of city |
|---|---|---|
| Madrid | +11.1% to €9,107/m² (inside the M-30) | +17.0% to €4,452/m² |
| Barcelona | +10.4% to €6,355/m² (five central districts) | +11.7% to €3,773/m² |
| Valencia | +13.3% to €4,500/m² | +18.2% to €3,275/m² |

The centres remain far more expensive — in Madrid the difference is more than €4,600 per square metre — but the gap is narrowing.

## A market in equilibrium

Alejandro Bermúdez, chief executive of Atlas Real Estate, says central districts have already been through their upward cycle. Years of strong demand have pushed prices so high that fewer buyers can afford them, and the market has reached a point where supply is not falling but prices are no longer climbing. This is most visible in the most gentrified streets, where homes stay on the market longer.

## Room to grow — up to a point

Outer neighbourhoods began rising later, from a lower base, so they still have room to grow, and they attract buyers priced out of the centre.

But Bermúdez warns that this cannot go on indefinitely. Each area has a ceiling set by local incomes and by the quality of its homes, streets and services. Asked about one of Madrid's working-class districts, he said that even if there were nothing on the market, nobody would pay €700,000 for a two-bedroom flat in Carabanchel.

## The Balearic angle

The same pattern is familiar in Mallorca, where years of price rises in Palma's most sought-after neighbourhoods have pushed buyers towards outlying districts and towns such as Marratxí, Llucmajor and Inca. As in the big cities, the limit is set by what local wages can pay.
