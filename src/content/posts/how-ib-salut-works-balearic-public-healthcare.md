Spain has a national health system run by the autonomous communities. In the Balearic Islands, the public service is called IB-Salut, or the Servei de Salut de les Illes Balears. It runs health centres, hospitals and emergency services, and it is the main provider of healthcare for residents. This guide explains how it is organised and how people get access, using IB-Salut's own pages and the Govern's description of its health department. It is general information and not medical advice. It was last checked on 29 October 2026.

## Who is in charge

Health policy in the islands belongs to the regional government. According to the Govern's website, the Conseller for Health is Manuela García Romero. The department sets policy and budget, and IB-Salut delivers the services. The national Ministry of Health sets common rules and standards, and the regions run the service within them.

## Who is covered

IB-Salut's access page says that it provides public healthcare to residents of the Balearic Islands who are recognised as insured persons or beneficiaries by the Instituto Nacional de la Seguridad Social (INSS). In practice, workers and their families who contribute to Social Security are covered, as are pensioners and others who qualify under national rules.

To use services at the health centre in your area, you request an individual health card, the Tarjeta Sanitaria Individual (TSI). The card identifies you as a resident of an autonomous community and lets you access services across Spain. You normally need to be registered in the municipal register, the padrón, and show your identity document and your Social Security recognition. The IB-Salut website explains the procedure and the places where you can apply.

## People who are not covered

Residents who are not insured under Social Security and have no right to cover by another route can sign a special agreement with IB-Salut, the convenio especial de asistencia sanitaria. It gives access to the basic common portfolio of services in return for a monthly fee. IB-Salut lists the fee as 60 euros a month for people under 65 and 157 euros a month for people aged 65 and over. The requirements are:

- Living in the Balearic Islands, with no right to the national health system by another route.
- Being registered in a Balearic municipality when applying. Each family member applies separately.
- Proof of effective residence in Spain, the EU, the EEA, Switzerland or the UK for at least one continuous year immediately before applying.
- Paying the monthly fee.

The agreement excludes pharmaceutical, orthoprosthetic and outpatient dietary products and non-urgent medical transport. IB-Salut says it takes a decision within one month. The application can be made online through the Govern's electronic office with a digital certificate, or in person at a registry. The document you receive is not an individual health card.

## The first point of contact: primary care

Most care begins at a health centre (centre de salut). Each resident is assigned a family doctor and a nurse. You make an appointment by phone, through the online system or at the centre. The family doctor treats common problems, orders tests, prescribes medicines and refers you to a specialist if needed. Health centres also provide vaccinations, child health checks and care for chronic conditions.

## Hospitals

The public hospitals named by IB-Salut and the Govern include:

- **Mallorca:** Hospital Universitari Son Espases in Palma, the main referral hospital. Others include Hospital Son Llàtzer in Palma, and the hospitals of Manacor and Inca.
- **Menorca:** Hospital Mateu Orfila in Maó.
- **Ibiza:** Hospital Can Misses in Ibiza Town.
- **Formentera:** a health centre in Sant Francesc, with serious cases transferred to Ibiza.

Patients are referred to hospital specialists by their family doctor. Waiting times for non-urgent care are published by the health authorities.

## Emergencies

The European emergency number 112 works throughout the islands and has English-speaking operators. In an emergency you can also go straight to the emergency department of a hospital, called urgencias. Ambulance services are part of the public system. Health centres have out-of-hours emergency services for urgent problems.

## Travelling for treatment

Patients who must travel between islands or to the mainland for treatment can claim compensation for travel expenses under a scheme that IB-Salut describes on its website. The rules set out who is eligible and how to apply. This is relevant on Menorca, Ibiza and Formentera, where some specialist care is only available in Palma.

## Medicines

Prescriptions are issued electronically, and pharmacies dispense with the health card. In Spain, patients normally pay a share of the price of medicines, which depends on their income and status. Pensioners and certain groups pay less. Pharmacies take turns to stay open at night and on weekends.

## Languages

Staff work in Catalan and Spanish, and many speak English in tourist areas. You have the right to use either official language, as the Statute of Autonomy says. The article on languages explains this. Interpreters are not always available, so bring a friend or use a translation service for complex appointments.

## Practical tips

- Register in the padrón of your town hall first.
- Apply for your health card as soon as you qualify.
- Keep your contact details up to date so the centre can reach you.
- Use the health centre for non-urgent problems and keep emergency services for real emergencies.
- If you are visiting, see the separate article on seeing a doctor as a visitor.

## Questions people ask

**How do I choose my doctor?** You are assigned one at your local health centre. Ask the centre about changing doctors if you need to.

**Do I need an appointment?** For most visits, yes. Emergencies and urgent problems are handled without one at urgencias or the out-of-hours service.

**Is the health card valid on every island?** The card identifies residents of the autonomous community and gives access to services across Spain.

**What if I move to another region?** You need to register with the health service of your new community. Ask your health centre for the procedure.
