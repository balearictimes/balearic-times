LaLiga has postponed Rayo Vallecano's home match against Real Oviedo because of the poor condition of the pitch at the Estadio de Vallecas, citing the need to protect the physical integrity of the players.

## The decision

The decision followed an inspection that concluded the playing surface did not offer the necessary safety guarantees. LaLiga acknowledged the efforts made by Rayo to fix the problem, but said the conditions to play could not be guaranteed. The new date for the match will be set by the competition.

## The background

The Vallecas pitch has been a source of complaints for some time. Heavy rain in Madrid in recent weeks worsened a surface already in poor shape, with drainage problems that left parts of the field waterlogged and unstable. Rayo's coach, Íñigo Pérez, had already criticised the state of the grass publicly.

In the days before the match, the club carried out urgent work, including laying new turf. But freshly laid grass needs time to root properly, and the inspection concluded that it was not yet safe for professional football.

## Why it matters

A poor pitch is not only a question of the quality of play. Uneven or loose ground increases the risk of injuries to the knees and ankles, and players and coaches have become increasingly vocal about the condition of surfaces in Spanish football.

Postponements are rare in LaLiga and complicate the calendar for both clubs, which must find a free date in an already busy season.

## An old stadium

The episode adds to a long-running debate about the Estadio de Vallecas, one of the oldest grounds in the top flight. The stadium, owned by the Community of Madrid, has suffered from structural and maintenance problems for years, and its future — including proposals to refurbish it or to move the club to a new site — has been the subject of tension between the club's owner, the regional government and Rayo's fans, who are deeply attached to their neighbourhood ground.

For now, the priority is to get the pitch into a playable state as quickly as possible, so that Rayo can return to home matches without further disruption.
