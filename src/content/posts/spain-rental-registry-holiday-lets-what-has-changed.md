Anyone who has booked a holiday flat in Spain recently may have noticed a long registration number on the listing. It comes from a national system, the Registro Único de Arrendamientos, that applies in the Balearic Islands as everywhere else in Spain. It sits on top of the islands' own tourism licensing rules, which are covered in a separate article. This piece sets out what the national rules say, based on the official texts and the Ministry of Housing's own announcements. It was last checked on 29 October 2026, and it describes the law rather than advising on any individual case.

## What the registry is

The registry was created by Real Decreto 1312/2024, published in the Boletín Oficial del Estado at the end of 2024. The Ministry of Housing and Urban Agenda, now called the Mivau in its web address, runs a single online counter, the Ventanilla Única Digital de Arrendamientos, and the property registrars issue a registration number for each rental unit. The decree entered into force on 2 January 2025, and its provisions began to apply from 1 July 2025, to give owners, platforms and administrations time to adapt.

The aim, in the Ministry's words, is to give the authorities one place to see which properties are being let on a short-term basis, to make it easier to enforce existing rules and to cut down on illegal listings.

## Which rentals it covers

The Ministry's description says the system covers:

- Tourist rentals, the apartamentos de uso turístico.
- Seasonal rentals, the alquileres de temporada.
- Room rentals.
- Boats and other properties that allow short stays, except where the accommodation is tied to a service that makes navigation possible.

It applies where the rental is offered through online platforms and has a main purpose other than permanent housing. A unit can hold only one number for each category and type of rental. Long-term rentals of a home as the tenant's main residence are not the target.

## What owners must do

Owners have to apply for a registration number, supply the information the registry requires, answer any requests for further information and update the data when it changes. They must also pass the number to the platforms they use. The number has to appear in every advertisement for the unit.

The information models for the applications were set by an order of the Housing Ministry in 2025, Orden VAU/1560/2025.

## What platforms must do

The decree puts obligations on the websites that carry the listings. According to the Ministry's summary, platforms must:

- Let owners enter the registration number and show it in the listing.
- Run random and periodic checks that the numbers are valid, and report negative results to the Ventanilla.
- Tell owners how to apply for a number.
- Send monthly data on each unit to the Ventanilla, including the number, the address and the listing links.
- Remove or disable listings linked to suspended or withdrawn numbers within 48 hours of an administrative order.

## What happened in the first months

The Ministry published figures after the first 90 days of the application system. It reported 18,104 applications, of which 8,787 had been granted a number, 7,393 were still under evaluation, and 1,924 had been revoked. In June 2025, the property registrars' professional body said the registries had generated more than 100,000 registration numbers across Spain, ahead of the date when the number became compulsory for advertising on 1 July 2025. Neither source gave a breakdown by region, so there is no official regional figure for the Balearics in those texts.

## Penalties

The decree does not create a new sanctions regime. The Ministry's summary says that until a specific regime is approved, existing national, regional and local penalties continue to apply. In the Balearics, that means the penalties in the regional tourism rules, which were raised in 2025 and which are explained in another article in this series.

## The apartment block rule

A separate change, passed in 2025, affects holiday lets in apartment buildings. A reform of the horizontal property law, Ley Orgánica 1/2025, came into force on 3 April 2025. Under it, an owner who wants to start using a flat in a block for tourist lets needs the approval of three fifths of the owners, counted both by number and by ownership shares. Owners who already ran a tourist flat before the change are not covered in the same way, and the details depend on each building's statutes and the date when the activity began.

The Ministry of Housing's page says nothing on existing flats, so owners should check the text of the law and take professional advice.

## What this means for holiday visitors

For guests, the registration number is meant to show that a listing is registered, and platforms must remove listings whose numbers are suspended or withdrawn within 48 hours of an administrative order. If a listing in the Balearics does not show a number, or the number does not match the property, that is a reason to ask the host or the platform questions before paying. A booking at a licensed hotel avoids the question.

## What this means for owners

The registry adds a layer of paperwork to the regional licence. In the Balearics, an owner of a holiday let needs the regional tourist registration, the national registry number, and if the property is in a community of owners, compliance with the building's rules. Seasonal lets and room rentals offered through online platforms are covered too. Anyone in doubt should read the decree or ask a lawyer or a gestor.

## What comes next

The Ministry's summary says a specific sanctions regime has still to be approved. The general election on 29 November 2026 may affect the legislative agenda, since the new Cortes will decide on any further housing laws. Regional rules, which the Govern can change by decree-law, continue to apply regardless of the national timetable.
