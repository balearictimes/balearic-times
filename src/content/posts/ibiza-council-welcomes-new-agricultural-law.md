## Introduction to the New Law
The Consell d'Eivissa, Ibiza's island council, has given a positive assessment of the new agricultural law proposed by the Govern de les Illes Balears, the regional government of the Balearic Islands. According to the council, this initiative is important for consolidating the positive evolution of Ibiza's primary sector and facilitating the work of farmers and livestock breeders.

## Key Developments in Ibiza's Agriculture
Vice-president Mariano Juan highlighted that the number of agricultural exploitations in Ibiza has increased by 16% from 1,527 to 1,773 between 2019 and 2025, while livestock exploitations have risen by 32% from 734 to 968. Additionally, the area dedicated to ecological agriculture has doubled in five years, from 792 hectares in 2020 to 1,590 hectares in 2025. Juan stated that "the problems of the countryside continue to exist, but these data show that there are farmers and livestock breeders who continue to bet on our land."

## Objectives of the New Law
The new law aims to reduce bureaucracy and provide new opportunities for the sector, including facilities to transform and market local products, recover abandoned land, and develop complementary activities to improve the profitability of exploitations. Juan also valued the new possibilities for agricultural service companies, such as those dedicated to machinery, maintenance, pruning, or harvesting, through the use of old agricultural constructions.

## Future Challenges and Opportunities
The vice-president emphasized the need to guarantee the economic viability of exploitations to favor generational change, with 102 applications for young farmers processed between 2020 and 2025. Juan recognized that the sector continues to face difficulties due to insularity, fragmentation of exploitations, and competition for land and labor, and defended the need for a regulation that facilitates agricultural activity. He concluded that "the best way to protect our agricultural landscape is to ensure that there continue to be farmers and livestock breeders who work it. This law can contribute to making the countryside an economic activity with a future."
