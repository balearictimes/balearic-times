The Balearic economy is still growing, but recent data show that the pace has slowed compared with the strong rebound of the post-pandemic years. Growth is returning to a more normal rate — even though hotels, airports and restaurants are as busy as ever.

## Activity is not the same as profit

Busy beaches and full planes are a sign of strong demand, but economic growth depends on more than the number of customers. It also depends on how much value each one generates. If businesses' margins are squeezed, more visitors do not automatically mean more growth.

## The cost squeeze

Hotels, restaurants and other businesses face rising costs:

- **wages**, after recent increases agreed in collective agreements;
- **energy and supplies**;
- **financing**, after the rise in interest rates;
- **housing for staff**, which some companies now provide to attract workers.

When costs grow faster than income, the net benefit shrinks, even with full occupancy.

## Limits to growth

There are also physical limits. On islands where hotel capacity is restricted, and with social pressure to contain tourist numbers, growth can no longer come from adding more visitors. It has to come from higher spending per visitor and from productivity — which is harder to achieve.

## A normal slowdown

Economists describe the situation as normalisation rather than crisis. After an exceptional recovery, the economy is settling into a more sustainable rhythm.

## What it means

For residents, steady growth can bring stability. But the slowdown also highlights a long-standing challenge: the Balearic economy remains heavily dependent on tourism, and productivity and wages have struggled to keep up with the cost of living, especially housing.

## What to watch

Policymakers and businesses will be looking at wages, investment and consumer confidence to see whether the slowdown is temporary or a longer trend. Diversifying the economy — in technology, the blue economy, renewable energy and other sectors — is often cited as the way to make growth less dependent on the number of tourists and more on the value created on the islands.
