Letting a home to holiday visitors can look simple: take photos, post a listing, hand over the keys. In the Balearic Islands, it is a regulated activity with several layers of rules, and penalties have risen. This guide pulls the official rules together into a checklist for owners. It relies on the Balearic Govern's announcement of its 2025 decree-law, the national decree creating the rental registry, the housing ministry's guidance and the government's information on property community law. It is general information, not legal advice, and it was last checked on 29 October 2026.

## Step 1: check whether you can let at all

The Govern's Decreto ley 4/2025, of 11 April, bans the creation of new tourist places in multi-family buildings in all the islands. Places can only be created by exchange of existing ones, one for one, under a global ceiling, and the transitional bank of places that handles this does not create new places. If you own a flat in an apartment block and do not already have a tourist licence, the decree closes that route for new places. Houses and villas are not covered by the same ban, but local rules, island ceilings and planning restrictions may apply, so check with your town hall and island council.

## Step 2: check your community

The reform of the horizontal property law, Ley Orgánica 1/2025, in force since 3 April 2025, says that starting a tourist let in a flat in a block needs the approval of three fifths of the owners, by number and by ownership shares. Read the building's statutes and the minutes of the owners' meetings. Neighbours can complain about noise and behaviour, and the community can take action.

## Step 3: hold the regional licence

Tourist accommodation must be registered with the Balearic tourism authorities and carry the registration number required by the regional rules. The Govern publishes the register and the conditions. Operating without it is an offence. The 2025 decree-law raised fines by 25 per cent. Minor offences can lead to fines of up to 5,000 euros, serious offences up to 50,000 euros and very serious offences up to 500,000 euros.

## Step 4: obtain the national registry number

Real Decreto 1312/2024 created the Registro Único de Arrendamientos. It entered into force on 2 January 2025, and its provisions apply from 1 July 2025. Owners must obtain a registration number for each unit, give it to the platforms they use, keep the data up to date and display the number in every advertisement. The system covers tourist rentals, seasonal rentals, room rentals and some boats. A unit can hold only one number per category and type of rental.

Platforms must let owners enter the number, check numbers at random, send monthly activity data to the registry and remove listings linked to suspended or withdrawn numbers within 48 hours of an administrative order. The Ministry says no new sanction regime has been created yet, so existing national, regional and local penalties continue to apply.

## Step 5: register for tax

Rental income is taxable. Residents report it in their income tax return. Non-residents are taxed under a separate regime. Some owners also have to charge VAT, depending on the services provided. A holiday let registered as a business may need to register with the tax authority and file quarterly returns. The Agencia Tributaria publishes the rules, and a gestor can advise on your case.

## Step 6: collect the tourist tax

The sustainable tourism tax is charged per person per night, and the host or manager collects it from guests and passes it to the Balearic tax agency, ATIB. Holiday rentals pay 2 euros per person per night in high season, which runs from 1 May to 31 October, and 0.50 euros in low season, with children under 16 exempt and a halved rate from the ninth consecutive night. Check the current rates on the ATIB website.

## Step 7: meet safety and quality standards

Holiday lets must meet the safety and habitability requirements in the regional rules, including fire safety, electrical and gas installations, hygiene and information for guests. Keep certificates, and provide guests with the required information, including emergency numbers and house rules.

## Step 8: register guests

Spanish law requires accommodation providers to record the identity of guests and send the data to the police. The Ministry of the Interior sets the procedure. Check the current requirements and use the approved electronic system.

## Step 9: insure the property

Take out insurance that covers short-term letting, liability to guests and damage. A policy for a private home may not cover commercial use.

## Penalties and enforcement

Owners who let without the correct licences face fines, and platforms share responsibility. The Govern has said that a sanctioned owner can reduce a fine by up to 80 per cent if he or she transfers the property for social or price-limited rental. The island councils and town halls also have inspection and sanction powers. Guests who are told to leave an unlicensed let may seek a refund from the platform, and owners can face claims.

## Common mistakes

- Advertising before getting the registry number.
- Letting a flat in a block without the community's approval.
- Assuming a licence transfers automatically on sale.
- Failing to collect the tourist tax.
- Not declaring the income.

## Questions owners ask

**If I already have a regional licence, do I still need the national number?** Yes. The national registry covers tourist, seasonal and room rentals offered through platforms, and the number must appear in every advertisement.

**Can a platform delete my listing?** It must remove listings linked to suspended or withdrawn numbers within 48 hours of an administrative order.

**What if my neighbours object?** Check the building's statutes and the owners' minutes. The reform of the horizontal property law gives owners a say on new tourist lets in flats.

**Do I have to charge the tourist tax in a villa?** Registered holiday rentals pay the tax, collected by the owner or manager. Check the rates on the ATIB site.
