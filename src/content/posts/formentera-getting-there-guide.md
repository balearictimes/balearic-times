Formentera has no airport, so everybody arrives by sea. The usual route is by boat from Ibiza, which makes the island easy to visit as a day trip or as a longer stay.

## The ferry from Ibiza

Boats leave from Ibiza Town's port for La Savina, Formentera's main harbour. The fast ferries take about half an hour, and the crossing is frequent through the summer, with fewer services in winter. Several companies operate the route, and prices differ by operator and time of day. Booking ahead helps on busy mornings in July and August, and you should check the return times so that you do not miss the last boat.

There are also seasonal links from other points on the Spanish coast to Ibiza and Formentera. If you are flying into Ibiza, the airport bus or a taxi takes you to the port.

## Cars and vehicle limits

Formentera has limited the number of vehicles that can enter in the summer, with the aim of reducing traffic and protecting the environment. The limits apply to cars and motorbikes, including those brought on the ferry, and the rules for rental vehicles are set by the island council. The quotas and dates are reviewed regularly, so check the current guidance before you travel. Most visitors choose to hire bikes or scooters on the island itself.

## Bikes

Formentera is largely flat, which makes it a good island for cycling. A network of paths and quiet roads covers most of it, and bike hire shops cluster around La Savina and the main resorts. The heat can be intense, so carry water, wear a hat and avoid midday rides in August. The only real hill is the climb to La Mola in the east.

## Scooters and quad bikes

Scooters are a common choice for visitors who want to cover longer distances, but they bring risk. Wear a helmet, ride slowly and keep to the rules. Insurance conditions on rented scooters can be strict, so read them.

## Buses

A bus network connects La Savina, Sant Francesc, Es Pujols, Es Pilar de la Mola and other places. Services are frequent in summer and cut back in winter. Buses reach many beaches, though not every one, and timetables are posted at stops and online.

## Taxis

Taxis operate from the port and the main villages, and they can be expensive for longer trips. Book ahead in busy periods.

## Staying overnight

The island has small hotels, apartments and campsites rather than large resorts. Es Pujols is the busiest area for restaurants and nightlife, while Es Pilar de la Mola and Cala Saona are calmer. Accommodation fills in summer, and prices rise, so book early.

## A day trip plan

- Take an early ferry from Ibiza.
- Hire bikes at La Savina and ride to Ses Illetes.
- Stop for lunch near Migjorn beach.
- Return in the late afternoon, leaving enough time for the last boat.

## Reaching Ibiza first

Because Formentera has no airport, nearly every visitor passes through Ibiza. Ibiza airport is about 7 kilometres from Ibiza Town, and the ferry terminal is in the harbour of Eivissa, below the old walled town of Dalt Vila. The journey from the airport to the port takes about 15 minutes by taxi, and there is a city bus. Plan to arrive at least an hour before departure, since ticket control and queues can be slow in summer.

## Ferry companies and boats

Several companies run the Ibiza to Formentera route, including Baleària, Mediterránea Pitiusa and Trasmed. Smaller boats also sail from Santa Eulària and from Ibiza Town to Ses Illetes during the season. Fast ferries carry foot passengers, and larger ferries take vehicles. Tickets are sold online and at the port, and prices vary by operator and date. The journey from Ibiza Town to La Savina takes between 30 and 40 minutes.

The ferry sails across the Estrecho de Es Freus, the channel between the two islands. On windy days, the crossing can be rough, and sailings may be cancelled. Keep an eye on the weather and the company's updates.

## Arriving at La Savina

The port of La Savina has the ferry terminal, a bus station, a taxi rank and bike hire shops. The village of Sant Francesc Xavier, the capital, is about three kilometres inland. The beaches at Ses Illetes and Llevant are about six kilometres to the north. Es Pujols, the main tourist resort, is a few kilometres to the north.

## Island transport

- **Buses**: lines connect La Savina to Es Pujols, Sant Francesc, Es Pilar de la Mola, Cala Saona and Migjorn.
- **Bikes**: the island has dozens of kilometres of cycle paths and quiet lanes, and bike hire is easy to find.
- **Scooters**: popular but risky, and some insurance policies exclude them.
- **Taxis**: available at La Savina and Sant Francesc.
- **Boats**: tours from La Savina to S'Espalmador and nearby coves.

## Vehicle rules

The Consell de Formentera applies a limit on the number of vehicles that can enter in the summer. Cars from Ibiza need a permit, and residents have priority. Rental companies on the island manage their own quotas. The system runs from late spring to early autumn, and the dates change each year. Check the Consell website before booking.

## Where to stay

The main areas are Es Pujols, a resort with bars and restaurants, Es Pilar de la Mola, a quiet village on the plateau, Sant Ferran de ses Roques, a crossroads village with a few lively bars, and Cala Saona, with hotels over the bay. Small hostals and apartments are common. Camping is not allowed outside official sites.

## Day trips from Ibiza

Many visitors come for the day. The easiest plan is to take the first fast ferry from Ibiza Town, hire bikes at La Savina, ride to Ses Illetes, have lunch and return in the late afternoon. For a longer day, visit La Mola by bus or scooter, or take a boat to S'Espalmador.

## Costs

Day trips cost the price of a return ferry ticket, plus bike hire and lunch. Accommodation is expensive in peak season, and restaurants charge more than on the mainland. Shops in Sant Francesc and La Savina sell groceries at lower prices than beach restaurants.

## Official information

The ferry operators publish timetables and booking pages, and the Consell de Formentera provides updates on vehicle rules and transport.
