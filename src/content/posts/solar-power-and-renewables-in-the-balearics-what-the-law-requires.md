Energy is a particular challenge on islands. The Balearics have no fossil fuels of their own, limited land and a population that rises sharply each summer. For that reason, the regional parliament passed a climate change and energy transition law in 2019 that sets targets and obligations for solar power, buildings, vehicles and businesses. This article summarises the law as the Govern's climate change pages describe it, and then reports what the Govern has said about its projects. Some dates in the law have passed, and the law may have been amended since, so check the consolidated text. It was last checked on 29 October 2026.

## The law

Ley 10/2019, de 22 de febrero, de cambio climático y transición energética, aims to apply the Paris Agreement in the Balearic Islands and to move toward a renewable, low-carbon energy system. The Govern's description lists these energy targets:

- **Renewables:** binding shares of 35 per cent by 2030 and 100 per cent by 2050 for the Transition Plan, as the page describes.
- **Energy saving:** a reduction of primary energy consumption against a 2005 baseline of 26 per cent by 2030 and 40 per cent by 2050.
- **Emissions:** a cut in greenhouse gas emissions against a 1990 baseline of 40 per cent by 2030 and 90 per cent by 2050. The page says these targets are binding for diffuse emissions and indicative for others.

## Solar panels on buildings and car parks

The law imposes direct obligations on property owners. According to the Govern's summary:

- New car parks over 1,000 square metres, and existing ones over 1,500 square metres, must install solar panels.
- New buildings over 1,000 square metres, and those that change use or undergo a full renovation, must install solar panels, except where the roof is made of fibre-cement.
- Isolated buildings may only be supplied with renewable energy.
- All public lighting was to be low consumption by 2025.

The Balearic Energy Institute (IBE), the public body created by the law, identified 794 car parks that could be covered with solar panels, according to a Govern notice, and it launched a project to determine the photovoltaic potential of every roof in the islands. The Govern also announced a solar canopy of 3,200 kilowatts at the ParcBIT technology park, which it described as one of the largest self-consumption projects in the islands.

## Self-consumption and records

Two further Govern notices bear on progress. One reports that one in four kilowatts of renewable power installed in the islands is for self-consumption, meaning panels that serve the property where they are installed. Another reports that the islands set a record for renewable production in May. The pages consulted for this article show the headlines but not the numerical details, so readers who want the figures should read the notices on caib.es and the data from the national grid operator, Red Eléctrica.

## Vehicles and mobility

The law sets targets for transport, according to the Govern's pages:

- 1,000 public electric vehicle charging points by 2025.
- Full decarbonisation of the vehicle fleet by 2050.
- Restrictions on new diesel cars and motorbikes from 2025, and on other polluting vehicles from 2035, with existing vehicles exempt.
- Car rental companies must progressively add electric or zero-emission vehicles, starting at 2 per cent in 2020 and reaching 100 per cent by 2035.
- Municipalities with air quality problems may restrict traffic.

These measures matter to visitors, because car hire is a major business on the islands. Readers should check the current rules and whether any of the dates have been amended.

## Power stations

The law also dealt with the older power infrastructure. The Govern's description mentions the closure of coal units at the Murterar plant in Mallorca, the increased use of the Son Reus gas combined-cycle plant while renewables expand, a shift to natural gas on Menorca and Ibiza and the closure of an old turbine in Formentera. Some of those dates fell in 2020 and 2025, and the pages consulted do not say whether each step happened as planned.

## Businesses

Large and medium-sized companies had to calculate and register their carbon footprint by 2020, and by 2025 they were to present and carry out reduction plans with binding targets. The law created the Balearic Climate Council, an advisory body, and a committee of experts that advises the government.

## What this means for you

- **Homeowners.** New buildings and major renovations above the size limits must include solar panels. Owners of smaller homes can install panels for self-consumption, and the Govern and the island councils announce grants from time to time.
- **Business owners.** Car park operators, hotel owners and developers should check the thresholds and the deadlines.
- **Drivers and visitors.** More charging points are planned, and rental fleets are meant to become cleaner.
- **Renters.** Ask about energy efficiency, since the law pushes the market toward efficient buildings.

## Limits and debate

The law has been debated, because large solar parks and panels on farmland raise questions about landscape and farming. The Govern's pages consulted for this article do not discuss the arguments. The decisions on specific projects are made by the Govern, island councils and town halls under planning rules.

## Questions people ask

**Do the car park rules apply to my small car park?** The thresholds quoted are 1,000 square metres for new car parks and 1,500 for existing ones. Smaller ones are outside those rules, though voluntary installations are possible.

**Who enforces the law?** The Govern and the Institut Balear de l'Energia, with town halls handling licences. Check the consolidated text for the latest procedures.

**Can I put panels on my own roof?** Self-consumption is part of the policy, and the Govern has reported that a quarter of installed renewable power is for self-consumption. Ask your town hall about licensing and the community of owners if you live in a block.
