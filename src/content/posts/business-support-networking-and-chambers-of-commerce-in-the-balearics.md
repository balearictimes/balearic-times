Starting or running a business in the Balearic Islands means dealing with many offices. The good news is that several public bodies exist to help, and most of their services are free or low cost. This guide describes the main official channels, what each one does and how to use them to learn the rules and meet other business people. It does not publish a calendar of private trade fairs, because dates and organisers change and the official sources do not keep a single list. It was last checked on 29 October 2026.

## One-stop advice: the PAE points

The government's guides to setting up a company or a self-employed business both refer to the Puntos de Atención al Emprendedor, or PAE. These are advice points run by public bodies, chambers of commerce and business organisations. They help founders fill in the electronic form (the DUE) that starts the process through the CIRCE system, which sends the information to the tax authority and Social Security. A PAE can also explain licences and registrations. Use one if you are unsure about the legal form, the forms or the order of the steps. The locations are listed on ipyme.org, the portal of the Ministry of Industry and Tourism for small and medium-sized companies.

## The central portal for small business

Ipyme.org publishes guides on the life cycle of a business, including creating and starting up a company, and the self-employed route. These are the guides used for the articles on this site about forming an SL and registering as an autónomo. The portal also lists support programmes and financing lines for small firms.

## Chambers of commerce

Spain has public-law chambers of commerce, and the Balearic Islands have chambers for the different islands: Mallorca, Ibiza and Formentera, and Menorca. They offer advice on starting a business, training, export support, mediation services and business events. Many act as PAE points. Membership benefits and fees vary, so look at each chamber's website. Chambers are also a good place to meet local business owners through breakfasts, conferences and training sessions.

## Regional government

The Govern de les Illes Balears has a department for Business, Self-Employed and Energy, led by the conseller Alejandro Sáenz de San Pedro García, and a department for Economy, Finance and Innovation, led by the First Vice President, Antoni Costa Costa, according to the Govern's website. Between them they run grants, support for self-employed people, energy programmes and innovation policy. Calls for grants are published in the official bulletin, the BOIB, and on caib.es. The Department of Labour, Civil Service and Social Dialogue covers employment and training, and the Servei d'Ocupació (SOIB) runs training courses and employment services.

## Island councils and town halls

Island councils promote tourism, agriculture and local products, and many run small grants. Town halls handle licences and often have a business desk. In Palma, the Ajuntament has a website with information on starting a business and on its open data. Ask the town hall about local business associations, because many neighbourhoods and tourist areas have their own.

## Export and foreign investment

For companies that want to sell abroad, the national agency ICEX España Exportación e Inversiones offers information on markets, training and trade missions. The Spanish Chambers also run export programmes. Foreign investors considering the islands can ask the regional government and the chambers for guidance on licences and on how to set up. The article on buying a hotel or tourist business describes the legal points to check.

## Tax and Social Security advice

The Agencia Tributaria runs help desks and online guidance for new businesses. The Tesorería General de la Seguridad Social explains registration and contributions. The Agència Tributària de les Illes Balears gives information on regional taxes. For complex questions, a gestor or accountant is usually worth the fee.

## Digital tools

The state's electronic offices let founders register a company, apply for a digital certificate, file tax returns and communicate with Social Security. Most procedures need a digital certificate or the Cl@ve system. The government guides recommend getting a digital certificate early, because it is needed for electronic signing.

## How to network

Business networking in the islands happens through several channels:

- **Chambers of commerce events,** including breakfasts and talks.
- **Industry associations,** such as hotel federations, restaurant associations and local commerce groups, which publish events for members.
- **Professional bodies,** for architects, lawyers, engineers and other regulated trades.
- **Coworking spaces and innovation hubs,** described in the article on start-ups.
- **Language and community groups** for foreign residents, many of which hold monthly meetings.

Event dates change each year. Check the websites and newsletters of these bodies, and ask your chamber or PAE adviser to add you to their mailing lists.

## A short plan for new founders

- Book a meeting at a PAE point to choose the legal form.
- Check that your premises can legally host the activity.
- Apply for the tax census and Social Security registration.
- Ask the town hall for a licence checklist.
- Join the local chamber of commerce or a trade association.
- Ask the regional government about grants, and subscribe to the BOIB alerts.

## Questions people ask

**Is help free?** PAE points and the government guides are free. Chambers of commerce offer some free services and some paid ones. Private advisers charge fees.

**Do I need to speak Spanish to use these services?** Some staff speak English in the main towns, but forms and procedures are in Spanish and Catalan. A bilingual adviser can help.

**Can foreign investors get help?** Yes, chambers, the regional government and national agencies provide information, and a lawyer should handle the legal steps.

**How do I hear about grants?** Subscribe to the BOIB alerts, follow the regional government's departments and ask your chamber or PAE adviser.
