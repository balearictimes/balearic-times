Menorca has its own food traditions, shaped by fishing, farming and a long history of foreign influence. The island's cheese and gin are known well beyond its shores, and there is a good range of seafood at the harbours.

## Caldereta de llagosta

The most famous dish is caldereta de llagosta, a stew made with spiny lobster, onions, tomato and herbs. It is closely tied to the fishing village of Fornells on the north coast, where many restaurants specialise in it. It is expensive, as the lobster is a prized catch, and it is usually shared between two or more people. Ask the price before you order.

## Mahón cheese

Queso Mahón-Menorca has a protected designation of origin and is made from cow's milk on small farms. Its flavour ranges from mild and buttery when young to sharp and crumbly when aged. It is often served in slices with olive oil and pepper as a starter, or grated over dishes. Many farms sell direct, and you can visit some to see the process.

## Gin

Xoriguer, a gin made in Mahón, is the island's best-known drink. Distilling began in the 18th century, when British troops and sailors were based on the island, and the taste for gin stuck. It is served in a drink called pomada, which mixes gin with cloudy lemonade over ice. Pomada is especially associated with the fiestas of Sant Joan in Ciutadella in June, but you can drink it all summer.

## Mayonnaise

Menorcans say mayonnaise was invented in Mahón, and that the name comes from the town. Historians dispute the story, and other places also claim it, but the sauce is certainly a local favourite. You will find a thick, garlicky version at many restaurants.

## Other dishes to look for

- **Oliaigua**: a tomato and vegetable stew, often made with figs or other fruit and served with bread.
- **Sobrassada and carn i xulla**: cured pork products found across the Balearics, in a local style.
- **Ensaïmades and amargos**: pastries and almond biscuits sold in bakeries.
- **Fresh fish**: bream, red mullet and scorpion fish, grilled or baked, are common at harbour restaurants.

## Shoes and shopping

Avarcas, the rubber-soled leather sandals known locally as menorquinas, are made on the island and sold in shops in Ciutadella and Mahón. They are a practical souvenir and last for years.

## Eating well

- Fornells and Ciutadella's port are the places for seafood, and booking helps in summer.
- Meals start late. Lunch is usually after two and dinner after half past eight, though tourist areas serve earlier.
- Look for a menú del día at lunch for a fixed price.
- Farm shops and weekly markets are the best places to buy cheese and local produce to take home.

## Where to eat it

Fornells is the traditional home of caldereta de llagosta. The restaurants along the harbour front serve it in clay pots, and the lobster is usually weighed before cooking, so ask the price first. In Mahón, the port area has restaurants serving fish and rice, and the Mercat Claustre del Carme sells fresh produce. Ciutadella's port and the streets around the cathedral have a number of traditional restaurants. Es Castell and Cala Figuera on the Mahón harbour have fish restaurants with views of the water.

## Cheese in detail

Mahón-Menorca cheese is made in several grades. Tierno is the youngest and mildest, semicurado is aged for a few months, and curado and añejo are aged longer and are sharper. Many farms around Alaior and Es Mercadal sell direct, and the cheese factory in Alaior can be visited. The cheese is often dressed with olive oil, pepper or a little paprika. Menorca also makes a smaller amount of sheep's milk cheese, and goat's cheese from the interior.

## Gin and the British legacy

Xoriguer gin is distilled in Mahón in a copper still heated by a wood fire. The distillery stands on the harbour front and is open for visits and tastings. The British, who held the island on and off in the 18th century, brought a taste for gin, and the Menorcan version is flavoured with juniper berries. Pomada, gin and lemonade, is the traditional summer drink, served at fiestas in Ciutadella and many village celebrations. The British also left behind words in the local dialect, such as "boínder", meaning bay window, and a number of dishes such as plum cake.

## Seasonal and festive dishes

- **Autumn**: wild mushrooms in the countryside, and the grape harvest on small farms.
- **Winter**: stews with sausage and beans, and the pig slaughter or matances in some villages.
- **Spring**: wild asparagus, artichokes and fresh peas.
- **Summer**: fish from the coves, tomatoes and melons.
- **Christmas**: pastissets, small sweet pastries, and almond-based sweets.

## Sweet treats

Ensaïmades are made in Menorca as well as Mallorca, and bakeries sell them in small and large sizes. Amargos are almond macaroons. Flaons, a small cheese tart, are also popular. Local honey is sold at markets. Ice cream shops in Ciutadella and Mahón make flavours from local fruit and almonds.

## Wine and other drinks

Menorca produces a small amount of wine, and vineyards in the south offer tastings. Beer and cider are made on a small scale. Local gin is sold in distinctive stoneware bottles, and these make a good souvenir.

## Practical tips

- Book fish restaurants in Fornells for peak weeks.
- Ask for a menú del día at lunchtime for the best value.
- Carry cash in smaller villages.
- If you want to take cheese home, ask the producer to vacuum pack it.
- Check the opening times in winter, since many restaurants close.

## Markets

Markets are held across the island on set days. Mahón's market is on Tuesdays and Saturdays, and Ciutadella has a daily covered market. Alaior, Es Mercadal and Ferreries have weekly stalls. Check local listings, since days can change.
