Mallorca is the largest of the Balearic Islands, and getting from one end to the other takes some planning. The public transport network is decent between the main towns, and a hire car opens up the coves and mountain villages. The right choice depends on where you stay.

## From Palma airport

Palma airport lies about eight kilometres east of the city centre. An airport bus runs to the city and the main bus station, and taxis wait outside the terminal. For resorts on the north and east coasts, the island bus company TIB runs services to Alcúdia, Cala Millor and other places, though transfers take an hour or more. Pre-booked private transfers and shared shuttles are popular for families with luggage.

## Buses

TIB runs the island's inter-town buses, and Palma's city buses are operated by EMT. Fares are low and the buses are air conditioned, but timetables thin out in the evening and in winter. Smaller villages and many coves are not served at all, so look up the timetable before planning a day.

## Trains and the Sóller tram

Mallorca has a small railway network that runs from Palma to Inca, Sa Pobla and Manacor. It is useful for market days and for reaching inland towns, but it does not serve the coast.

The historic wooden train between Palma and Sóller is one of the island's best-known trips. It runs through a tunnel in the Tramuntana mountains and ends at Sóller, where an old tram continues to Port de Sóller. It is slow and aimed at visitors as much as commuters.

## Hiring a car

A car is the easiest way to reach beaches, vineyards and mountain villages. Book early for summer, and compare prices carefully, since extras can add up. Check the fuel policy, the excess on the insurance and whether the firm charges extra for a second driver.

Driving in the Tramuntana is slow on narrow roads with tight bends, and groups of cyclists are common, especially in spring. Leave extra time, and do not rely on the satnav's estimate.

## Cap de Formentor

In recent summers, the road to the Formentor lighthouse has been closed to private vehicles, including hire cars, during the day, with exceptions for residents and people with limited mobility. A summer bus service runs from Alcúdia and Port de Pollença to the lighthouse. The dates and hours have varied, so check the current rules before you go.

## Cycling

Mallorca is one of Europe's top spots for road cycling, especially in March, April and October. Bike hire is easy to find, and many roads have wide verges. Wear a helmet and expect a lot of hills.

## Where to base yourself

- **For nightlife and restaurants**: Palma, which has a good old town and plenty of bars.
- **For family beaches**: Alcúdia, Muro or Cala Millor.
- **For mountains and villages**: Sóller or Pollença, with a car or good bus connections.
- **For quiet coves**: the south-east around Santanyí, which is best with a car.

## Public transport by area

Palma is the hub of the public bus network. The city operator, EMT Palma, runs urban lines including the A1 airport express to the city centre, and the main bus and rail station, Estació Intermodal, sits under Plaça d'Espanya. From there, TIB buses and the SFM trains go to the rest of the island. TIB lines link Palma with Alcúdia, Pollença, Sóller, Valldemossa, Andratx, Santanyí and many other towns, and the network runs fewer services in the winter. The Sóller line often uses the road tunnel that opened in 1997, which makes the journey much quicker than the old mountain road.

The SFM train line to Inca runs from the same Estació Intermodal and continues to Sa Pobla and to Manacor. It is a good way to reach the Inca market on Thursdays and the Sineu market on Wednesdays. Sineu is one of the oldest market towns on the island and has a tradition of livestock sales.

## Day trips by public transport

- **Valldemossa**: a direct TIB bus from Palma takes around 30 minutes and drops visitors close to the Carthusian monastery.
- **Sóller**: the TIB bus runs through the Sóller tunnel, and the historic train offers a slower route.
- **Alcúdia**: a long bus ride from Palma or the airport, with an old walled town to explore and a market on Tuesdays and Sundays.
- **Pollença**: a hillside town whose steps, called Calvari, are a classic walk, and whose market takes place on Sundays.
- **Santanyí**: a stone town with a Wednesday and Saturday market, and a good base for the south-east coves.

## Cycling and the Via Verde

Mallorca is a major cycling destination. The roads up through the Tramuntana, including the Sa Calobra climb and the road to Lluc, attract professional teams in winter training camps. Bike hire firms operate around Alcúdia, Port de Pollença, Palma and Port d'Andratx. For gentler rides, the Via Verde paths follow former railway lines in the east, and the flat route along the Bay of Alcúdia passes through the S'Albufera wetland.

## Driving tips

Hire cars are widely available at Palma airport. Check the fuel type, the insurance excess and the cost of a second driver before booking. Parking in Palma's old town is limited, and many streets are restricted to residents, so use the underground car parks at Plaça Major or the Parc de la Mar. Mountain roads are narrow, with tight bends and cyclists. The Ma-10, which runs along the whole Tramuntana coast, is one of the most scenic and demanding roads on the island.

The Sa Calobra road, with its famous knotted bend, has long been a bottleneck in summer. Local councils have introduced paid parking and access controls in busy places, so look at signs on arrival. The same applies at Caló des Moro, where the road narrows and parking is limited.

## Ferries and airports

Palma's port has ferry links to Barcelona, Valencia, Ibiza and Menorca. The Baleària and Trasmed lines sail from the port, and a fast ferry connects Alcúdia with Ciutadella in Menorca. Palma's airport, Son Sant Joan, has a main terminal and a short taxi rank. The airport is the busiest in the Balearics, with hundreds of flights a day in summer.

## Official information

The TIB (Transport de les Illes Balears) site at tib.org publishes timetables for buses and trains, including the Formentor service. EMT Palma publishes city bus information, and Aena provides airport details at aena.es. The traffic authority, the DGT, gives road warnings.
