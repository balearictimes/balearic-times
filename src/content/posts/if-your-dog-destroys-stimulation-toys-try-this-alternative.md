Many dogs love to chew, but some destroy enrichment toys in minutes — and may even swallow the pieces, risking choking or an intestinal blockage. A dog trainer from Tarragona has shared a simple homemade alternative on social media that has caught the attention of owners.

## Why dogs destroy toys

Chewing is natural: dogs explore the world with their mouths, and some breeds have very strong jaws. The problem is not the chewing itself, but how quickly a toy breaks and whether the dog swallows fragments.

When a dog constantly shreds toys, it may be a sign of boredom, frustration, anxiety or a lack of physical and mental exercise. Buying tougher toys rarely solves the underlying cause.

## The frozen pepper

The trainer's idea is to stuff half a red pepper with natural yoghurt, kibble, egg and pieces of apple, and then freeze it. To get at the food, the dog has to lick, sniff and work at the pepper, turning a quick snack into a longer activity.

## The benefits

- It mimics natural foraging behaviour.
- It reduces boredom.
- Repetitive licking has a calming effect, making it useful before the dog is left alone or when visitors arrive.

## Safety tips

- Use plain, unsweetened yoghurt, and never products containing xylitol, which is toxic to dogs.
- Remove the core and seeds from the apple.
- Cook the egg, and offer it only occasionally as part of a balanced diet.
- Supervise the dog the first few times, and introduce new foods gradually in case of stomach upsets.
- Account for the calories in the dog's daily ration.

## Food enrichment

Veterinary behaviourists call this kind of activity "food enrichment": using food to stimulate the dog's mind through searching, licking and problem-solving. Other options include snuffle mats, scattering kibble in the garden or hiding treats around the house.

## When to get help

Consult a vet with behavioural training or a qualified trainer if your dog:

- destroys every toy in minutes;
- repeatedly tries to swallow pieces;
- shows signs of anxiety or compulsive behaviour.

A frozen pepper is not a cure-all, but it is a safe, cheap way to channel a dog's natural need to work for its food.
