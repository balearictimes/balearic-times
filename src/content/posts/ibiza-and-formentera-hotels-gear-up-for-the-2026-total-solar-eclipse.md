Hotels in Ibiza and Formentera are preparing for the total solar eclipse of 12 August 2026, which is expected to attract visitors from across Europe and give the islands a chance to promote themselves as a destination for astronomical tourism.

## Early interest

Some hotels are already receiving enquiries and bookings linked specifically to the eclipse dates. The event will be visible as a total eclipse from parts of Spain, including the Balearic Islands, for the first time in more than a century.

## A workshop for businesses

The Ibiza and Formentera Hotel Federation (FEHIF) organised a workshop to help businesses prepare. Experts explained how the eclipse could affect travel patterns and what operators can do to turn it into an opportunity while keeping guests safe.

## The topics

- designing eclipse-themed experiences for guests;
- safety during solar observation;
- planning transport for larger crowds;
- managing maritime operations, crucial for island destinations where many visitors will want to watch from boats.

## Ideas for hotels

The workshop was led by Javier Ares, co-founder of Polaris Menorca, a company specialising in astronomical tourism. He suggested ideas such as eclipse viewing packages, guided talks on the night sky and themed meals, to attract visitors who are looking for more than just a room.

## Logistics and safety

With more visitors expected in what is already the busiest month of the year, FEHIF stressed the importance of early coordination between hotels, transport companies and the authorities, to manage traffic, emergency services and crowds.

Safe viewing is essential: the Sun must never be looked at directly without certified eclipse glasses or a proper solar filter, except during the brief moments of totality. Hotels are being encouraged to provide or advise guests on proper eye protection.

## An opportunity

FEHIF believes the eclipse could help position the Balearics as a leading European destination for astronomical tourism — a niche that also fits with efforts to promote the islands' dark skies and attract visitors outside the peak season.
