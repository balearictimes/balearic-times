For years, electronics advertising has focused on specifications: faster processors, bigger screens, more megapixels. A new campaign from the retailer My Electro takes a different approach, built around the idea that the best solutions start by listening.

## The concept

The campaign starts from a familiar feeling: going through the day on autopilot, eyes on our phones, rushing from one task to the next. Its argument is that what many customers really want is not more power, but more time — to rest, to pause and to connect with the people who matter.

In that story, the shop assistant who asks the right question matters more than the product on the shelf.

## The stories

The campaign is told through short scenes set in its stores:

- **A stressed customer** comes in after a grey day. Instead of a quick sale, an assistant asks what he is really looking for, turning the visit into a conversation.
- **A grandfather and his granddaughter**, who hides behind her headphones, walk the aisles. He is looking for a way to hear a song his late wife used to sing. A young assistant offers to show him how to find it on a streaming service — and he, in turn, introduces her to the pleasure of listening to a whole album without skipping.

## Technology as a bridge

The message is that technology works best when it brings people together: sharing a playlist, recovering a memory or simply making contact across generations.

## A wider trend

The campaign reflects a broader shift in retail marketing. With products increasingly similar and online shopping dominating on price, physical stores are trying to compete on experience: advice, service and human contact. Emotional storytelling, rather than technical comparison, has become a common way for brands to stand out.

## Does it work?

Emotional campaigns can build brand loyalty, but they also raise expectations. Customers who are drawn in by a promise of attentive, empathetic service will expect to find it when they walk into a store. For retailers, the challenge is to make the experience match the message.
