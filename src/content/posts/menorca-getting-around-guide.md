Menorca is smaller and less crowded than Mallorca or Ibiza, but it still takes some planning to get around, especially if you want to reach the south coast coves.

## Flying in

The island's airport is at Mahón, on the east side, a short drive from the town. Direct flights from the UK are mostly seasonal. Airlines including Jet2 and British Airways have flown from British airports in the summer, with fewer services in spring and autumn, and schedules change from year to year. Outside the season, most visitors connect through Barcelona, Madrid or Palma, so check the options before booking.

A taxi from the airport to Mahón is short and cheap, and buses link the airport with the town. To reach resorts on the west coast, a transfer or hire car is a better choice.

## Ferries

You can also reach Menorca by sea. Ferries run to Ciutadella and Mahón from Mallorca, Barcelona and Valencia, with fast services from Alcúdia to Ciutadella taking about one to two hours depending on the vessel. Crossings are less frequent in winter. They are a good way to combine Mallorca and Menorca without flying.

## Buses

The island has a bus network that connects Mahón, Ciutadella and the main resorts. Services are frequent in summer and thin out in winter. Buses reach many south coast resorts, but not the quieter coves, which can be a long walk from the nearest stop. Look up the timetables before you travel.

## The two towns

Mahón on the east coast and Ciutadella on the west are roughly 45 kilometres apart, linked by a single main road that runs through the middle of the island. The drive takes a little under an hour. Mahón has a large natural harbour, and Ciutadella has a small old port with restaurants.

## Hiring a car

A car is the most practical option for beaches, especially in the south. Book early for July and August, and compare prices with local firms as well as the large chains. Roads are generally easy to drive, but country lanes can be narrow, and parking at the popular coves fills early.

## Cycling and walking

Menorca is good for cycling and walking. The Camí de Cavalls, a footpath around the whole island, is about 185 kilometres long and can be walked in sections. Mountain bikes are available to hire in most resorts, and some routes are suitable for families. Spring and autumn are the best times for long walks.

## Sample plans

- **A week with a car**: base yourself in the middle of the island, near Ferreries or Es Mercadal, and reach both coasts within half an hour.
- **A week without a car**: stay in Mahón or Ciutadella, where buses, shops and restaurants are close, and take a boat trip or bus to the beaches.
- **A short break**: fly into Mahón and stay in town, with a day trip to Fornells on the north coast.

## The airport and the first steps

Menorca's airport is a compact terminal on the south side of Mahón. Car hire desks are in the arrivals hall, and the car park is next to the building. The town centre is a ten-minute taxi ride, and the bus network connects the airport with Mahón and the resorts on the south and east coasts. The road from Mahón to Ciutadella, the Me-1, is a straight main route through the centre of the island. Along it, the towns of Alaior, Es Mercadal and Ferreries make natural stops.

## The towns in more detail

Mahón, called Maó in Menorcan, is the island's capital. Its harbour is among the largest natural harbours in the world and runs for around five kilometres. Steps called the Costa d'es General lead up from the port to the old town. The bus station and the main taxi rank are close to the port, and the Mercat Claustre del Carme sells local cheese and fish.

Ciutadella is on the opposite side of the island, with a pretty port, a cathedral and a market. The bus station is outside the old town. Parking is hard in summer, so use the large car park on the edge of the old centre and walk in.

Es Mercadal, in the centre, is the best base for exploring both coasts. The hill of Monte Toro, at 358 metres, is the island's highest point and has a monastery and a viewpoint at the top. A road leads up to it. Fornells lies a short drive to the north, and Es Migjorn Gran, a quiet village, is a short drive south.

## The Camí de Cavalls

The path, called GR 223, circles the island in 20 stages and is around 185 kilometres long. It follows the old route used by horsemen defending the coast. The path is waymarked, and it is open to walkers, riders and cyclists. In places it crosses private land, so keep to the route and close gates. You can walk short sections from the coves, such as the stretch between Cala Galdana and Cala Macarella.

## Ferries

Menorca's two main ports, Mahón and Ciutadella, have ferry services. The fast ferry from Alcúdia to Ciutadella is the shortest crossing from Mallorca, and slower ferries link Mahón with Barcelona and Valencia. Baleària and Trasmed are the main operators. In winter, services are less frequent, and the sea can be rough, so allow a margin before connecting flights.

## Buses and taxis

Bus operators on the island run services between Mahón and Ciutadella, with stops at Alaior, Es Mercadal and Ferreries. Other lines serve the south and north coasts, and summer lines reach the larger resorts. Taxis are available at ranks in the main towns and can be booked in advance. A taxi between Mahón and Ciutadella is a long and costly ride.

## Hire cars, scooters and bikes

Hire cars are available at the airport and in the main towns. Cars are the best way to see the island, and a small car is easier on narrow roads. Scooters and electric bikes are available in the resorts. Roads are generally in good condition, but rural lanes are narrow and often have walls on both sides.

## Parking at the coves

Popular coves such as Macarella and Turqueta have parking areas that fill by mid-morning. The island council and local councils have introduced paid parking and shuttle buses in some places. Check signs on arrival and avoid parking on the verge, which can lead to fines.

## A three-day plan

- **Day one**: Mahón's harbour, a boat trip, and the Cova d'en Xoroi in the evening.
- **Day two**: the south coast, Cala Macarella, Turqueta and Ciutadella in the evening.
- **Day three**: Monte Toro, Fornells and the north coast.

## Official information

The Consell Insular de Menorca publishes bus timetables and ferry operators publish their own. Aena provides flight information at aena.es.
