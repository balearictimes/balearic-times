Visitors to the Balearic Islands sometimes see banners and demonstrations against mass tourism, and read that locals are losing patience. It is fair to ask how widespread those feelings are. The Balearic government's own tourism agency (AETIB) carries out a regular survey of residents, and its latest published report gives the clearest official answer. This article sets out what the survey found, without adding opinion of its own. It was last checked on 29 October 2026.

## How the survey was done

The 2024 residents' opinion survey on tourism was carried out in October 2024, and the report was published in September 2025. It covered 2,008 residents aged 18 and over: 738 in Mallorca, 460 in Menorca, 460 in Ibiza and 350 in Formentera. Interviews were done by telephone. The sample was drawn by quotas and weighted to match the census, which is a standard method but not a random sample, so small differences should be read with care.

## Satisfaction has fallen

The survey asked whether people agreed with the statement that they are very satisfied with tourism in the Balearic Islands. In 2024, 42 per cent agreed or strongly agreed, while 38.8 per cent disagreed. The same question gave 60.1 per cent in 2016 and 71.4 per cent in 2018, so satisfaction has dropped by about 30 points in six years.

The answer is very different from one island to another. Satisfaction was 68 per cent in Formentera, 65.6 per cent in Menorca, 48.3 per cent in Ibiza and 38 per cent in Mallorca. In other words, Mallorca is where the lowest figure was recorded.

## Crowding

Asked about numbers, 75.6 per cent of residents said there are too many tourists at certain times of the year or all year round. That is up from 70.6 per cent in the previous survey, in 2022. A smaller group, 33.2 per cent, said there are too many tourists overall, and 19.8 per cent were satisfied with the number of arrivals.

On the drawbacks, 65.5 per cent reported a major impact of crowding in streets, shops and transport, and 62.3 per cent a major damage to natural resources.

## Housing and prices

The strongest concern is housing. The survey found that 77.2 per cent of residents see a major impact of tourism on rising housing costs, and 55.3 per cent see a major impact on the price of goods and services. These figures are about perception, not measured prices, but they show where public worry is focused.

## Quality of life, identity and culture

Residents were split on what tourism does to daily life. For quality of life, 19.5 per cent said it improves, 36.3 per cent said it maintains it, 30.8 per cent said it harms it, and 13.4 per cent said it has no influence. On identity, culture and heritage, 15 per cent said it improves them, 38.4 per cent said it maintains them and 32 per cent said it harms them. The report also found that 61.1 per cent think tourism helps cultural heritage, while 51.5 per cent think it harms natural heritage.

## The benefits people see

The survey is not only negative. Residents also named benefits: 49.8 per cent saw a major impact on wealth and income, 37.2 per cent on skilled jobs and 36.1 per cent on leisure offerings. Tourism is the main driver of the Balearic economy, as an article in this series on the economy sets out, and many households depend on it directly or indirectly.

## What residents want done

On management measures, support for limits was strong:

- **Limiting visitor numbers:** 69.1 per cent in favour overall, and 77.4 per cent in Mallorca.
- **Limiting cruise ships:** 69.2 per cent in favour.
- **Limiting tourist establishments and places:** 68.4 per cent.
- **Limiting holiday rentals in private homes:** 80.5 per cent.
- **Raising the Sustainable Tourism Tax:** 67.6 per cent.
- **Improving infrastructure:** 92.9 per cent.

The Govern's own press release on the survey carried a headline built on the 69.1 per cent figure, saying that it matches the tourism limits the Govern approved in May. These results explain why the 2025 decree-law on tourism containment, which is covered in another article, was politically possible.

## What the survey cannot tell us

A survey measures opinions, not causes. It does not prove that tourism is the reason for high rents, nor that limits on tourist places will lower them. It was carried out in autumn 2024, before the decree-law took effect, so it cannot say how residents feel now. Another survey is expected in the usual cycle, and the AETIB publishes its reports on its tourism statistics pages. Opinions also differ sharply by island and by whether a person works in the tourism sector. The report breaks results down by island, and the full document should be consulted for finer detail.

## What visitors can do

None of this means that visitors are unwelcome. The same survey shows that most residents recognise the economic benefits. Respecting local rules and neighbours helps. Practical steps include:

- Avoid noise at night in residential areas.
- Respect beach and nature rules, including bans on anchoring on seagrass.
- Choose registered accommodation.
- Spread your visit outside July and August where you can.
- Use public transport where it is available.

## Where the discussion goes next

Regional and municipal elections are due on 23 May 2027, and the general election is on 29 November 2026. Housing and tourism are likely to be raised in both campaigns. The official statistics published by IBESTAT and the AETIB will provide the data on which the debate is based.

## Questions people ask

**Is the survey representative?** It uses quotas and census weights, which is a standard method, but it is not a random sample. Island and age groups are covered.

**Are the results published in full?** Yes. The AETIB publishes the report on its tourism statistics site, with results by island.

**Does the survey ask about particular nationalities?** The summary used here does not. It asks about tourism in general.
