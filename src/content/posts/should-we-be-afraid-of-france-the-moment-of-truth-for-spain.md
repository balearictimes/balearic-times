Spain's opponent in the World Cup semi-final is one of the most feared teams in international football. France beat Belgium 2–1 in a tight quarter-final, having already overcome Sweden, Paraguay and Morocco on their way to the last four.

## The numbers

- **16** goals scored and only **2** conceded.
- **Kylian Mbappé:** eight goals and three assists, the tournament's top scorer.
- **Ousmane Dembélé:** four goals and two assists.
- **Michael Olise:** five assists, more than any other player.

## Deschamps's method

Didier Deschamps, in charge of France since 2012, has built his success on a simple principle: a solid defence creates the conditions to win. His teams are compact, win their individual duels and attack quickly once they recover the ball. The results speak for themselves: world champions in 2018, finalists in 2022 and now semi-finalists again.

## The midfield

Deschamps's choice of midfielders — N'Golo Kanté, Manu Koné, Adrien Rabiot, Aurélien Tchouaméni and Warren Zaïre-Emery — shows his priorities: physical power, energy and defensive work rather than a classic creative playmaker. Rabiot and Koné are expected to form the double pivot, breaking up the opposition's play and feeding the attackers.

## The attack

Up front, France has an extraordinary range of options. Alongside Mbappé, Dembélé and Olise, Deschamps can call on Désiré Doué, Bradley Barcola, Marcus Thuram, Maghnes Akliouche and Jean-Philippe Mateta — pace, dribbling and finishing that can punish any defensive mistake.

## A possible weakness

France are at their best in transition, with space to run into. Against teams that keep possession and deny them counter-attacks, they can look less fluent, as the midfield lacks a natural organiser to unlock compact defences.

That is precisely where Spain may see an opportunity. A side built on controlling the ball could limit France's chances to break — but any loss of possession in midfield could be punished in seconds by the speed of Mbappé and his team-mates.

## The verdict

France are not a team to fear so much as one to respect. The semi-final looks like a clash of styles: Spain's possession against France's power and pace. Whoever imposes their game is likely to reach the final.
