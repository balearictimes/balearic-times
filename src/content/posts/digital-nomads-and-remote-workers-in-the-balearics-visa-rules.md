The Balearic Islands appeal to people who can work from anywhere: a mild climate, good flight links, coworking spaces in Palma and a lifestyle that suits a laptop and a sea view. Spain has a specific route for non-EU citizens who want to live in the country while working remotely for foreign employers or clients, created by the national start-up law in 2022. This article summarises the rules as the Spanish Ministry of Foreign Affairs describes them. It is general information, not immigration advice, and conditions change, so check the consulate's page for your country and consult a lawyer before applying. It was last checked on 29 October 2026.

## The legal basis

The route comes from Ley 28/2022, the law on promoting the ecosystem of emerging companies, published in the Boletín Oficial del Estado in December 2022. It created a visa and residence authorisation for international teleworking, often called the digital nomad visa. People already legally in Spain can apply for the residence permit directly, without a prior visa, through the Unidad de Grandes Empresas y Colectivos Estratégicos (UGE-CE).

## Who qualifies

According to the Ministry's consular page:

- The route is for non-EU nationals who travel to Spain to do remote work or a professional activity for companies located outside Spain. It does not apply to EU citizens or to people covered by EU law.
- Employees may work only for companies outside Spain.
- Self-employed professionals may work for a Spanish company, but that work can be no more than 20 per cent of their total professional activity.
- The applicant first needs a NIE, the foreigner identification number.

## The income test

The teleworker must show income of at least 200 per cent of the monthly minimum wage, known as the SMI. The national minimum wage for 2026 is 1,221 euros a month in 14 payments, so the test implies 2,442 euros a month, based on the 2026 figure. Treat that calculation as an illustration, and check the consulate's page for the current threshold. For family members, the page requires at least 75 per cent of the SMI for the first member and at least 25 per cent for each additional one. Any proof is accepted, but you must show that the funds are yours, legal and available.

## Qualifications or experience

The applicant must have either a graduate or postgraduate degree from a prestigious university, vocational training or business school, or at least three years of professional experience in comparable functions. If the profession is regulated, the qualification must be recognised in Spain. The visa cannot be granted if the conditions match those of an intra-company transfer visa.

## The employer or client

For employees, the page requires at least three months of seniority with the company and explicit authorisation from the employer to work remotely. Self-employed applicants need a contractual relationship of at least three months. The company must have existed for at least one year, shown by a certificate from the commercial registry. Registration with Social Security, or an equivalent certificate under an international agreement, is required.

## How long it lasts

The visa is valid for one year, or for the length of the residence permit if shorter. Residence authorisations for international teleworking can last up to three years for people already in Spain, according to the page. A Foreigner Identity Card is optional. The visa allows residence and remote work in Spain.

## Family members

A spouse or unmarried partner, financially dependent children, including adult children who have not formed their own family unit, and dependent relatives in the ascending line can apply. Proof of the relationship and of dependency is required.

## Health insurance

Applicants need health insurance from an insurer authorised in Spain that covers all the risks covered by the Spanish public health system. This is not required if you are covered by Spanish Social Security or by an international agreement certificate. The article on healthcare explains the public system, and the article on private care discusses insurance.

## Tax and registration

Residence for tax purposes depends on days spent in Spain and where economic interests lie, and the Agencia Tributaria publishes the rules. Remote workers who become tax residents are taxed on worldwide income at the rates set out in the article on taxes. Registration with the town hall (padrón) is needed for many services. Anyone who wants to work for Spanish clients beyond the 20 per cent limit, or to set up a local business, needs a different type of authorisation. The article on becoming self-employed explains the local route.

## Working in the islands

Palma has coworking spaces and cafés that suit remote work, and other towns are catching up. Connectivity is covered in the article on fibre and 5G. The practical points to consider are housing, which is competitive and expensive, as the housing article explains, seasonal rent changes, and the fact that some villages have weaker mobile coverage. If you plan to rent, check that the landlord's property is properly registered.

## EU citizens

EU citizens do not need a visa to live and work in Spain. They register as residents after three months and need a NIE. The route described above is for non-EU nationals, including British citizens.

## Questions people ask

**Can I work for a Spanish client?** The Ministry says self-employed professionals may work for a Spanish company, but that work can be no more than 20 per cent of their professional activity. Employees may work only for companies outside Spain.

**Can I bring my family?** Yes, spouses or partners, dependent children and dependent relatives in the ascending line can apply, with proof of the relationship and dependency.

**Do I need to speak Spanish?** The Ministry's page does not list a language requirement for the visa. Daily life is easier with Spanish, and Catalan helps in many contexts.

**What if I am already in Spain?** People who are already legally in Spain can apply for the residence authorisation without a prior visa, through the national unit that handles it.
