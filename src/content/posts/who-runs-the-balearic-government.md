The Balearic Islands are one of Spain's 17 autonomous communities. That means they have their own regional government, called the Govern de les Illes Balears, which sits in Palma, and their own parliament, the Parlament. The Govern runs health, education, tourism, housing, transport and many other areas that, in some other countries, are controlled from the national capital. This guide explains how it is made up and who holds the main posts, using the Govern's own website and the Statute of Autonomy. The names were last checked on 29 October 2026.

## How the Govern is built

The Statute of Autonomy, a national organic law passed in 2007 that works as the community's constitution, sets out the structure in article 57. The Govern is a collective body made up of the President, any Vice Presidents and the consellers, who are the heads of departments. It carries out executive and administrative work and sets the general direction of policy. It answers jointly to the Parlament, while each member is also responsible for his or her own department.

The President is chosen after each regional election. The 59 deputies of the Parlament vote, and the candidate who wins the necessary support becomes President. The President then appoints the vice presidents and consellers. The term is four years, tied to the length of the Parlament's mandate. The most recent regional election was held on 28 May 2023, and the next is due on 23 May 2027.

Departments are sometimes renamed or merged, so a minister's title can change even when the person stays in post.

## What the Govern controls

Under the Statute and the national system of devolved powers, the regional government has authority over a wide set of areas. The most important for daily life are these:

- **Health**: the Servei de Salut, known as IB-Salut, runs public hospitals and health centres, including Son Espases in Palma. A separate article in this series explains how it works.
- **Education**: schools and the regional university system, including the rules on teaching in Catalan and Spanish.
- **Tourism**: planning, licensing of tourist accommodation and the sustainable tourism tax, which is collected from overnight guests.
- **Housing and land use**: regional housing policy and the rules that shape what can be built and where.
- **Transport**: regional roads and public transport planning, together with the island councils.
- **Social services**: care for older people, children and dependants.
- **Environment and agriculture**: natural parks, water and fisheries.

Some powers stay with the national government. Defence, foreign affairs, airports (run by Aena), the main taxes and immigration are national matters. Others are shared, and some are in the hands of the four island councils or of the town halls. This is why a single issue, such as housing, can involve all four layers of government at once.

## How money works

The Govern's budget is approved each year by the Parlament. It is funded mainly by transfers from the national government, as set by the regional financing system, and by taxes that have been handed over to the community, together with taxes the region itself sets. The Balearic Tax Agency, the Agència Tributària de les Illes Balears (ATIB), collects regional taxes such as the transfer tax on property purchases and the tourist tax. The tourist tax revenue is meant to be spent on environmental and sustainability projects.

## Checks on the Govern

The Parlament can question the President and consellers, and it can reject the budget. The Govern also has to follow national laws and the Constitution, and its decisions can be challenged before the courts. Decree-laws, which the Govern may use in urgent cases, must be approved by the Parlament within a fixed period to remain in force. The tourism containment measure of 2025 was one of these, and an article in this series explains it.

## How to contact the Govern

The Govern's website, caib.es, publishes the full organisation chart, press releases, the official bulletin and a directory of departments. Residents can use the same site to apply for many regional services. The official bulletin, the Butlletí Oficial de les Illes Balears (BOIB), publishes decrees, orders and notices in both Catalan and Spanish.

## Questions people ask

**Is the President elected by voters directly?** No. The Parlament votes after each regional election, and the candidate with enough support becomes President.

**How long does the government last?** The mandate is four years, tied to the Parlament's term, with the next election due on 23 May 2027.

**Can the Govern pass laws?** The Parlament passes laws. The Govern can approve decree-laws in urgent cases, which the Parlament must validate within a set period.

**Where do I find the official list of departments?** The Govern's website, caib.es, lists the structure and is updated when the organisation changes.
