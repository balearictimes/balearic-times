Spain will hold a general election on Sunday 29 November 2026. The date comes from Real Decreto 806/2026, signed on 5 October 2026 and published in the Boletín Oficial del Estado (BOE) the next day. The decree dissolves the two chambers of the Cortes Generales, the Congreso de los Diputados and the Senado, which were elected on 23 July 2023. This article sets out what the official documents say, with the dates and numbers that matter for the Balearic Islands. Details were last checked on 29 October 2026.

## Why the election is happening now

The Prime Minister, Pedro Sánchez, announced the dissolution on 5 October 2026 from La Moncloa. The government's own statement gave its reasons as the wish to continue its social and economic agenda and to seek a broader mandate, and it mentioned pending reforms on inequality, public services and housing. The statement is the government's account of its motives, and opposition parties have their own views about why the vote was called. This article does not weigh those arguments. It sticks to the process.

Under the Constitution, the Prime Minister can ask the King to dissolve the chambers and call an election, and the decree sets a fixed calendar once that happens. The calendar is the same for every part of Spain.

## The calendar

The decree and the official guides published by the government and the Instituto Nacional de Estadística give these dates:

- **1 August 2026**: the electoral roll was closed for this election.
- **12 to 19 October**: the period to consult the roll and make claims.
- **28 October**: the candidacies were published in the BOE.
- **Friday 13 November at 00:00**: the official campaign begins. It lasts 15 days.
- **From 2 November**: voter cards are sent by post.
- **31 October to 4 November**: the draw for polling station members.
- **Friday 27 November at 24:00**: the campaign ends, followed by a day of reflection on Saturday 28 November.
- **Sunday 29 November**: polling stations are open from 09:00 to 20:00.
- **4 to 7 December**: the Provincial Electoral Boards carry out the definitive count.
- **23 December at 10:00**: the new Cortes meet for their constituent sessions.

The government says the electorate is 38,728,356 people, of whom 35,958,711 live in Spain and 2,769,645 are registered abroad. About 1,615,127 people in Spain are able to vote in a general election for the first time.

## What the Balearics elect

For the Congreso, the Illes Balears is a single constituency and elects eight deputies. For comparison, the official figures list 38 for Madrid and 32 for Barcelona. Of the 350 seats in the Congreso, each province gets a minimum of two, Ceuta and Melilla get one each, and 248 are distributed according to population. Voters choose a closed and blocked list, which means they vote for a party list as a whole and cannot reorder it. Seats are allocated with the D'Hondt method, which tends to favour larger lists in smaller constituencies.

For the Senado, the electoral system works differently. Mallorca elects three senators, and Menorca, Ibiza and Formentera each elect one. Those senators are chosen directly by voters on the island concerned. The Senate also includes members appointed by the regional parliaments. The Parlament de les Illes Balears designates senators in addition to the directly elected ones, and the number depends on the population of the community. The senators who took office after the 2023 election lose their seats at dissolution and the new Senate will be formed after 29 November.

The two ballots look different. The Congress ballot is white and the Senate ballot is sepia. On the Senate ballot, voters can mark more than one name in constituencies with several seats. The instructions on the ballot paper say how many names you can mark, so read them before you vote.

## Who is leaving and who is standing

The Balearic Islands have been represented in the Congreso by eight deputies elected in 2023. One of them, Francina Armengol, of the PSIB-PSOE, was President of the Congreso from 17 August 2023 until the dissolution on 6 October 2026, according to the Congreso's own records. She now presides over the Diputación Permanente, the standing committee that acts while the chamber is dissolved.

The final lists for the Balearic constituency were published through the BOE on 28 October 2026 as part of the national candidacies. The names are in the BOE, and the Junta Electoral Provincial in Palma can be contacted for questions on the Balearic lists. This article does not reproduce the lists, because the parties can still present small corrections within the legal deadlines and the BOE publication is the authoritative source.

## How to vote

You can vote in person or by post. In person, you go to the polling station assigned to you on your voter card, which you can also look up through the INE and Ministry of Interior online tools. You need to show an identity document with a photograph. The accepted documents are the DNI, a passport or a driving licence. People who work on election day are entitled to a period of paid time off to vote, up to four hours.

For a postal vote within Spain, the request must be made at a Correos office or online by 19 November 2026. The online route requires a DNIe or a digital certificate. The documents are sent out from 9 November. The vote must be posted by certified mail, which is free for the voter, before 25 November. Do not put your identity document inside the envelope with the ballot.

Spaniards living abroad have separate deadlines. People registered in the CERA register (Censo Electoral de Residentes Ausentes) and those temporarily abroad must apply by 31 October, in person at a consulate in some cases, and documents are sent from 3 November. The INE lists ballot-box voting at consulates between 21 and 26 November.

Only Spanish nationals can vote in a general election. Residents who are citizens of the European Union or the United Kingdom can vote in municipal elections, and EU citizens can also vote in European Parliament elections, but not in general elections. A separate article in this series explains the rules for foreign residents.

## What comes after the vote

The result will not name a government on its own. After the Cortes meet on 23 December, the King consults the party representatives and proposes a candidate for Prime Minister. That candidate must win the confidence of the Congreso. The details are set by the Constitution.

For readers in the Balearics, regional and municipal elections are a separate matter. Under the electoral law, they take place on the fourth Sunday of May every four years, which puts the next ones on 23 May 2027. The general election does not change that date.
