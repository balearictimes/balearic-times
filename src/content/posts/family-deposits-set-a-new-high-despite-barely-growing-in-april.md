Spanish households held a record €1.1024 trillion in bank deposits in April 2026, according to Bank of Spain data — but the increase from the previous month was minimal.

## The figures

| | April 2026 | Change |
|---|---|---|
| Household deposits | €1.1024 trillion | +€600 million on March |
| Year-on-year | | +€51.2 billion (+4.9%) |
| Company deposits | €348.4 billion | -3.6% on March, +7.6% year-on-year |

## Growth almost flat

The monthly increase of just 0.05% suggests that the strong growth in household savings deposits seen in recent years has levelled off. Over 12 months, however, families have added more than €51 billion to their deposits.

## Companies

Deposits held by non-financial companies fell by 3.6% in April compared with March, to €348.4 billion, although they remain 7.6% higher than a year earlier. Monthly movements in company deposits often reflect the timing of tax payments, dividends and investment.

## Why families keep so much in the bank

Several factors explain the high level of savings held in deposits:

- **Caution:** after years of economic uncertainty and inflation, many households prefer to keep savings safe and easily accessible.
- **Interest rates:** higher rates in recent years made deposits more attractive, although banks have been slow to pass them on fully to savers.
- **Saving for housing:** with rising house prices and the need for large deposits to buy, many families, particularly younger ones, are saving to buy a home.

## What it means

The stalling of deposit growth could indicate that households are starting to spend more, or to move money into other products such as investment funds, government bonds or shares in search of better returns. Banks and policymakers will be watching closely to see whether that trend continues.

## Tips for savers

With significant sums sitting in current accounts earning little or nothing, it is worth comparing the interest rates offered on savings accounts and fixed-term deposits, and considering other low-risk options, such as Spanish Treasury bills, for money that is not needed in the short term.
