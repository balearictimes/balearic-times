Ibiza has a mild Mediterranean climate, but the island feels like two different places depending on the month. The summer is hot, busy and expensive. The winter is quiet, cool and partly closed. The shoulder months in between suit many visitors best.

## January and February

These are the coolest months, with daytime temperatures that often sit in the low teens Celsius. Many hotels, restaurants and beach clubs are shut. Almond trees blossom in late winter, and the island is quiet enough for long walks along the coast. Bring a jacket and do not expect swimming weather.

## March and April

Spring brings milder days and greener hills. Some hotels reopen for Easter, and the first visitors arrive. The sea is still cold, but the walking is good and the island is calm.

## May

May is a strong choice for people who want warm days without the crowds. The big clubs hold their opening parties, and beach spots begin to fill. The sea is cool but swimmable for the hardy.

## June

June is usually sunny and not yet at its peak heat. Beach clubs and restaurants are open, and prices are lower than in July and August. It is one of the best months to visit if you want both good weather and some space.

## July and August

These are the busiest and hottest months. Daytime temperatures often pass 30°C, the sea reaches its warmest and the island is at full capacity. Roads and beaches are crowded, and accommodation prices are at their highest. Book flights, hotels and any hire car well ahead.

## September

Many residents say September is the best month. The sea is still warm from the summer, the crowds thin, and the days are often settled and bright. Some of the biggest closing parties take place at the end of the month.

## October

The season winds down. Early October can still be warm enough for the beach, but the weather becomes less predictable and a few storms are likely. Many seasonal places close by the end of the month.

## November and December

The island is quiet and cooler, and some days are wet. Most clubs have closed. Christmas brings local fiestas and traditional food, and some visitors enjoy the peace.

## A quick guide

- **Best for beaches and swimming**: June to September.
- **Best for fewer crowds with good weather**: late May, June and September.
- **Best for walking and cycling**: March to May and October.
- **Best for clubs**: July and August, or the weeks either side for lower prices.
- **Cheapest**: November to March, though many places will be closed.

Whatever month you pick, check that your hotel, restaurants and any boat trips are open, as the dates vary by business and by year.

## What each month looks like on the ground

In January, almond trees flower around Santa Agnès de Corona and the valleys of the north, and the beaches are empty. February continues the blossom, and some village restaurants offer winter menus. March brings the first warm days, and the Easter processions in Ibiza Town's Dalt Vila are among the most striking events of the year, with hooded penitents carrying religious images through the old streets in Holy Week.

April and May see seasonal workers return and shops reopen. The Santa Eulària Hippy Market at Es Canar and the Las Dalias market near Sant Carles resume their weekly schedules. In May the opening parties begin, and the sea warms slowly.

June sees the feast of Sant Joan on 23 and 24 June and celebrated with bonfires on beaches and in villages across the island, including Sant Joan de Labritja. Midsummer is also the time when the days are longest, with sunsets after 9 pm.

July and August are the hottest months. The sea reaches around 26°C, and the beach clubs at Ses Salines, Cala Jondal and Platja d'en Bossa are at full capacity. Local patron saint days take place across the island, including the festivities in Sant Antoni on 24 August.

September is often called the best month. The festival season ends with closing parties, and the sea remains warm. October brings the first rains, and the almond harvest begins. By November, the island is quiet and most seasonal businesses have closed.

## Weather facts worth knowing

Ibiza gets about 300 days of sunshine a year, and the annual rainfall is low, mostly falling between October and December. The prevailing wind in summer is a light sea breeze, while in winter the cold tramontana can blow from the north. August nights are warm, with temperatures rarely falling below 22°C, so air conditioning is useful. Midday sun is intense from June to August, and the UV index is high.

## Planning around events

- **Easter**: processions in Dalt Vila and Santa Eulària. Book early, since hotels in Ibiza Town fill.
- **Late May**: the opening parties at the main clubs, with good weather and lower prices than July.
- **23 and 24 June**: Sant Joan bonfires and festivities.
- **Late August**: patron saint celebrations in Sant Antoni.
- **Late September and early October**: closing parties and the quiet return to normal life.

## Choosing by interest

For swimmers, the best period is from late June to the end of September, when the water is at its warmest. For hikers, the cool months from October to April are better, with walks along the coast and through the pine woods of Santa Agnès, Sant Joan and the Serra de Sa Talaia, which at 475 metres is the highest point on the island. For photographers, the soft light of spring and autumn suits the whitewashed villages of Santa Gertrudis, Sant Carles and Santa Eulària, and the old town walls of Dalt Vila at dusk.

## Prices and bookings

Peak weeks in July and August can cost twice what the same room costs in May or October. Hire cars sell out first, so book early. Restaurants in popular beach areas often take reservations weeks in advance. In the shoulder months, accommodation is easier to find, and the island offers better value overall.

## Official information

The Spanish weather agency, AEMET, publishes forecasts for the Balearics at aemet.es, and the regional government gives heat and storm warnings. In summer, check the heat warnings before planning long days on exposed beaches.
