A new four-part documentary series about Rocío Jurado, the Andalusian singer known as "La más grande" (The Greatest), presents her as a woman ahead of her time in a Spain still emerging from dictatorship.

## From Chipiona to the world

Jurado was born into a modest family in Chipiona, in the province of Cádiz, and grew up under Franco, at a time of limited freedoms and rigid expectations of women. Her extraordinary voice and stage presence took her from local performances to national stardom, and in the 1980s she toured extensively in Latin America, where audiences dubbed her "la más grande".

## The series

The series, released on Movistar Plus+, traces her journey from humble beginnings to international stardom and shows how her personal life reflected the transformation of Spanish society.

It is narrated by the Chilean actress and singer Daniela Vega, who was too young to have experienced most of Jurado's career but is a passionate admirer. The director explains that Vega approaches the story almost like a fable, telling it with enjoyment and emotion.

## A hidden manuscript

Her daughter, Rocío Carrasco, executive producer of the series, explains that her mother never kept a diary. However, after the death of Jurado's former husband, the boxer Pedro Carrasco, she found an envelope containing photographs and a written account of her mother's life up to the couple's separation. She kept it for years and only rediscovered it while researching the documentary. The manuscript adds personal reflections and details previously unknown or only rumoured.

## A declared feminist

Jurado never hid her views. When asked on television whether she was a feminist, she replied that of course she was — an answer that surprised many at the time.

In 1974, she caused a sensation by wearing a semi-transparent dress at a television gala, an episode often linked to the political turbulence that ended the tenure of the then information minister, Pío Cabanillas. Jurado insisted the daring outfit belonged to her stage persona, not her private self.

## Mother and star

Rocío Carrasco recalls that her mother's work often took her away for months at a time, but that she always felt loved. Her first trip, at eight months old, was to Mexico, where her mother was performing.

## The songwriters

The series highlights the role of two composers: Manuel Alejandro, who wrote many of the passionate songs that defined her image, and Juan Pardo, who helped her reinvent herself with the album *Punto de partida* (1989).

## Her legacy

Rocío Jurado, who died in 2006, remains one of the great voices of Spanish song — and, as the series argues, a woman whose independence and self-expression challenged the conventions of her time.
