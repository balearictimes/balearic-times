Taylor Swift has won a trademark dispute with a US home textiles company that wanted to use the name "Swift Home" for bedding.

## The dispute

The company, Cathay Home, applied to register "Swift Home" as a trademark for products such as sheets and pillows. Swift's legal team filed a formal opposition with the US Patent and Trademark Office.

Her lawyers argued that the name, combined with a logo in cursive lettering that closely resembled Swift's own registered signature, could lead consumers to believe she endorsed or was connected with the products.

## The company backs down

Cathay Home, which sells home textiles through major US retailers, withdrew its application. Its lawyer said the trademark was not essential to the company's business. The company had not actually sold products under the "Swift Home" name.

## Protecting the brand

Swift is known for protecting her name and work closely. Over the years she has registered a large number of trademarks, covering everything from album titles and lyrics to merchandise, and her team regularly challenges applications that could be confused with her brand.

## Why it matters

Trademark law protects not only company names but also the reputation and goodwill attached to famous people. When a brand uses a celebrity's name or image in a way that might suggest an endorsement that does not exist, the celebrity can object. For artists whose names are worth billions, defending them is part of protecting their business.

The case is also a reminder for small businesses: choosing a name that echoes a famous person — even unintentionally — can lead to costly legal disputes. Checking existing trademarks before applying is always a good idea.
