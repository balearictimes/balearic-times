The 2026 tourist season in the Balearic Islands is shaping up to be similar to the successful 2025 season, according to the first indications from the industry. But behind the positive forecasts, one problem dominates every conversation: housing.

## Demand holds up

Tour operators report steady demand for the islands. At FITUR, the tourism fair in Madrid, Steve Heapy, chief executive of Jet2, warned of economic headwinds in Britain, saying UK consumers were feeling the effect of higher taxes and delaying some purchases. Even so, advance bookings point to another solid year.

## The accommodation crunch

The housing shortage now affects almost every sector of the island economy. Hotels, restaurants, shops and nautical businesses all report difficulties housing seasonal staff, and many employers have had to find their own solutions — renting buildings or flats for workers, or offering accommodation as part of the job.

Some workers turn down jobs because they cannot find a room they can afford, and some businesses have had to reduce opening hours or services because they cannot complete their teams.

## A crisis for residents

For residents, the problem is not operational but personal. Rents have risen far faster than wages in recent years, and many young islanders cannot afford to leave home, let alone buy. Public efforts to build protected housing are under way, but the homes will take years to arrive.

## Some progress

There have been improvements in other areas. Measures to limit traffic in the busiest places during peak months, combined with better public transport, have eased congestion at some well-known bottlenecks. Authorities have also stepped up action against illegal holiday rentals in residential buildings, aiming to return homes to the long-term rental market.

## The challenge

Tourism remains the engine of the Balearic economy, and a good season matters to thousands of families. But the success of the season increasingly depends on something outside the industry's direct control: whether the people who make it work can find somewhere to live. Solving that will require cooperation between governments, employers and the construction sector — and it will not happen in a single season.
