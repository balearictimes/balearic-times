Many people associate heatstroke with lying in the sun, but doctors warn that it can happen anywhere the body is unable to get rid of heat — including indoors.

## What is heatstroke?

Heatstroke occurs when the body can no longer control its temperature. Normally, sweating cools us down, but in heatstroke the body's core temperature rises, often above 40°C, and the body's own cooling system fails. Organs, including the brain, can be damaged. It is a medical emergency.

## It can happen indoors

Direct sunlight is not the key factor; the inability to lose heat is. Risky situations include:

- **closed, poorly ventilated rooms,** especially when humidity is high and sweat cannot evaporate;
- **physical effort in warm conditions,** such as exercise, sport or heavy work in a hot gym, garage or kitchen.

## Other factors

The risk increases when:

- humidity is high, above around 60%;
- there is little or no air movement;
- air pollution is high;
- people live in dense urban areas where buildings and asphalt hold the heat, especially at night.

## Who is most at risk?

- older people, particularly those who live alone;
- people with chronic illnesses such as heart failure, diabetes, kidney disease or dementia;
- people taking several medicines, some of which affect the body's ability to cope with heat;
- babies and young children;
- outdoor workers and people exercising in the heat.

Doctors describe one of the highest-risk profiles as an elderly woman living alone with several health conditions.

## Warning signs

- A body temperature of 40°C or more.
- Confusion, agitation or unusual behaviour.
- Hot, red skin, which may be dry or sweating.
- Nausea or vomiting.
- Rapid breathing and heartbeat.
- Headache.

## What to do

1. **Call 112 immediately.**
2. **Move the person somewhere cooler** — shade, an air-conditioned room or near a fan.
3. **Remove excess clothing.**
4. **Cool them quickly:** apply cool, wet cloths to the neck, armpits and groin, or use cool (not icy) water.
5. **Give small sips of water** only if the person is fully conscious and able to swallow.

## Prevention

- Drink water regularly.
- Keep your home cool: close blinds during the day and ventilate at night.
- Avoid physical effort in the hottest hours.
- Check on older relatives and neighbours during heatwaves.
