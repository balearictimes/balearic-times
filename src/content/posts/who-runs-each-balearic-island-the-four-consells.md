Spain has provinces, autonomous communities and town halls, and the Balearic Islands add a fourth layer that visitors rarely hear about: the island councils. Each of the four main islands has a Consell, which is the government of that island. They sit between the regional Govern in Palma and the local town halls, and they decide a lot about roads, tourism promotion, culture, social services and, since 2025, how many tourist places an island can hold. This article explains how they work and who leads them. It was last checked on 29 October 2026.

## What the law says

The Statute of Autonomy of 2007 says in article 61 that the Consells Insulares are the governing institutions of each island, with responsibility for its government, administration and representation. For Mallorca, Menorca and Ibiza, the required bodies are a plenary assembly, a president and an executive council. The councillors serve four years, and the president is elected by the plenary from among its members. The executive council, which works like a cabinet, is made up of the president, any vice presidents and the executive councillors.

Formentera is different. Under article 63.2 of the Statute, the Consell de Formentera is made up of the councillors of the town hall of Formentera, so there is no separate executive council. Formentera is a single municipality, so island and town government are in effect combined.

Councillors are elected on the same day as the regional and municipal elections, with the next ones due on 23 May 2027. On that day, Balearic voters are normally handed separate ballots for the Parlament, the island council and the town hall, so one visit to the polling station involves three choices. The Formentera council, as the Statute describes it, is made up of the councillors of the town hall.

## Mallorca

The Consell de Mallorca is based in Palma. Its president, according to the council's electronic office, is Llorenç Sebastià Galmés Verger, of the Partido Popular. It manages the island's roads, much of the cultural and heritage policy, social services, sports facilities, the Serra de Tramuntana's public paths and a number of agencies. It also runs the island's tourism promotion in partnership with the Govern. Mallorca is by far the largest island, with the greatest share of the Balearic population, so the council's budget and staff are the largest too.

## Ibiza

The Consell d'Eivissa is based in Ibiza Town. Its president, according to the council's website, is Vicent Marí Torres, of the Partido Popular, who also holds the portfolio for tourism promotion, rural and marine areas. The vice presidents listed are Mariano Juan Colomar and Sara Ramón Roselló. Other executive councillors named on the site are Salvador Losa Marí, Maria Fajarnés Costa, Carolina Escandell Ferrer, Marilina Ribas Cardona and Ignacio José Andrés Roselló. The council runs the island's roads, culture and heritage, and it is the body behind the vehicle limits that apply in summer, which are administered through the Ibiza Circular system.

## Menorca

The Consell Insular de Menorca is based in Maó. The council's website blocks automated access, so this article does not name its current president. The council's own pages list the president, the vice presidents and the executive councillors, and they should be consulted directly at cime.es. Menorca is a UNESCO Biosphere Reserve, and its council has a long record of environmental policy, including the management of the coastal path, the Camí de Cavalls, and the rules on building in the countryside.

## Formentera

The Consell de Formentera is based in Sant Francesc Xavier. As the Statute says, the council is made up of the island's town councillors. The council's website could not be read automatically when this article was checked, so it does not name the president. The current office holders are listed at consellformentera.cat. The council manages the island's roads, the tourist information service, the rules on vehicles in the summer and the relationship with the natural park of Ses Salines, which is shared with Ibiza.

## What the island councils control

Powers differ in detail, but the main areas are:

- **Roads**: island roads outside towns.
- **Culture and heritage**: museums, archaeological sites and festivals.
- **Social services**: care for older people and dependants in coordination with the Govern.
- **Tourism promotion**: each island has its own tourist board and brand.
- **Sport and youth**: grants and facilities.
- **Spatial planning**: island territorial plans, which set rules on where construction is allowed.

A key change in 2025 came from the tourism containment decree-law approved by the Govern. It asked each island council to set a ceiling on tourist places and to define the carrying capacity of its island. That puts the councils at the centre of one of the most debated issues in the islands.

## Why it matters for residents and businesses

Because island councils set the rules on planning and tourism, a business or a resident can be affected by decisions made at three levels. A hotel needs a licence from the Govern, planning approval from the town hall and compliance with the island territorial plan. A walker on a mountain path in Mallorca uses a route maintained by the Consell. A visitor to Ibiza in July faces vehicle rules set at island level.

## How to follow what they do

Each council publishes its plenary agendas, budgets and decisions on its website, and the meetings are usually open to the public. The BOIB publishes official acts in both languages. The councils' press offices give regular updates, and the rules on transparency oblige them to publish contracts and salaries.

## Questions people ask

**Is a Consell the same as a town hall?** No. A town hall governs a municipality, while the Consell governs the whole island. In Formentera, the two are combined because the island is a single municipality.

**Who do I contact about island roads?** The island council in charge of that island. Town roads belong to the town hall.

**Can I attend council meetings?** Plenary sessions are normally open to the public, and the agendas are published online.
