## Introduction to Inclusive Sport
The Consell d'Eivissa's educational programme, DIE Esport Inclusiu a l’Escola, has held its third day of activities as part of its sixth edition. The programme aims to promote inclusive sport and raise awareness about disability among students. On this occasion, 43 students from 6th grade of primary school at CEIP Sa Graduada participated in the event, which included a talk by paralympic table tennis player Álex Díaz Tirado.

## Sharing Experiences
Álex Díaz, the first athlete from Ibiza to participate in the Paralympic Games, shared his personal and sporting trajectory with the students. He talked about the challenges he has faced and his experience in international competitions. The students had the opportunity to ask questions and learn about the values and demands of paralympic sport.

## Practical Activities
After the talk, the students participated in a practical session where they could try out different adaptive sports, such as adapted table tennis, sitting volleyball, goalball, and athletics for people with physical and visual disabilities. The activities were supported by technicians from the DIE programme, teachers from the school, and the coordinator of Sports at the Consell d'Eivissa, Antoni Suñer. Álex Díaz himself led a workshop on adapted table tennis, sharing his knowledge and experience with the students.

## Programme Objectives
The DIE Esport Inclusiu a l’Escola programme is promoted and financed by the Department of Sports of the Consell Insular d’Eivissa, in collaboration with the Càtedra Fundación Sanitas d’Estudis sobre Esport Inclusiu (CEDI) and the Ibiza table tennis delegation. The programme combines the testimony of paralympic athletes with the practice of different adaptive disciplines, with the objective of raising awareness about disability, promoting participation, and fostering values such as cooperation, respect, and equality of opportunities. The programme will continue during the 2026-2027 academic year with new activities at different educational centres on the island, featuring paralympic athletes and professionals in adaptive sport.
