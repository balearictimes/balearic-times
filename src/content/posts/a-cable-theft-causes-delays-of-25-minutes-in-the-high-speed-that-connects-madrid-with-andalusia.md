A theft of cable on the high-speed railway between Madrid and Andalusia caused delays of around 25 minutes to trains on one of Spain's busiest rail corridors.

## What happened

Thieves stole copper cable from the section between Malagón and Ciudad Real, damaging part of the signalling system. Signals tell trains when they can proceed safely; when they fail, trains must run at reduced speed or under special procedures, which slows the whole line.

## The response

Adif, the public company that manages Spain's rail infrastructure, sent technical teams to locate the damaged sections, replace the stolen cable and test the signalling system before restoring normal operations.

## Advice for travellers

Passengers travelling on the line were advised to check the status of their train with their operator before going to the station, as knock-on delays can affect services later in the day.

## A recurring problem

Cable theft is a persistent problem for Spain's railways. Copper is valuable on the scrap market, and the long, often isolated stretches of track make it difficult to protect. Each theft can cause significant disruption for thousands of passengers, as well as costly repairs.

The Madrid–Seville high-speed line, which opened in 1992 and was Spain's first, carries large numbers of passengers between Madrid, Córdoba, Seville and Málaga. Because so many trains share the same tracks, a problem in one section can quickly spread across the network.

## Prevention

Measures used to tackle cable theft include more patrols and surveillance, alarm systems that detect when cables are cut, marking copper so it can be traced, and closer checks on scrap dealers. Some rail operators are also replacing copper with materials that have little resale value.

## Passengers' rights

Depending on the length of the delay and the operator, passengers may be entitled to compensation. It is worth checking each company's conditions and keeping tickets and booking confirmations.
