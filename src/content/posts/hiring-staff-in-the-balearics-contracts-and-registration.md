Hiring people in Spain follows a clear sequence, and the Balearic Islands add a seasonal twist: many employers need to recruit dozens of workers within weeks every spring. This guide sets out the steps in the order an employer should follow them, using the official guides on ipyme.org, the minimum wage decree for 2026 and the statistics from the Consell Econòmic i Social (CES) and the Balearic tourism agency. It is general information, not legal advice, and it was last checked on 29 October 2026.

## Step 1: register as an employer

Before hiring anyone, the business must register as an employer with the Tesorería General de la Seguridad Social, using form TA.6, according to the government guide for individual entrepreneurs. This applies to self-employed people with staff and to companies. The registration gives the business an employer account code, which is used for all contributions.

## Step 2: affiliate each worker

Each worker must be registered with Social Security before they start. The guide lists forms TA.1 or TA.2/S for this step. Registration gives the worker cover for health, accidents, unemployment benefit and pension. Working without registration is illegal and leaves the worker without protection, and it exposes the employer to penalties.

## Step 3: put the contract in writing and register it

Contracts are registered with the public employment service, the SEPE, within 10 days of signing. Written contracts are required for many types of employment, and the contract should state the job, pay, hours, trial period and the collective agreement that applies. A copy goes to the worker.

## Step 4: open the workplace with the labour authority

The employer must notify the regional labour authority that the workplace is opening, either before opening or within 30 days after. In the Balearics, this is handled by the department responsible for labour, which the Govern's website lists under Labour, Civil Service and Social Dialogue.

## Step 5: display the work calendar

The work calendar, which sets the year's working days, holidays and schedules, must be displayed at the workplace.

## Contract types

Spanish law has a small number of contract families. The main ones for Balearic employers are:

- **Indefinite contract (contrato indefinido).** The default permanent contract.
- **Fijo discontinuo.** A permanent contract for seasonal activity, in which the employee works in the periods the business operates and is called back each season. It is widely used in hospitality and tourism.
- **Fixed-term contract.** Allowed only for defined reasons, such as a temporary rise in demand or replacing an absent worker, after labour reforms in recent years.
- **Training and apprenticeship contracts.** For people combining work with study.
- **Part-time contracts.** With hours stated in writing.

The Balearic tourism agency reported that the rate of temporary contracts in tourism was 13.6 per cent in the fourth quarter of 2025, up 4.3 percentage points. The statistics show that temporary work remains a significant part of the sector.

## Pay

The national minimum wage in 2026 is 1,221 euros a month in 14 payments, or 17,094 euros a year, set by Real Decreto 126/2026. Collective agreements often set higher pay by category. In hospitality, the collective agreement for the Balearic Islands sets pay scales, working hours and conditions. These agreements are published in the regional official bulletin, the BOIB. An employer must apply the agreement that covers their business.

Employers deduct income tax withholding and the worker's social security contribution from pay, and pay their own contribution. The Agencia Tributaria publishes withholding tables.

## Seasonal peaks

The CES reports that the number of workers registered with Social Security in July 2024 was 34.9 per cent higher than in December. Employers therefore plan recruitment around the calendar. Many start interviewing in winter, ask returning staff to confirm their availability and look for workers abroad. The regional employment service, the SOIB, and the national service, SEPE, publish job offers and can help match candidates.

## Housing for staff

Housing is the main obstacle for recruiting in the islands. Some employers provide staff accommodation or help with rent. If you provide housing as a benefit, check the tax and Social Security rules on payments in kind. The article on housing explains the regional and national measures that apply.

## Workers from abroad

European Union citizens can be hired without a work permit, but need a NIE. For non-EU nationals, the employer or worker must apply for a work and residence authorisation. The Ministry of Foreign Affairs and the immigration offices publish the steps. Employers should not take on anyone who does not have the right to work.

## Health and safety

Employers have duties on occupational risk prevention. These include assessing risks, giving training, providing protective equipment and, for larger employers, arranging health checks. The Balearic labour authority and the national labour inspectorate supervise compliance.

## Ending a contract

Rules on dismissal, notice, severance and unemployment benefit are set by the Workers' Statute. Seasonal workers on fijo discontinuo contracts are not dismissed at the end of the season, but suspended until the next call. Consult a labour lawyer before ending any contract.

## Payroll help

Many small businesses use a gestoría to run payroll and filings. Ask for a clear scope of services, check that they file the monthly contributions on time and keep copies of everything.

## Questions people ask

**Can I hire someone for just the summer?** Yes, through a fixed-term contract if the legal reasons apply, or a fijo discontinuo contract for recurring seasonal work. Seek advice on which fits.

**Do I need an adviser?** Not by law, but many small employers use a gestoría to run payroll and file contributions on time.

**What if a worker has no NIE?** An EU citizen needs a NIE to be registered. A non-EU citizen needs work authorisation. Do not employ anyone without registering them.

**Where do I post vacancies?** The SOIB and SEPE job portals are public. Many employers also advertise through trade associations.
