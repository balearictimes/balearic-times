Handheld gaming consoles are perfect for summer trips — until the battery runs out. With manufacturers such as Trust launching new ranges of power banks aimed at gamers, here is what to look for before you buy.

## Capacity: what the mAh figure really means

Capacity is measured in milliamp-hours (mAh). But not all of it reaches your device: some energy is lost in conversion, so a power bank typically delivers around 60–70% of its rated capacity.

- **5,000 mAh:** a top-up for a phone or a partial charge for a console. Small enough for a pocket.
- **10,000 mAh:** around one full charge for most handheld consoles, or two for a phone.
- **20,000 mAh:** several charges, but heavier and bulkier.

## Charging power

Power is measured in watts. For most handheld consoles and phones, **20 W** via USB-C Power Delivery is a good minimum. Some PC-style handhelds need considerably more power to charge while being played, so check your console's requirements.

## Ports

- **USB-C** is now the standard for consoles and phones.
- Extra **USB-A** ports are useful for older devices and accessories.
- Check whether the power is shared when several devices charge at once.

## Practical features

- **A display** showing the exact percentage remaining.
- **Built-in cables** or plugs, so you carry less.
- **Size and weight**: a slim design fits more easily in a console case.

## An example: Trust's summer range

Trust's new line illustrates the options. The Pact Pocket (5,000 mAh, €19.99) is pocket-sized, with a built-in cable and 20 W charging. The Avala range (10,000 and 20,000 mAh, €29.99 and €39.99) offers one USB-C and two USB-A ports. The Fiera range, at the same prices, has a thinner, phone-like profile.

## Flying with a power bank

Airlines generally allow power banks under 100 Wh (roughly 27,000 mAh) in hand luggage only — never in checked bags. Some airlines now restrict using or charging them during the flight, so check the rules before you travel.

## Summer care

Heat shortens battery life and can be dangerous. Don't leave power banks in direct sunlight, on the beach or in a hot car, and stop using any that swell, get unusually hot or are damaged.
