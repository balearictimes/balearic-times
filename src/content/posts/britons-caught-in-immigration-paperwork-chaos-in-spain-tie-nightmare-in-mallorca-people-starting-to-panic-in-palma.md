Foreign residents in Mallorca, including many Britons, are reporting long delays in obtaining their residence cards, as the immigration offices struggle with a surge in demand.

## Why the TIE matters

The Tarjeta de Identidad de Extranjero (TIE) is the identity card for non-EU nationals living in Spain, including British residents covered by the Brexit Withdrawal Agreement. It is needed for many everyday procedures, from banking to renting a home, and is used to prove legal residence when travelling.

## The appointment problem

Many people who have already applied for a new or renewed TIE are stuck at the last step: getting an appointment to have their fingerprints taken and collect the card.

- Applicants say they check the online appointment system daily for weeks without finding a slot.
- Even lawyers say they cannot secure appointments for their clients.

## Why now

The pressure has increased because of Spain's extraordinary regularisation programme for undocumented migrants, which has generated hundreds of thousands of applications. According to official figures, around 550,000 applications had been registered by 21 May, but only a small proportion had moved on to processing. The deadline for applications is 30 June.

Even after an application is approved, obtaining a Social Security number and the physical card can take weeks or months because of the shortage of appointments.

## Voices

Lawyer Vicente Marín says that some cases are moving within a reasonable time, but that the overall system feels chaotic. Vicky Columba, of the Regularización Ya platform, says many applicants whose cases have been approved still cannot work because they lack the card and Social Security registration. Bea Cantero, president of the association Mujeres Pa'lante, describes each stalled case as a life on hold.

## The government's response

The Ministry of Inclusion has urged applicants to stay calm and submit their papers before the deadline, saying errors can be corrected later. The Interior Ministry says police are working extra hours and acting against the illegal resale of appointments.

## Practical advice

- Keep your application receipt and any emails as proof that your application is in progress.
- Keep your expired card and passport together with the receipt.
- Check the appointment system at different times of day, including early in the morning.
- Before travelling abroad, check the current rules for re-entering Spain with an application receipt.
- Seek help from a lawyer, a recognised adviser or the British consulate if you are unsure of your status.
