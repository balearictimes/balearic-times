Mallorca has several very different night-time scenes. There are lively party resorts, a cosmopolitan city centre and small harbour towns where the evening is about dinner and a drink on a terrace. Knowing which one suits you matters more than the name of any one bar.

## Palma

Palma's old town has the widest choice. Cocktail bars, wine bars and small music venues sit among the narrow streets, and the district of Santa Catalina is known for its restaurants and relaxed bars. Paseo Marítimo, the seafront road, has larger clubs and late-night venues. The city is more varied than the big resorts and attracts residents as well as visitors.

## Magaluf

Magaluf, in the municipality of Calvià, is the island's best-known party resort for British visitors. The main strip is lined with bars and clubs, and it is busy from May to October. The local authorities have introduced strict rules in recent years. They include limits on drinking in the street, restrictions on happy hours and on bar crawls, and tougher penalties for behaviour such as balconing. Ignoring them can lead to fines.

## Playa de Palma

Playa de Palma, east of the city, is home to the beer halls and clubs that attract many German visitors. It has a long beach and a mix of hotels, and it is subject to similar rules on alcohol sales and promotions. The area is lively, but the atmosphere is different from Magaluf.

## Cala Ratjada and the east

Cala Ratjada, in the north-east, has a smaller party scene with bars and clubs, mainly in summer. It is popular with younger visitors from Germany and other countries. It is calmer than the big resorts and suits groups who want a night out without the biggest crowds.

## Quieter evenings

- **Alcúdia and Port de Pollença**: terrace dinners, ice cream and a walk along the seafront.
- **Sóller and Deià**: small restaurants and music in the mountains.
- **Santanyí and Cala d'Or**: restaurants and a calmer scene in the south-east.

## Rules to know

Drinking in the street is banned in several tourist zones, and there are limits on the sale of alcohol late at night. Some all-inclusive hotels in the party areas cap the number of drinks per day. The rules and the fines have changed repeatedly, so check the local guidance and your hotel's policy.

## Staying safe

- Agree a meeting point with friends, and keep your phone charged.
- Use licensed taxis, which display a licence number, rather than unmarked cars.
- Watch your drinks and your belongings in crowded bars.
- Do not jump between balconies or into the pool from a height. Falls are a leading cause of serious injury among visitors.
- Know the emergency number, 112, which works across the European Union.

## Palma neighbourhood by neighbourhood

Palma's nightlife is spread over several districts. Santa Catalina, west of the old town, is lined with small restaurants, wine bars and cafés, and it is the neighbourhood that locals prefer for a relaxed evening. Plaça de Santa Catalina Thomàs is the heart of it. The old town, around Plaça Major and Carrer de la Unió, has cocktail bars and music venues, and the streets around Plaça Drassana are known for more traditional bars.

Paseo Marítimo, along the bay, is where the larger clubs and late-night bars gather. Tito's, a club near the seafront that opened in the 1960s, is among the best known. The marina area, Portixol, has a quieter set of bars and restaurants by the sea.

## Magaluf and Punta Ballena

Magaluf's main street, Punta Ballena, is lined with bars and clubs, and it fills from late evening. The resort has its own beach clubs and a few large venues such as BCM Planet Dance. Local police patrol the strip, and the authorities have tightened the rules on street drinking, group promotions and bar crawls. The streets behind the strip hold family hotels, so noise rules apply late at night.

## Playa de Palma and S'Arenal

Playa de Palma, between Can Pastilla and S'Arenal, is known for its German-style bars and for Megapark, an open-air venue near balneario 6. The area is also known for the Ballermann name, which German visitors use for the bars on the beach. The streets near balneario 6 and 15 host most of the beer halls and clubs. Local rules on alcohol and happy hours apply in the main tourist zones.

## Cala Ratjada and the north-east

Cala Ratjada is a harbour town in the north-east. Its summer scene is centred on the streets behind the harbour, with bars and small clubs that attract younger visitors from Germany and the UK. The nearby beaches of Cala Agulla and Cala Gat make a good morning-after destination.

## Live music and culture

Palma's Auditorium on the seafront and the Teatre Principal host concerts and theatre. The Castell de Bellver, a round castle on a hill above the city, hosts summer concerts under the stars. Valldemossa, Pollença and Deià hold classical music festivals in the summer, and the Pollença Festival is one of the most established. Small jazz clubs operate in Palma's old town all year.

## Sunset and rooftop bars

Palma has a number of rooftop bars on hotels and terraces, and these are popular at sunset. The cathedral, La Seu, lights up at night and is visible from the Parc de la Mar, a public park by the water. In summer the harbour fills with yachts, and the marina bars are busy.

## When things happen

Bars usually start to fill from ten at night, and clubs in the resorts are busiest between midnight and four. In Palma, many bars close by two or three, and clubs stay open later. In winter, only a handful of places stay open in the resorts, and Palma remains the main centre of nightlife all year.

## Transport home

Taxis operate from ranks in Palma and the resorts. The night bus network is limited, so check the last departures. In Magaluf and Playa de Palma, taxi queues form after closing time, and ride apps are an alternative.

## Rules and safety

Street drinking is banned in the main tourist areas, and the authorities have restricted alcohol sales at night. Do not buy from street sellers, which can lead to fines. If you have a problem, the Policia Local and the Guardia Civil are the police forces, and the number for emergencies is 112.
