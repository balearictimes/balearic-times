Cala Deià is one of the most photographed coves in Mallorca: a small pebble beach at the bottom of a steep valley on the Tramuntana coast, framed by pine trees, cliffs and old fishermen's huts. Getting there takes some effort, and having it to yourself is now almost impossible — but it remains spectacular.

## Getting there

The cove lies below the village of Deià. The road down is narrow and winding, and in summer the small car park fills very early. Unless you arrive first thing in the morning, expect to wait or to drive back up the hill. Many visitors prefer to leave the car in the village and walk down along the footpath, which takes around half an hour.

## At the beach

The reward is some of the clearest water on the island, ideal for swimming and snorkelling. The rocky shoreline creates small corners to explore, and despite the crowds the cove keeps a slightly wild feel. There are no sunbeds or sand to speak of, so bring water shoes and something comfortable to sit on.

## Ca's Patró March

Above the water sits Ca's Patró March, a seafood restaurant whose terraces hang over the rocks. It gained international fame after appearing in the BBC series *The Night Manager*, and is now one of the most sought-after tables on the island. Booking well in advance is essential in summer; turning up and hoping for a table rarely works. Expect fresh fish and seafood at prices that reflect the location.

## Worth it?

Cala Deià is no longer the hidden secret it once was, precisely because word has spread about its scenery and its water. Go early or late in the day, out of peak season if you can, and treat the cove with care: it is a small, fragile place that receives many more visitors than it was ever meant to.
