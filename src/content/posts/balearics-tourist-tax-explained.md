Visitors staying in the Balearic Islands pay a small tax on top of their accommodation bill. It is officially called the sustainable tourism tax, or ITS, and is often described as the ecotax. The money goes towards environmental and tourism-related projects on the islands. It applies on Mallorca, Menorca, Ibiza and Formentera.

## How it works

The tax is charged per person, per night, and it is collected by the hotel or host, who passes it on to the regional government. It is added to your bill, and VAT is applied to it. Tour operators sometimes include it in the price of a package, and sometimes ask you to pay it at the hotel.

You do not pay it at the airport, and it is not charged on activities, tours or attraction tickets.

## The rates

Rates depend on the type of accommodation and the season. For 2026, the high season runs from 1 May to 31 October and the low season from 1 November to 30 April. As a guide, the figures per person per night before VAT were as follows:

| Type of accommodation | High season | Low season |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 5-star, luxury and superior 4-star | €4 | €1 |
| 4-star and superior 3-star | €3 | €0.75 |
| 1, 2 and 3-star hotels | €2 | €0.50 |
| Holiday rentals | €2 | €0.50 |
| Hostels, guesthouses and campsites | €1 | €0.25 |
| Cruise passengers | €2 | €0.50 |

Tourist apartments pay at rates that depend on their category. These figures can change, so check with your accommodation provider or the Balearic government for the current rates.

## Who is exempt

Children under 16 do not pay the tax. Other reductions apply to longer stays. From the ninth consecutive night in the same accommodation, the rate is cut by half.

## A worked example

A couple staying in a 4-star hotel for seven nights in August would pay about €42 before VAT, which is two people for seven nights at €3. A family of four staying in a holiday rental for a week, with two children under 16, would pay about €28 before VAT.

## Cruise passengers

Visitors arriving on cruise ships have been subject to the tax as well, at rates that vary by the type of stay and by the rules at the time. The regional government has said it wants to link the charge to the pressure that cruise traffic puts on the islands.

## Why it exists

The islands receive many millions of visitors each year, and the tax is meant to help pay for the cost of this. The revenue is used for projects such as protecting natural areas, improving public transport, restoring heritage and managing water. Supporters say it spreads the cost of tourism more fairly. Critics say the amounts are too low to change behaviour, or that more of it should go to residents.

## What to do

- Ask your hotel or host whether the tax is included in your quoted price.
- Keep your receipts, as the tax should appear as a separate line.
- If you are booking through a tour operator, check whether the tax is paid in advance or at the property.
- Be aware that illegal holiday rentals may not collect it, and that using them can leave you without protection if something goes wrong.

## The law behind it

The sustainable tourism tax was introduced in 2016 under Balearic law, after an earlier version from the early 2000s was repealed. The Balearic government, the Govern, collects the revenue and spends it through a dedicated fund for sustainable tourism. The tax applies to hotels, tourist apartments, holiday rentals, campsites and similar accommodation licensed in the islands. The Agència Tributària de les Illes Balears manages collection and publishes the rules in Catalan and Spanish.

Before the tax was reintroduced in 2016, the islands had tried a different version in 2002 and 2003, which was dropped after a change of government. The current system has been adjusted several times, with new rates, a longer high season and higher charges for cruise passengers.

## How it is applied across the four islands

The same tax applies on Mallorca, Menorca, Ibiza and Formentera. There are no separate island rates. The tax is collected at the place where you sleep, so you pay it on the days you stay in a licensed property in any of the islands. If you stay a week in Mallorca and a week in Menorca, you pay the tax in each place on the nights you spend there.

## Common questions

**Do I pay on the day I arrive and the day I leave?** The tax is charged per night, so the night you arrive counts and the day you leave does not.

**Is it charged for children?** Children under 16 are exempt, but the host may ask for their age.

**What if I rent a villa?** Registered holiday rentals pay the same tax, collected by the owner or the manager. Ask for a receipt that lists the amount.

**Do I pay if I stay with friends?** The tax applies to commercial accommodation, not to stays in private homes with family or friends.

**Can I pay by card?** Yes, in most places the tax is added to the final bill and can be paid by card.

## What the money funds

The Balearic government says revenue is spent on environmental protection, restoring heritage and improving tourist areas. Past spending has included the purchase of natural land, the restoration of old farm buildings, help for water supply and sewage treatment, and training programmes. The government publishes lists of funded projects on its websites, and the amounts are discussed each year in the regional parliament.

## Criticism and debate

Business groups, including hotel federations, have argued that raising the tax could make the islands less competitive with other destinations. Residents' associations and environmental groups, such as GOB, have argued that the amounts are too small to deal with the effects of tourism, and that the money should go more directly to housing and public services. The debate over how much tourists should pay, and how the funds should be used, continues in the Balearic parliament.

## If you disagree with a charge

If your host charges you more than the legal rate, ask for the calculation. If a rental firm does not provide a receipt, you can ask the Balearic tax authority for guidance. Consumer organisations also help visitors with disputes.

## How it compares

Several other parts of Spain charge a tourist tax, including Catalonia and a growing number of cities. Rates vary widely. The Balearic rate is moderate compared with Barcelona, though it has risen over time. Many European cities, from Amsterdam to Venice, also charge visitors for overnight stays or day entry.

## Official information

The Balearic government's tax portal, at caib.es, explains the tax and the current rates. The Agència Tributària de les Illes Balears, ATIB, handles collection. Check these sources, since the details change.
