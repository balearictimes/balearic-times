Tens of thousands of people who live in the Balearic Islands are not Spanish citizens, and many ask whether they can vote. The short answer is that it depends on the type of election and on your nationality. This guide sets out the rules in the national electoral law and the agreements that adjust them. It was last checked on 29 October 2026. Rules on registration are applied by the Instituto Nacional de Estadística (INE) and the town halls, so you should confirm the current procedure on their websites before you act.

## The basic rule

The electoral law, the Ley Orgánica del Régimen Electoral General (LOREG), gives the right to vote in general elections to Spanish nationals who are of age. That means a foreign resident cannot vote for the Congreso or the Senado. The same applies to regional elections for the Parlament de les Illes Balears, where the right to vote belongs to Spanish nationals who are registered as residents in the islands.

The exceptions apply to local and European elections.

## Municipal elections

Citizens of other European Union countries who live in Spain can vote in municipal elections if they are registered on the electoral roll. They can also stand as candidates for a town council. These rights follow from EU law and the electoral law.

Citizens of some non-EU countries can also vote in municipal elections where Spain has a reciprocity treaty with their country. The United Kingdom is the case that matters most in the Balearics. After Brexit, Spain and the UK signed an agreement that keeps reciprocal voting rights in municipal elections for each other's nationals who live in the other country. It was published in the Boletín Oficial del Estado in 2019, and the government's La Moncloa website announced it. The conditions for British nationals, including any minimum period of legal residence and the process to apply, are set by the agreement and applied by the INE. The government page does not set out the registration steps, so check the INE's information for foreign voters or ask at your town hall before the next election.

## European Parliament elections

EU citizens living in Spain can vote for Spanish candidates in European Parliament elections, or they can vote for the candidates of their own country instead, but not both. They must choose which one in advance when they register. British citizens no longer vote in European Parliament elections, since the UK left the EU.

## Registering to vote

Foreign residents do not become voters automatically. To vote in a municipal election you must be on the electoral roll, which is a separate list from the padrón (the municipal register of residents). You must first be registered in the padrón of your town hall. Then you apply to be included in the electoral roll through the INE's electoral census office, using a form that the INE publishes for each election, and the application has to be made before the deadline that the INE announces. Once you are on the roll, you generally stay on it unless you move to another municipality or leave Spain.

The INE sends registered voters a card with the address of their polling station. If you have not received one a few weeks before the vote, check the roll online or at your town hall. The roll is published for consultation for a short period before each election, and any claims about errors have to be made in that window.

## How to vote in a municipal election

Voting works the same way for everybody. In the Balearics, voters receive separate ballots for the Parlament, the island council and the town hall on the day when regional and municipal elections coincide. A foreign voter who is registered to vote only in municipal elections can use only the town hall ballot. You show an identity document at the polling station. For foreign citizens this is normally a passport, a residence card or the Spanish identity document for foreigners (the TIE). The doors open at 09:00 and close at 20:00.

You can also vote by post in municipal elections if you will be away, using the procedure published by Correos and the INE. Requests must be made within the deadline set for each election.

## When you become a Spanish citizen

If you obtain Spanish nationality, you have the right to vote in all elections. You will need to be registered on the electoral roll, and for Spanish citizens that normally happens automatically, because the roll is updated from the civil registry and the padrón. A Spanish citizen may also keep another nationality in some cases, depending on the other country's laws and the agreements Spain has with it. The Ministry of Justice and the INE publish the procedures.

## The next elections

The next vote is the general election on 29 November 2026. Foreign residents cannot vote in it. The next local elections are due on 23 May 2027, the fourth Sunday of May, and the deadline to register for those will be announced by the INE. If you want to vote in them, the practical steps are to:

- Make sure you are on the padrón in the municipality where you live.
- Check the INE's information on how foreign nationals apply to the electoral roll.
- Apply as soon as the INE opens the window.
- Check that your name appears when the roll is published.

## Why it matters

Town halls decide many of the things that affect expatriates directly, including property tax, planning rules, licences for rental properties and rules about noise and public spaces. A vote in a municipal election is the main way a foreign resident can influence these. Because registration is not automatic, foreign residents who want to vote need to act before the deadline rather than wait for a card to arrive.
