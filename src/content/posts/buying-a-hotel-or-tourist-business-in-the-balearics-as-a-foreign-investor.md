Foreign investors have long been active in the Balearic hotel and tourist sector. Buying a hotel, an apartment complex, a restaurant or a licensed holiday let in the islands is legal for foreigners, but it involves more than a purchase contract. The most important question since 2025 is whether the tourist places that come with a property can be kept, moved or expanded. This article lists the main points to check, using the official sources for the regional decree-law, the national registry of rentals, the company law guidance and the tax authorities. It is general information, not legal or investment advice, and a buyer should always use a Spanish lawyer, notary and tax adviser. It was last checked on 29 October 2026.

## Identity and set-up

A foreign buyer needs a NIE, the foreigner identification number, to sign deeds, open bank accounts and pay taxes. Many investors buy through a Spanish company, often an SL, which can hold the property and the licences. The government's guide to forming an SL explains that foreign partners need a NIE, that non-resident capital partners need a NIE for economic purposes and that a minimum of one partner is required. A separate article in this series covers the steps. A foreign investor may also have to report the investment to the Spanish authorities for statistical purposes. The notary and the adviser can say which forms apply to the transaction.

## Asset purchase or share purchase

A buyer can purchase the property and the business assets directly, or buy the shares in the company that owns them. An asset purchase gives a clean start but requires a new transfer of licences and contracts. A share purchase keeps the company and its history, including its liabilities. The choice has tax consequences, and due diligence should cover both options.

## The key question: tourist places

The regional decree-law 4/2025, approved on 11 April 2025, changed the rules on tourist places. According to the Govern's summary:

- New tourist places cannot be created in multi-family buildings.
- Places can only be exchanged one for one, under a global ceiling, and a transitional bank of places was created that does not generate new ones.
- The island councils are to set island ceilings and define the carrying capacity of each island.
- Obsolete hotels may be converted into affordable housing.
- Fines were raised by 25 per cent, with serious fines reaching 50,000 euros and very serious fines reaching 500,000 euros.
- Booking platforms share responsibility for illegal listings.

For a buyer, this means that the number of licensed places attached to a property is a core part of its value. A buyer should ask for the tourist licence, the entry in the tourist register, the number of places and proof that they are legal. A lawyer should check that no sanction proceedings are open and that the rules of the island council in question allow the planned use.

## Planning and licences

Besides the tourist licence, a hotel needs planning permission, an activity licence from the town hall and compliance with building, fire and accessibility regulations. If the plan is to renovate or extend, the town hall's planning rules and the island territorial plan decide what is allowed. Ask for the file of the building licence and any technical inspection reports. If the property is in a protected area, further limits apply.

## Holiday lets

A buyer of a licensed holiday let must check the national registration number as well as the regional licence. The national registry for rentals, created by Real Decreto 1312/2024, requires each unit to show a registration number in advertisements. Platforms must remove listings linked to suspended numbers within 48 hours of an administrative order. In apartment blocks, the horizontal property law reform of 2025 requires approval by three fifths of the owners to start a new tourist flat. A buyer should review the building's statutes and minutes.

## Taxes on acquisition and operation

Buying a hotel or tourist property involves transfer tax or VAT, stamp duty, notary and registry fees, and later the annual property tax. The regional tax agency ATIB publishes the Balearic rates. Operating the business involves VAT on services, corporate tax or income tax on profits, local taxes and the sustainable tourism tax, which is collected from guests and passed to ATIB. Corporate tax in 2026 is 25 per cent in general. New companies may pay 15 per cent under conditions, and small companies with turnover below one million euros pay 19 per cent on the first 50,000 euros of profit and 21 per cent on the rest, according to the Agencia Tributaria.

## Employees

Buying a going concern often involves taking over staff. The buyer has to follow labour law on transfers of undertakings, and the collective agreement for hospitality applies. The article on hiring explains the employer registration steps.

## Due diligence checklist

- Title to the property in the land registry, with no unexpected charges.
- Tourist licence and number of places.
- Planning licence and activity licence.
- Fire, safety and accessibility certificates.
- Tax and Social Security compliance certificates.
- Employment contracts and the collective agreement.
- Pending sanctions or complaints.
- Insurance policies and claims history.
- Environmental and water supply issues.

## Risks to weigh

The regulations on tourist places are recent, the islands are about to hold regional elections on 23 May 2027, and a general election takes place on 29 November 2026. Rules could change again. Island councils are still setting their ceilings. A cautious buyer builds this uncertainty into the price and the timetable.

## Questions people ask

**Can a foreigner own a hotel in the Balearics?** Yes, directly or through a Spanish company, with a NIE and the usual registrations.

**Do tourist places transfer with the property?** That is exactly what a lawyer must check. The 2025 decree-law limits creating new places and allows exchanges one for one, so the licence and the number of places are central to value.

**How long does a purchase take?** The official sources do not give a standard time. It depends on due diligence, the licences and financing.
