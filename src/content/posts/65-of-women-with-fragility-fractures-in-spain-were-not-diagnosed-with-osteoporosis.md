Around two in three women in Spain who have suffered a fragility fracture — a bone broken by a fall from standing height or less — have never been diagnosed with osteoporosis, according to a survey by the Hispanic Foundation for Osteoporosis and Metabolic Bone Diseases (FHOEMO).

## The survey

The survey of 934 people, mostly women with an average age of around 62, found that 64.5% of women with a fragility fracture had never been told they had osteoporosis.

Osteoporosis affects around 22% of women and 6.8% of men over 50 in Spain.

## A silent disease

Osteoporosis is often called a silent disease because it causes no symptoms until a bone breaks. Fracture pain may be mistaken for arthritis or other problems, delaying diagnosis.

## Information gaps

The survey also revealed problems in communication:

- more than half of patients said their doctor had never explained how to prevent the disease;
- 72% left appointments with unanswered questions;
- after diagnosis, 45% were not told about available treatments, and 56% did not know their benefits and risks.

Many people also confuse osteoporosis with osteopenia, a milder loss of bone density that can progress to osteoporosis if not addressed.

## The impact

Almost half of those who had suffered a fracture reported limitations in their daily activities. Loss of mobility can lead to insecurity and even depression, making it harder to stay active.

## Women most affected

- One in four women over 50 has osteoporosis.
- Four out of five osteoporosis-related fractures occur in women.
- Each year, around 330,000 women in Spain suffer a fragility fracture.

## What helps

**Prevention.** Weight-bearing exercise, particularly during childhood and adolescence when bone mass is built, together with a diet rich in calcium and protein, adequate vitamin D and avoiding smoking and excess alcohol.

**Screening.** Women with risk factors, particularly after the menopause, should ask their doctor about a bone density test before a fracture occurs.

**Fracture liaison units.** Spain now has 85 hospital units dedicated to coordinating care after a fragility fracture. They help most patients stay on treatment and could prevent more than 1,200 further fractures a year.

Anyone who has broken a bone in a minor fall after the age of 50 should ask their doctor whether they should be assessed for osteoporosis.
