Menorcan football is mourning Juan Francisco Mora Guardia, known to everyone as "Cholo", a man whose family history was bound up with the game on the island for three generations.

## A football family

Cholo's grandfather, Juan Mora Terrés, was the first president of the club in Maó, and his father, Paco Mora, later worked in its management. Growing up with the club as part of family life, he was drawn to football from an early age.

## Player and director

He came through the club's youth teams and went on to play for its amateur side. At the age of 27 he joined the board of directors of the Maó club, where he remained until 1993.

His years on the board were a time of significant change for football in the town, including the merger that gave rise to Sporting Mahonés. After stepping down, he remained a regular in the stands, following the team he had helped to shape.

## A familiar face

Beyond his formal roles, Cholo was known for his loyalty to the club and its people. He went to matches, helped out at club events and was always ready with encouragement for younger players.

## Tributes

News of his death prompted messages of condolence from the club, former team-mates and supporters. The club said he had helped to build the institution and that he leaves a mark that will remain in every corner of it. Many fans shared photographs of him at matches and memories of his warmth.

## A different kind of legacy

Cholo never needed trophies to earn his place in the history of Menorcan football. His contribution was the decades of commitment that keep a small island club alive — on the pitch, in the boardroom and in the stands — and that is how he will be remembered in Maó.
