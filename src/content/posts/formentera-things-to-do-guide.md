Formentera is small enough that you can see most of it in a few days. Beyond the beaches, the island offers lighthouses, salt flats, watchtowers and a slow pace that many visitors find restful.

## La Mola

La Mola is a plateau at the eastern end of the island, and it is the highest point on Formentera, at around 190 metres. The lighthouse there, built in the 19th century, stands on the cliff edge, and the view looks straight over the sea. A monument nearby is dedicated to Jules Verne, who mentioned the lighthouse in one of his novels. The village of Es Pilar de la Mola holds the market and some small restaurants.

## Cap de Barbaria

Cap de Barbaria, on the south-west tip, has a lighthouse at the edge of a flat, dry plain. The landscape is stark and open, and the sunsets are popular with visitors. The road is bumpy in places, so go carefully on a scooter. There is little shade or water.

## Ses Salines Natural Park

The salt flats in the north of the island are part of Ses Salines d'Eivissa i Formentera Natural Park. They are home to flamingos and other wading birds, especially in the cooler months, and a footpath and viewing points let you see them without disturbing the wildlife. Bring binoculars if you have them.

## Sant Francesc Xavier

Sant Francesc is the island's small capital. It has a whitewashed fortified church, shops and cafés around a central square, and a handful of galleries. A walk around the village is enough to get the feel of the place.

## Cycling and walking routes

Marked cycle routes link the main points, and several walking paths follow the coast. A trail goes along the cliffs near La Mola, and another crosses the salt flats. Spring and autumn are the best seasons for walking.

## Sunset spots

Formentera faces the setting sun on its west side, and several places draw crowds in the evening. Cala Saona and Cap de Barbaria are popular, as are some terraces near Es Caló. Arrive well ahead of sunset, since places fill quickly in summer.

## Boat trips

Boat trips run from La Savina and Ibiza to S'Espalmador and the nearby coves. Prices differ with the size of the boat and the length of the trip. Sailing or kayaking in the calm bays is also an option in good weather.

## Diving and snorkelling

The clear water and sea-grass meadows make for good snorkelling, and there are dive centres on the island. Beginners can take introductory courses. Respect the rules about not touching the seagrass.

## Rainy day ideas

The island has a small museum in Sant Francesc, and the salt pans have information panels. If the weather turns, many cafés offer a long lunch and a view of the grey sea.

## The lighthouses and the cliffs

The Far de la Mola was built in 1861 at the eastern end of the island, and it stands at 120 metres above the sea. A monument near the lighthouse recalls Jules Verne, who mentions La Mola in one of his novels. The plateau has a village, Es Pilar de la Mola, with a small church, craft shops and a market. In the 1960s and 1970s, the village became a refuge for hippies and artists, and the legacy is still visible in the craft stalls and the relaxed atmosphere.

At the opposite end of the island, the Far de Barbaria, built in 1972, stands on a flat limestone cape. The lighthouse is a short walk from the car park, and the landscape of low scrub and sea cliffs is popular at sunset. The area also has a coastal path and a watchtower, Torre des Garroveret.

## Towns and history

Sant Francesc Xavier has a white fortified church, built in the 18th century, which was designed to protect residents from pirates. The square outside is the centre of village life, with cafés and shops. The Museu Etnològic de Formentera, close by, shows how people lived on the island. Ca na Costa, a Neolithic burial chamber between La Savina and Sant Francesc, is one of the oldest monuments on the island.

## Birdlife and salt

Ses Salines Natural Park covers the salt pans and lagoons, and it is home to flamingos, avocets and herons. The Estany Pudent and Estany des Peix lagoons, in the north, are important for migrating birds. A lookout and a path give views of the water. The salt flats, which once supplied salt to the Mediterranean, still have old storage buildings and a small salt production area.

## Cycling routes

The island has marked routes for cyclists. One goes from La Savina to Ses Illetes and the northern tip, another from Sant Francesc to Cala Saona, and another along the coast to Es Caló. The route to La Mola includes the island's only real climb. Local hire shops provide maps and recommend routes, and e-bikes are available for visitors who want help with the climb.

## Walking routes

The island has a network of marked walking routes covering coastal paths, farmland and the plateau, with signposts and information boards along the way. Allow time and carry water, since there is little shade.

## Boat and sea activities

Boat trips run from La Savina to S'Espalmador and around the coast. Sailing, kayaking and paddleboarding are available at Es Pujols and Migjorn. Diving centres offer courses and guided dives, and the seagrass meadows are among the healthiest in the Mediterranean. Freshwater is scarce, so respect the island's rules on water use.

## Sunsets

The best sunsets are seen from Cap de Barbaria, Cala Saona and the western edge of the Es Cap area. Arrive early and bring a jacket, since the wind can pick up after dark.

## Rainy day ideas

Visit the ethnological museum in Sant Francesc, browse the shops in Es Pilar de la Mola, or take a long lunch at a village restaurant. Check opening hours, since small museums close in the afternoon.

## Practical tips

- Wear sunscreen and a hat, since there is little shade.
- Take water on bike rides.
- Respect protected areas and keep to paths.
- Avoid the midday heat in summer.
