Epic Games has unveiled Unreal Engine 6 at its State of Unreal 2026 event, setting out a plan that brings together its game engine and Fortnite's creation tools into a single platform.

## One engine for everything

Marcus Wassmer, who leads the UE6 project, said the new engine will merge two separate development streams — Unreal Engine 5, used by studios around the world, and Unreal Editor for Fortnite, used by creators to build experiences inside Fortnite — into a single product over the next two years.

UE6 will keep the qualities developers value in Unreal: high-quality rendering, faster processing, quicker iteration and better performance on mobile. But Epic says it will be much more than an incremental update.

## Three pillars

**1. Verse.** UE6 will adopt Verse, the programming language Epic created for Fortnite, as a core language. Epic says it is designed to scale from a solo indie project to huge teams.

**2. Built-in AI.** Artificial intelligence will be integrated into the engine's workflows, with connections to external AI services, to help creators generate assets, test gameplay and automate repetitive tasks.

**3. Open economies.** Epic wants content to move more freely between games using open standards, so that items such as cosmetics and skins — and even player accounts — could work across different titles built on UE6.

## Universal skins?

Since Fortnite will also run on UE6, Epic's own games, such as Fortnite and Rocket League, could become test beds for sharing items between titles — the idea of a skin you own working in multiple games.

## Timeline

Developers will get early access at the end of 2027. The preview phase is expected to last at least 12 to 18 months, with regular free updates.

## A smooth transition

Epic insists that existing projects will not be broken. Early versions of UE6 will keep familiar tools such as Actors and Blueprints, with conversion tools added later.

## A risky bet

Merging a professional engine with a consumer platform is ambitious, and some developers are wary of becoming more dependent on Fortnite's ecosystem. But if Epic pulls it off, UE6 could change how games are built — and how the items players buy are used across different worlds.
