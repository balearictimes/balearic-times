Anyone planning to move to the Balearic Islands, or already living there on local pay, asks the same question: how far does a salary go? Official statistics answer part of that question. They cover pay, minimum wages, taxes and the opinions of residents, but they do not publish a single "cost of living" figure for the islands that could be compared with, say, the United Kingdom. This article sets out what the official sources do say and where readers should look for the latest numbers. It deliberately avoids price estimates taken from property websites, travel blogs or news reports. It was last checked on 29 October 2026.

## Pay and labour cost

The Consell Econòmic i Social (CES) reports that the average labour cost per worker in the Balearics was 2,254.9 euros per month in 2024. That is 2.0 per cent below the national average, and 4.8 per cent above the 2023 figure. Labour cost includes wages plus the employer's social security contributions, so the take-home pay is lower.

Nominal GDP per person in the islands was 34,381 euros in 2024, up 10.9 per cent, according to the same report. GDP per person measures output divided by population. It is not an average income, and it includes income that goes to companies and to people who do not live in the islands.

## The minimum wage

The national minimum wage for 2026 was set by Real Decreto 126/2026 at 40.70 euros per day or 1,221 euros per month, in 14 payments, which makes 17,094 euros a year. It applies throughout Spain, including the Balearics, where prices are higher than in many other regions. Some collective agreements in hospitality and other sectors set pay scales above the minimum for certain categories.

## Residents say costs are a problem

The AETIB's 2024 residents' survey found that 77.2 per cent saw a major impact of tourism on housing costs, and 55.3 per cent saw one on the cost of goods and services. These are opinions, not price statistics, but they show how people in the islands perceive their budgets. The survey is explained in another article.

## What tends to cost more on islands

The Spanish Constitution and the Statute of Autonomy recognise insularity as a factor, and the Statute requires that it should not lead to economic imbalances. In practice, island households face extra costs for goods that must be shipped in, for travel to the mainland and for energy. Air and sea fares for residents of the islands are subsidised by the state, and Balearic politicians have called for more help. Details of the resident discount are published by the Ministry of Transport.

## Taxes shape the budget

Income tax, VAT and local taxes affect the cost of living. The state income tax scale starts at 9.5 per cent and rises to 24.5 per cent for the highest incomes, and each community adds its own scale. VAT is 21 per cent on most goods and 10 per cent or 4 per cent on some. The article on taxes explains these in more detail.

## Healthcare and schooling

Public healthcare is free at the point of use for people covered by the national system, which includes residents who are insured under Social Security. Those not covered can buy into the public system through the special agreement, at 60 euros a month for those under 65 and 157 euros a month for those aged 65 and over, according to IB-Salut. Public education is free, and the regional government sets the rules on school places and fees. A separate article covers healthcare.

## Where to find official price data

The statistics institute, INE, publishes the Consumer Price Index each month, with a breakdown by region and by group of goods and services, such as housing, food, transport and restaurants. The release for September 2026 is on the INE website, and the Balearic figures can be compared with the national average. The INE also publishes the Household Budget Survey, which shows what families spend. IBESTAT, the regional statistics office, publishes regional series on prices, wages and household income.

For a new arrival, those tables are more reliable than anecdotal lists of prices. They show how inflation changes over time and how the islands compare with other regions.

## Questions to ask before moving

- What will my take-home pay be after income tax and social security? The Agencia Tributaria has a guide to withholding.
- What will my housing cost be? Ask for contracts and check that any rental is registered. The article on housing sets out the national and regional measures.
- Will I be covered by public healthcare? See the article on IB-Salut.
- Do I need a car? Public transport is better in Palma than elsewhere.
- Do I need to pay for private school? Public schools teach in both official languages.

## What changes through the year

The cost of living is not the same in July as in January. Seasonal demand affects rents, and many workers have gaps between contracts. The article on seasonal work explains this. A budget for the islands should allow for months of lower income.

## Questions readers ask

**Is the minimum wage higher in the Balearics?** No. The national minimum applies everywhere in Spain. Collective agreements may set higher pay for specific jobs.

**Can I compare the islands with the UK?** The official sources used here do not publish a direct comparison. International bodies such as Eurostat publish price level comparisons between countries, but they do not break the figures down by Spanish region.

**Are prices different on each island?** Residents and businesses often report differences, especially on the smaller islands, but this article found no official regional price comparison to cite. The INE's price index gives the regional picture for the community as a whole.

**What should I budget for?** Housing, utilities, transport, food, health insurance if needed, taxes and a reserve for the months when work is scarce.
