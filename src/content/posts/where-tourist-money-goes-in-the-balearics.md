More than 18 million tourists visit the Balearic Islands every year, and between them they spend more than 22 billion euros. Those numbers are large enough that every household in the islands is touched by them in some way. This article follows the money using only official statistics: the Consell Econòmic i Social (CES), the Balearic tourism agency (AETIB), the regional statistics office IBESTAT and the national statistics office INE. Figures were last checked on 29 October 2026.

## The headline numbers

According to the CES report for 2024, the islands received 18.7 million tourists that year, which was 4.9 per cent more than in 2023 and 13.7 per cent above the level of 2019. Total spending by tourists was 22,381 million euros, an increase of 12.2 per cent. Average daily spending per tourist rose 7.0 per cent to 187 euros. Hotel overnight stays rose 7.1 per cent, while the average length of stay fell 2.8 per cent, which means visitors are coming for slightly shorter trips and spending more each day.

The same pattern appears in the AETIB's January 2026 summary. Tourist arrivals that month were 244,275, down 11.7 per cent, but spending was 265.1 million euros, up 0.3 per cent. Per person, visitors spent an average of 1,085.44 euros, up 13.6 per cent, or 130.31 euros per day, up 4.3 per cent. In other words, fewer people spent about the same total.

## Where visitors come from

IBESTAT's August 2026 note, which is provisional, counted 3,167,905 tourists in the month, up 2.33 per cent on August 2025. Three markets dominate:

- **United Kingdom:** 649,101 tourists, 20.49 per cent of the total, up 10.45 per cent.
- **Germany:** 639,022 tourists, 20.17 per cent, up 0.47 per cent.
- **Rest of Spain:** 571,447 tourists, 18.04 per cent, up 1.29 per cent.

Over the first eight months of 2026, the islands received 14,357,229 tourists, up 1.89 per cent on the same period a year before. British visitors rose 6.96 per cent over the period, while German visitors fell 1.05 per cent. The British market is therefore the largest single source of growth this year, according to the official count.

Taken together, these three markets accounted for nearly 59 per cent of the August total, so most of the rest comes from a long list of smaller countries, including France, Italy, the Netherlands, the Nordic countries and the United States.

## What money buys

Tourist spending in the statistics is split into several categories, including accommodation, food and drink, transport within the islands, shopping and activities. The AETIB publishes the breakdown in its survey of tourist expenditure, Egatur, which is run with the INE. The detail is on the AETIB's website, and the figures change each month. In general, accommodation and food are the largest items, but readers should check the latest release for the proportions.

Beyond the visible spending, there are indirect effects. A hotel buys food from wholesalers, pays electricity bills, hires laundry services and uses contractors. The employees who earn wages in tourism then spend on rent, shops and services in the islands. Economists call this the multiplier effect, and the size depends on how much of the supply chain is local.

## Who gets the money

Part of the spending ends up as wages. In the fourth quarter of 2025, there were 94,706 workers registered in tourism, or 19.1 per cent of all workers in the islands, working in 8,825 tourism businesses. Part goes to owners as profit, part to suppliers on the mainland and abroad, and part to the state and regional governments through taxes. VAT, corporate tax, income tax and social security contributions are all paid on the activity.

The regional government also collects a sustainable tourism tax, which guests pay per night in tourist accommodation. The rates depend on the season and the type of accommodation and are published by the Agència Tributaria de les Illes Balears. The revenue is intended for environmental and sustainability projects.

## The winter dip

The January 2026 AETIB summary shows how quiet the low season is. Open hotels had an estimated 22,909 places and an occupancy rate of 36.6 per cent. Rural tourism places numbered 2,059 and were 28.8 per cent full. A large part of the hospitality sector shuts between November and March. Many seasonal workers go home or move to find other work, which links to the issue of seasonal employment covered in another article.

## Why average spending is rising

The CES says the daily spend rose faster than visitor numbers in 2024. The figures do not by themselves explain why, but the report points to price increases in accommodation and food, as well as the arrival of visitors who spend more. Inflation also pushes up nominal spending. Anyone comparing figures over time should keep in mind that a rise in euros does not always mean a rise in real purchasing power.

## What the debate is about

The same numbers feed an argument. Supporters of the current model point to the income and jobs. Critics point to crowding, pressure on housing and strain on infrastructure, which the resident survey of 2024 shows many residents share. The regional government's policy since 2025 has been to try to contain the growth in tourist places while encouraging higher spending visitors. The effects will show up in the official statistics over the next few years.

## How to follow the data

- IBESTAT publishes monthly border tourism notes, called Frontur, with the number of tourists by country of origin.
- INE publishes Frontur and Egatur nationally, including the Balearic breakdown.
- The AETIB publishes "El turismo en datos" every month.
- The CES publishes the annual report on the economy.

## Questions people ask

**Do tourists spend more in summer than in winter?** Total spending is highest in summer because there are many more visitors. The January 2026 figures show the daily spend per person was 130.31 euros, compared with the 2024 annual average of 187 euros, but the two are measured differently, so do not compare them directly.

**Who counts as a tourist in these statistics?** The border statistics count non-resident visitors who stay overnight. Day visitors and cruise passengers are measured differently.

**Where can I find spending by category?** The AETIB publishes expenditure surveys on its tourism statistics pages.
