Technology in the Balearic Islands is not a single story. It is a set of changes that show up in different places: how people pay their taxes, how they catch a bus, how they work remotely, how hotels manage energy and how public bodies publish data. This article gives an overview, and each section points to a more detailed article in this series or to the official source. It deliberately sticks to what government bodies and public institutions have published, and it does not repeat claims from commercial press releases. It was last checked on 29 October 2026.

## Public services are moving online

Almost every dealing with the Spanish state now has an electronic route. The Agencia Tributaria, Social Security, the Registro Mercantil and the Ministry of Foreign Affairs run electronic offices. Starting a business can be done through the CIRCE system, which sends one electronic form to several offices, as the articles on forming a company and registering as self-employed explain. Many procedures require a digital certificate or the Cl@ve identification system, and the government guides recommend getting a certificate early.

The Balearic Govern and the island councils publish notices, calls for grants and the official bulletin online. Town halls run electronic offices where residents can request licences, certificates and appointments.

## Open data and city services

Palma's town hall runs a website called SmartOffice, with a section on open data, and it has published strategic plans on innovation and smart city projects. Its notices describe a free app with real-time information on public transport, parking and traffic, and a dashboard that uses data to measure the number of tourists in the city and adapt services. Palma's bus company, EMT, also has an app that shows when the next bus will arrive. The town hall's website holds the details of these projects and their dates, and readers should check them directly. This article does not report usage figures, because the ones published are from earlier years.

## Connectivity

The Ministry for Digital Transformation publishes an annual broadband coverage report. Its 2024 edition, with data to 30 June 2024, said fibre to the home reached 95 per cent of Spain, 5G reached 96 per cent of the population, and fixed gigabit-capable networks reached 94 per cent of homes. A later notice from the Ministry in May 2026 reports that 5G now reaches 99 per cent of the Spanish population. The reports let readers look up coverage by region and municipality through interactive maps, but the pages used for this article did not give Balearic figures. The article on fibre and 5G explains where to find them.

## Remote work

Spain created a visa and residence authorisation for international teleworkers in the start-up law of 2022. The Ministry of Foreign Affairs describes it as available to non-EU nationals who work remotely for companies outside Spain, with an income requirement of 200 per cent of the monthly minimum wage and either a recognised qualification or three years of experience. The islands have attracted remote workers, and the article on digital nomads sets out the rules.

## Energy

The Balearic Islands passed a climate change and energy transition law in 2019. The Govern's notices report that the islands set a record for renewable production in May of one year, that one in four kilowatts of installed renewable power is for self-consumption, and that the Balearic Energy Institute identified 794 car parks that could be covered with solar panels. The Govern also promoted a large solar canopy of 3,200 kilowatts at the ParcBIT technology park. The article on solar and renewables explains these developments.

## Tourism technology

Hotels and tourist businesses use booking platforms, digital check-in, energy management systems and data tools to manage demand. The national registry for rentals, created by Real Decreto 1312/2024, is itself a technology system: platforms must send monthly data on each unit to a central electronic counter and delete listings within 48 hours of an order. That shows how technology is now used for regulation as well as for marketing.

Tourism businesses also use artificial intelligence for customer service, pricing and planning. The official sources consulted for this article, however, do not publish Balearic statistics on AI use in hotels, so no figures are given.

## Start-ups and innovation

The national start-up law, Ley 28/2022, set up a framework for emerging companies, including the teleworker visa. The Balearic Govern has a department that covers innovation, and technology parks such as ParcBIT in Palma host companies. The article on the start-up scene describes how to find support.

## What to watch

- **Regulation of tourist rentals.** Technology allows the authorities to cross-check listings, and the registry's monthly data feeds are an example.
- **Energy.** The islands depend on imported fuel, and renewables are a policy priority.
- **Broadband.** Rural areas depend on mobile networks and fibre roll-out.
- **Data protection.** Public bodies and companies must comply with the data protection rules, which the national agency enforces.

## Questions readers ask

**Do I need a digital certificate to live in Spain?** It is not compulsory, but many procedures with the tax office, Social Security and other bodies are easier or only possible with one. The government's business guides call it optional but useful.

**Is the registry for holiday lets a privacy issue?** The decree sets out which data owners and platforms must provide, including the registration number, address and listing links. Data protection law applies to how public bodies handle it.

**Where can I see public data about the islands?** IBESTAT publishes regional statistics, the AETIB publishes tourism data and Palma's town hall runs an open data section.

**Will technology solve the housing problem?** The official sources do not claim so. Technology helps the authorities identify unregistered lets and track data, but the supply of homes depends on planning, construction and policy decisions explained in the housing article.
