Plenty of people dream of opening a beach bar, a small restaurant or a shop in the Balearic Islands. The idea is easy, and the rules are the hard part. A business that serves the public needs permission from the town hall, registrations with the tax and Social Security authorities, and in some cases approvals from regional bodies. This guide explains how the system is arranged, based on the government's guides to starting a business and the structure of local government set out in the Statute of Autonomy. It is general information, and local rules differ by municipality, so check with the town hall before signing a lease. It was last checked on 29 October 2026.

## Start with the legal form

Before applying for anything, decide whether you will operate as a self-employed individual or through a company. The article on the autónomo route and the article on setting up an SL set out each option. Your choice affects who signs the licence application and who is liable if something goes wrong.

## Check the premises first

Planning rules decide what activities are allowed in a given building or street. A shop unit may not be permitted to have a kitchen, and a bar may be restricted in areas near homes. Before you sign a lease, ask the town hall's planning department whether the premises can legally be used for your activity. If the premises previously held a different use, a change of use may be needed. Island territorial plans and municipal urban plans set the rules, and the town hall can issue a certificate of urban planning compatibility.

## The activity licence

The government's guide for individual entrepreneurs says you should apply to the town hall for the licence that fits your activity, which may be a licence of activity, a responsible declaration or a prior communication. These are different procedures:

- **Licence.** The town hall examines the project and grants permission before you open. This is typical for activities with greater impact, such as bars with music or large venues.
- **Responsible declaration.** You declare that you meet the requirements and can start, with the town hall able to check afterwards.
- **Prior communication.** You inform the town hall of the start of the activity.

Which one applies depends on the activity, the size of the premises and the municipal ordinance. The town hall's website or its business advice desk explains the options. Some home-based activities that do not involve public attendance are exempt.

## Extra permissions for food and drink

A bar or restaurant handles food, and food businesses must be registered with the health authority. The registration, health inspection requirements and staff training rules are set by national and regional food safety law. Kitchens need ventilation, grease traps and fire protection, and the town hall may require a technical project signed by an engineer or architect. Terraces on public land need separate permission from the town hall, with limits on size, hours and furniture.

Music, noise limits and opening hours are also controlled. Many Balearic towns have local rules on closing hours and noise, and in the party zones of Magaluf, Playa de Palma and the West End of Sant Antoni, regional rules restrict promotions such as open bars and happy hours, as described in the article on rules for visitors. Selling alcohol late at night in shops is also limited in those zones.

## Retail

A shop is usually simpler than a bar. It needs the right to use the premises, the town hall's licence or declaration, tax and Social Security registration and, for some products, a sector registration. Opening hours are set by regional commercial rules, and the Govern decides each year on the days when shops can open on Sundays and public holidays. Sellers of food, pharmaceuticals, tobacco and weapons have additional requirements.

## Tax and Social Security

The tax registration is the same as for any business. An individual files form 036 or 037 with the Agencia Tributaria before starting, and a company does so after incorporation. A person who employs staff registers as an employer with Social Security before hiring anyone, and the article on hiring covers the steps. The business will charge VAT on sales at the rates set by the Agencia Tributaria, which has a general rate of 21 per cent and reduced rates of 10 per cent and 4 per cent. The Agencia publishes a yearly list of which items belong in which rate, so a restaurant owner should check the list for food service.

## Costs

This article does not give price estimates. Costs vary by municipality and property, and the official sources do not publish a single figure. The main costs to budget for are:

- Rent or purchase of the premises, plus the deposit.
- The technical project and fees for the licence, set by the town hall.
- Fit-out, kitchen equipment, signage and furniture.
- Notary and registry costs if you form a company.
- Insurance, which is often required for public venues.
- Wages and social security contributions if you employ staff.

## Timing

Licences can take weeks or months depending on the town hall and the complexity of the project. Seasonal businesses often plan to open in the spring, so applications need to be submitted in winter. Delays are common when documents are incomplete, so ask for a checklist at the start.

## Who to ask

- The town hall's licensing and planning departments.
- The nearest PAE advice point, which helps with the electronic procedures.
- The local chamber of commerce.
- A gestor or architect with experience of local licences.

## Mistakes to avoid

- Signing a lease before checking the permitted use.
- Starting work before the licence is granted.
- Assuming a previous owner's licence transfers automatically. Check with the town hall.
- Ignoring noise and terrace rules.
- Underestimating the time needed for inspections.
