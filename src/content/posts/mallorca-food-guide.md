Mallorcan cooking is simple and built around what the island produces: olive oil, almonds, tomatoes, pork, fish and vegetables. Some dishes are tied to the season, and many are served in village restaurants rather than in the big resorts.

## Ensaïmada

The ensaïmada is a spiral pastry made with flour, sugar, eggs and pork lard, which gives it its soft, flaky texture. It is eaten for breakfast or with coffee in the afternoon, either plain or filled with cream, pumpkin jam or cabell d'àngel. You will see it in bakeries all over the island, and in round boxes at the airport. Plain ones taste best fresh.

## Sobrassada

Sobrassada is a cured, spreadable sausage made with pork and paprika, and it is protected by a European origin label. It is eaten on bread, often with honey, or cooked into dishes. The black Mallorcan pig is the traditional source. Look for it in markets and delicatessens.

## Pa amb oli

Pa amb oli means bread with oil. A slice of local bread is rubbed with tomato and topped with olive oil, then served with cured meats, cheese or vegetables. It is a standard lunch and is cheap, filling and found almost everywhere.

## Tumbet and other vegetable dishes

Tumbet is a baked dish of aubergine, courgette, potatoes and peppers with a tomato sauce, often served as a side or on its own. It shows how much Mallorcan cooking relies on vegetables. Coca de trampó is a flatbread topped with peppers, onions and tomatoes, and it is usually sold by the slice in bakeries.

## Meat and rice

Frit mallorquí is a fry-up of offal, potatoes and fennel that visitors tend to find strong, but locals value highly. Arròs brut, which means dirty rice, is a rich winter dish made with meat, vegetables and spices. Fish is also common on the coast, with grilled and baked dishes appearing on most menus.

## Sweet things

Gató d'ametlla is an almond cake served with almond ice cream, and it appears on menus across the island. Almonds grow widely in Mallorca, and the blossom in late winter is a well-known sight. Look for local ice cream and for cocas filled with sweet fillings at fiestas.

## Wine and drink

Mallorca's two main wine regions are Binissalem, around the town of the same name, and Pla i Llevant, in the east. Local grapes include Manto Negro and Callet. Hierbas, a herbal liqueur flavoured with anise, is the traditional end to a meal, and it comes in dry and sweet versions. Gin from Menorca is also easy to find on Mallorcan bar shelves.

## Tips for eating well

- Look for restaurants that offer a menú del día at lunch. It usually includes a starter, main course, dessert and a drink at a fair price.
- In resorts, move a few streets back from the promenade to find places with local customers.
- Markets are a good place to try local produce. Palma's Mercat de l'Olivar is the main food market, and many towns hold weekly markets on set days.
- Book ahead for popular restaurants in July and August.

## Where to find the real thing in Palma

Palma's Mercat de l'Olivar, off Plaça d'Espanya, is the city's main covered food market, with fish stalls on the ground floor and tapas counters above. The older Mercat de Santa Catalina, in the neighbourhood of the same name, is smaller and tends to be calmer. Ensaïmada is sold in bakeries across the old town, and the best known is Can Joan de s'Aigo, a café that has been serving ensaïmades, almond ice cream and hot chocolate since 1700. The original café is on Carrer de Can Sanç.

For sobrassada and cured meats, look in delicatessens along the streets around Plaça Major, and in the town of Sineu, where a long-running Wednesday market sells farm produce. Binissalem, a small town about 25 minutes from Palma, is the heart of the island's wine country, and several wineries open for tastings.

## Food by region

- **Sóller valley**: oranges and lemons, used in juices, marmalade and cakes. Local orange juice is available fresh in the town square, and Sóller's ice cream is well known.
- **Pollença and Alcúdia**: fish dishes and the Sunday markets, where you can buy local cheese, olives and cured meats.
- **Santanyí and the south-east**: figs, almonds and capers, and restaurants overlooking the coves.
- **Inca**: the leather and cellar restaurants called cellers, which serve roast suckling pig and lamb in huge stone-walled halls. Inca's Thursday market is the island's biggest.
- **Sa Pobla**: potatoes and the Albufera marshes. The town is known for rice dishes and for eel stews, and its Sunday market sells local vegetables.

## Seasonal dishes

Mallorca's calendar still follows the farming year. The almond blossom of January and February turns the plains pale pink and white. In late winter, artichokes and broad beans appear. At Easter, bakeries sell panades, savoury pies filled with lamb, and robiols, small sweet turnovers. Around the feast of Sant Antoni on 16 and 17 January, villages hold bonfires called foguerons, with sausages grilled over the flames. In autumn, the harvest brings olives, grapes and figs, and the Binissalem wine festival, the Festa des Vermar, takes place in September.

## Wine and olive oil

Binissalem and Pla i Llevant are the two designations of origin. The Manto Negro grape makes light, fruity reds. Local producers also use international grapes such as Syrah and Cabernet Sauvignon. The Serra de Tramuntana produces an olive oil with its own designation of origin, made from the Mallorquina and Arbequina varieties. Olive groves cover many of the terraced hillsides, and some mills welcome visitors.

## Sweets and ice cream

Besides ensaïmada, Mallorca makes greixonera, a baked bread pudding, and cocarrois, small pastries filled with vegetables. The Sóller valley makes orange cakes and almond biscuits. In summer, almond ice cream is a popular flavour, often served with a slice of almond cake.

## What to expect to pay

A menú del día at lunch costs a fraction of dinner prices, and bakery items such as a slice of coca cost only a few euros. Restaurants in Palma's old town range from simple tapas bars to formal dining rooms, while the beach restaurants of the east coast charge more for seafood.

## Food markets by day

- **Wednesday**: Sineu and Santanyí.
- **Thursday**: Inca.
- **Saturday**: Santanyí.
- **Sunday**: Alcúdia, Pollença and Sa Pobla.
