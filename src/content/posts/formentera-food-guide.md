Formentera's food reflects its history as a small, poor island that lived off fishing, salt and what it could grow. The cooking is plain and built around fresh fish, vegetables and olive oil. Most of the best meals are eaten at simple places near the sea.

## Peix sec

Peix sec, or dried fish, is a traditional food of the island. Fish were salted and dried in the sun, which kept them through the winter. Today it appears in salads and as a topping, and it has a strong, salty flavour. Try it once to see what islanders ate for generations.

## Fish stews and rice

The Formentera version of the Ibizan bullit de peix is made with fresh fish, potatoes and saffron, and it is often followed by a rice dish cooked in the same stock. Restaurants near Es Caló, Es Pujols and La Savina serve fresh grilled fish and seafood. Prices depend on weight and on the day's catch, so ask before you order.

## Ensalada payesa and vegetables

Local salads are made with tomatoes, peppers and onions, often with fish and olives. Formentera also grows figs, and dried figs, a traditional fruit, are sold in markets and shops. Look for them as a snack or as a topping on cheese.

## Flaó

Flaó is the cheesecake of Ibiza and Formentera, made with fresh cheese, eggs and mint. It is eaten at celebrations and sold in bakeries, and it makes an easy sweet ending to a meal.

## Salt

Salt was one of the island's earliest industries, and the salt pans at Ses Salines, which stretch between Formentera and Ibiza, are now a protected area. Local salt is sold in small packages and makes a light and useful souvenir. Flamingos and other birds can be seen on the pans in the right season.

## Honey, herbs and liqueur

Bees feed on wild herbs, and honey is sold at markets and shops. Herb liqueurs, similar to the hierbas of Ibiza, are popular after meals. Local wine is produced in small quantities, and a few bars serve it.

## Markets

The market at Es Pilar de la Mola is held on certain days in summer, with crafts, clothing and food stalls. It dates from the island's hippy years in the 1960s and 70s, when travellers settled in the island's quieter corners. Sant Francesc has a smaller regular market. The days and times change, so check locally.

## Tips

- Many restaurants open only from spring to autumn.
- Lunch often runs from two in the afternoon, and dinner from half past eight.
- A menú del día is less common than on the mainland, but some places offer a lunchtime option.
- Prices are higher than on Ibiza because everything arrives by boat. Compare menus before you sit down, and keep an eye on fish by weight.
- Book beachfront restaurants ahead for July and August.

## The villages and where they cook

Sant Francesc Xavier, the capital, has a handful of restaurants around the fortified church and a small market. Es Pujols, on the north coast, has a long line of fish and rice restaurants along the seafront. Es Caló de Sant Agustí, a small fishing hamlet on the east coast, is known for simple seafood served close to the boat sheds. Es Pilar de la Mola, on the plateau, has a few country restaurants and the artisans' market. Cala Saona and Migjorn have beach restaurants where you can eat with your feet in the sand.

La Savina, the port, has shops and bakeries, and it is a good place to pick up supplies for a picnic. Sant Ferran de ses Roques, at the crossroads in the middle of the island, has a few bars and bakeries and is known for its small hotels.

## Traditional dishes in more depth

Peix sec is fish such as ray or conger that is salted and dried, then grilled or served in salads with potatoes, tomatoes and olives. Fishermen once hung the fish from lines in the sun, and some families still prepare it at home. It is a salty, chewy dish, and it goes well with cold drinks.

Bullit de peix, the fish stew shared with Ibiza, is made with firm white fish, potatoes and saffron. The rice is cooked afterwards in the cooking broth, which gives it a deep flavour. Ask whether the dish needs to be ordered in advance, and whether the price is per person or per portion.

Sofrit pagès is a hearty country dish of meats and potatoes, served in autumn and winter. Ensalada payesa and tomato salads appear in summer, and fresh figs are served with cheese.

## Sweets and local products

Flaó is the best-known dessert, but you will also find greixonera, a bread pudding, and almond biscuits. Fig preserves and fig bread are made from local fruit. Honey is sold at roadside stalls and markets, and herb-based liqueurs, such as hierbas and frígola, are served after a meal. Frígola is made with thyme and has a mild sweetness.

Sea salt from the island's salt flats is harvested by hand in some places, and the island exports a small amount of flor de sal. You can buy it in shops, along with olive oil and sobrassada.

## Markets

The Es Pilar de la Mola market takes place on Wednesday and Sunday afternoons in summer, and it is a mix of crafts, jewellery and food stalls. The market in La Savina is smaller and features local crafts. Sant Francesc has a small market with fruit and vegetables. Dates change from year to year, and many markets close in winter.

## Local drinks

Beer and wine are served in most places. A few vineyards in Formentera make small amounts of red and white wine from grapes grown in sandy soil. Local gin and rum are available in bars. In summer, fresh fruit juices and cocktails are served in beach restaurants.

## What to expect in terms of price

Because many goods arrive by boat, prices on Formentera are higher than in Ibiza or on the mainland. Beachfront restaurants often charge a premium, and fish is priced by weight. A set menu at lunch is the cheapest way to eat well. Supermarkets in Sant Francesc and La Savina sell groceries and picnic supplies at lower prices.

## Eating outside season

Many restaurants close between November and April. Those that stay open are in Sant Francesc, La Savina and Es Pujols, and offer a short winter menu. The best time to eat local food is in the shoulder months, when restaurants are open but not full.

## Tips

- Book beachfront restaurants ahead in July and August.
- Ask how fresh fish is priced before you order.
- Try a local liqueur after a meal.
- Take cash for markets and small bars.
- Bring a reusable bag for market shopping.
