Cape Verde, an Atlantic archipelago of just over half a million people, have reached the knockout rounds of their first World Cup. A goalless draw with Saudi Arabia in Houston, combined with Spain's 1–0 win over Uruguay in Guadalajara, secured second place in Group H — and a meeting with Lionel Messi's Argentina, the defending champions.

## Three draws, no defeats

The Blue Sharks finished the group unbeaten with three draws, in a group that also included former world champions Spain and Uruguay. Coach Bubista built a compact, disciplined side, and goalkeeper Vozinha became the symbol of their resistance.

## A tense final game

With both sides knowing that only certain results would take them through, the opening minutes against Saudi Arabia were nervous, full of rushed passes and few chances.

Cape Verde lined up in a compact 4-1-4-1, defending as a unit and looking to break quickly. Forward Willy Semedo was their most dangerous player in the first half and forced a good save from the Saudi goalkeeper, Al-Owais. At the other end, Vozinha made a crucial stop from a header in added time before the break.

## Growing confidence

As the game went on, Saudi Arabia struggled to create anything, and Cape Verde grew in confidence, finding space between the lines. Their best chance came in the 74th minute, when a fast counter-attack ended with Nuno da Costa setting up Laros Duarte, whose close-range shot was saved by Al-Owais.

The final minutes were played on emotion rather than tactics, but Cape Verde kept their composure to see out the draw.

## Celebrations in Houston

When news of Spain's victory over Uruguay came through, the Cape Verdean supporters in the stadium erupted. One of the smallest nations at the tournament had become one of its great surprises.

Facing Argentina will be the biggest game in the country's football history. Few expect the Blue Sharks to go further — but few expected them to get this far.
