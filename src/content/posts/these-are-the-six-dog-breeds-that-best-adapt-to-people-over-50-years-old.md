Getting a dog later in life can bring companionship, routine and exercise. But the right breed depends on your lifestyle. These six are often recommended for people over 50 — for very different reasons.

## Cavalier King Charles Spaniel: the cuddler

Small (around 6–8 kg) with a soft, silky coat, the Cavalier is affectionate and patient, and gets on well with children, other pets and visitors. It enjoys short walks and play but does not need long hikes, making it a good choice for a calm home. Prospective owners should choose a responsible breeder, as the breed can be prone to heart problems.

## Spanish greyhound (galgo): fast but calm

Galgos are built for speed, but at home they are famously lazy, spending much of the day asleep. They are gentle, calm and bond strongly with their families, though they can be shy in noisy places. A daily walk and the occasional run in a safe, fenced area are enough. Many galgos are available for adoption from rescue organisations in Spain, including in the Balearics.

## Maltese: small and loyal

Weighing under 6 kg, the Maltese has a white coat that needs regular grooming. It is playful and devoted, following its owner everywhere, and can become protective without good socialisation. Short walks and indoor games are enough, and it adapts well to flat living.

## Labrador Retriever: the all-rounder

A medium-to-large dog (25–36 kg), the Labrador is known for its kindness, patience and eagerness to please, which is why it is so often used as a guide and therapy dog. It does, however, need plenty of exercise to stay fit and avoid weight gain.

## Poodle: clever and long-lived

Available in toy, miniature and standard sizes, poodles are highly intelligent and enjoy mental challenges and training. Their curly coat sheds little but needs regular grooming. They tend to live long lives.

## Brittany: for active owners

A medium-sized, athletic hunting dog, the Brittany is energetic and enthusiastic outdoors but gentle at home. It suits people who enjoy walking, hiking or running, and can become anxious if left alone for long periods.

## Choosing well

- **Be realistic about your energy and time.**
- **Think about space:** a flat, a house with a garden, or a rural property.
- **Consider grooming and vet costs.**
- **Consider adoption:** older dogs from shelters are often calmer and already trained.

Whatever breed you choose, regular vet check-ups, socialisation and a balanced routine of exercise and affection are the keys to many happy years together.
